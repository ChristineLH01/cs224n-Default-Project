O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Purchased with fastidious alchemy,
And stol'n again with a redier rosary?
O, love, me see not but you see,
For all eyes are but stores of your true light.
My tongue is the herald of the ocean,
But all others seek our sport,
And none else their food: their prey is mine alone.
  Make no much of stars, love,
  Save what you can love that you can take.

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
The sullied tallity of stone dead.
Dian himself could not lift stone to do his work
Save in storms and contracted spaces;
The force of the blow was decayed by the burn,
Therefore struck monuments blessed in love.
Now of this beauty did none report
Nor so famous thence to write,
Since therein lays the main of all knowledge;
Nor so was he unto worms vilified in hearsay,
Pitiful love and mutual deceivance proved,
  Save in creeds rare, till his name be remembered.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Delayed or reposed them betimes;
Who for sweet motion slumbered on sweet rest,
Such sleepers bereft they all they lacked,
Whilst beloved hymns for voices still sang;
Therefore these lame songs did Presetes blame,
And poets so fleet with their travel,
Creating first a prickle, then blind errand;
  As playful stones and their foiled pattern,
  From sweet drops to potions play in eternal war.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,

For the only knowledge she hath is what I tell;

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And for a perpetual jewel in heaven
My holy trust devotes most; yet it must decay
Before my eternal hope can dwell there.
My other love doth pencil, being a perpetual child,
For doting on love doth add still more delight;
And truth says this fond in love's pencil,
"Till objects with pen look so good, happiness then must die."
  For pencils of deep thoughts do bear that name,
  And if love's interest doth express a hundred thoughts,
  But one tells how sweetly it doth dwell.
