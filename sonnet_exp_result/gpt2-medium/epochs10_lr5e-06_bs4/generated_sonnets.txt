Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
A height I might have defended many ages hadst thou sang,

My mind I affrighted not then with youth,

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which, thus clouded by rude vision's contraries,
Is mute to the true objects of my perception?
Hath vanity here wrest my long account,
While my noblest thoughts rule the world?
O, what hope did my judgment place on that thy brow bore?
Mine eyes are this, eyes and not secrets;
Who before hears the thought that here lay

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Were nayseries thought by women, vilest fools,
And ruled men's pleasures by whim,
To hover their dull methods on her thanks,
Or call the censors cowering all wrong.
Since then he gave in, love having spent itself,
And weep for sweet root of health, unless it spend there.
  O ye pitying ladies, I speak ill of thee.
  We ought not more love to pray for pleasure than

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
O'ering another age's may do him boast,
Inteating memory of his past or absent youth;
But my youth will do what wisdom cannot,
Sufficient yet to chuse truth from men who thought it bad,
To strike up, with bold resolution,
The dead nest that dryder incense served in his strength,
To weep for loving lost, with bitter disgust;
  And will lose again how off I feebly I expostulated,
  That I should now meet rude friends with pen,
  Inapraved for thoughts which once were ages.
