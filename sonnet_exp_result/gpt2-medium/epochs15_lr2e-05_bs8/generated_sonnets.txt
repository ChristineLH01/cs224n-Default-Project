Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
O, curse their beauteous faces now that they have forsaken me;
Mine eye only they saw expressed their grief;
They that build them to this new castle would cheer me if they would confess
That I had no love for them but by rote
And all losses before my veil been rent to make another;
  Yet, heaven knows, one of them, if not all, should shun me and live
  Lascivious barons used to be, yet this face hath strangely struck me.


1
Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Which were renewed in thee on high
When for thee alone my kings did fight?
Why dost thou excuse the error, why shalt thou tarry
In making a quixotic base, the objects
Of self-love and love for others,
Conspiring together with malice in one,
Forstifling revolution in their well-balanced affections?
  Thy love would be malignant where it thrives;
  But mine is sweet, yet not willful in thy doing.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Spending the cry for fresh resuscitation
For Time's present to repair,
Until some aid from thee in death remove
His last sovereign stores from my weary breast;
But this surfeit sweet love, this secret I do swear,
Shall never be idle till his memory be reigned,
And then his worth, fleeting as it is,
Like buried treasure returned to an thrall.
  This hate I predict to come, for my thought doth rhyme,
  And my love in that rhapsody lives on in memory.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
When motion in eyes tells the secret of parts?
O, if eyes tell me of thy coming--
Or else if I shall behold thee see,
O, it are no great loss to me,
My fears o'ersawed and my beauty misapprehended.
Then of my story do I writ, either thou art clear,
Or else moving eyes tell--
  Then mine is sweet judgment everywhere,
  Then travels all languages to learn thy name.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
And yet linger in moods of indigestion,
Spending treasure too much, hasting too soon, fearing no good,
Whilst I watch thoughts fleet in fixed orient
Haply embarked upon nothing to do,
Desiring nothing enough once more when sea and dry land
Haply do give way; that's the excellence,
Which when after present I lack, I all alone for sorrow
Dost fill again, but with love, happier than I was
At first, subdued with poverty, converted thence into praise.
  To this be due glad men that thou wilt


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
Against thy wild war against passion base?
Sick feeling, yea, even foul vexation,
Pitiful love, fearful self-love, seldom minded conquest;
Rigorous pains; hardships of tomb and altars,

Sickness, unless it be from thyself,--
Which sorrow, heavily making the sinner feel,

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
And let love itself confess in true style
What high purpose this of impiety!
How long Time forgot what strength his laws wrought,
Which of little hours in the front spied,
And now found the desire so fierce
That constant hours were not had but for trophies
Of delight and to steal tears;
Since morn some one shall say "Fair, do thee disgrace"
And each after another for hate repair
The folly of neglecting that sacred respect.
  All will cry "Cheater! Sweetheart! Lest love itself be set sweet,"
  To love what was never had.


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
O, why should it so? Let the fool at last take me
Whilst I am hearkened not, my dead being never born;
A single false age calls upon death,
Both sin and canker swift for a tombs.
  Ah, but it is such dear love that lives eternal,
  That no half-dead breed must the worms overthrow.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Proving swift defeat by wide-spread flame,
His folly as he took it from the pitch,
Where Nature's breath had first put it.
Where fame leapt off gulls bending to him,
And birds sang hymns to him as fair,
To mourn was her sickling's frailty wrought;
His flesh now burning ill to life paid.
  But cunning dead women make long beseeching verses,
  And lovers spite in their spite bring
  To stand to grief honor's due whose name is fond.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Lunged, batt'ring more, with foot in breast faster.
A gentle nurse feeding them with golden honey
Placed buds which, for beauty's sake of numbers,
Drawn that conquest from dearest soil,
Were stealthily put to growing lips to kiss,
Making sickle against cross wrought,
Struck with poisoned wound whose spring abounds.
Then such time of penury
As heaven delivered o'er thorns is this doom's training-bed.
  If by truth or gentle nurse's fair I be wrong,
  The lovers' looks lost shall sweetly cure me


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Renting a world idle heruen with tanned brows,
Entitled to his manors all other's,
Returning with wretched state not to one;
But when she doth acknowledge she is thine,
Wishing to make my praise false for evermore,
Upon a false epitaph do I steep
My love with a rude novel heaped;
  By any why her living thy lie she doth defy.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Full lively to rest, perfect in knowledge;
He knows my prayers and my sins,
That calls on no adjunct, but pities me
By rhyme or the eye and faith alone.
O, what love can be which shames not?
My beloved would groan or crow if he thought himself poor,
Sic dullness there if times should permit.
  All other loves excell my lesser,
  But you are mine, and this proves more.
