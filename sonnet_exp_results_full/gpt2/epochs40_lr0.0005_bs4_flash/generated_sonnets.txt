Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While thy outer shell doth suffer in suppression?
My feeble spirit, the vessel of my feeble rage,
Doth launch upon thy vast and welcoming sea,
While my flesh, being made of four, welcomes the view,
Proving thy strength by thy heavily gilded vessel.
But, if thou wilt, I cannot boast,
Of my thrall, thou shalt be blamed,
For slander's mark is ever drawn on thine.
  So, if thou wilt, I'll bear the smart,
  For slander's taint clings to the fairest skin.

For that which longer nurseth the disease,

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I betray
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may strive
For victory in love; the flesh, now alive,
Rises at your name, its purpose to derive
From your affection, its reason to thrive.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, ever so near,
  To stand by you, to fall by your side, my dear.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And for myself but forsworn, to give voice.
For thou art blamed shall not be thy defect,
Nor praise it be thine to claim,
For it depends upon that love of thine.
And that is the love of action;
So I will not praise thee for my deeds I see,
Nor thou shalt be blamed for my neglect
Of thee, nor I to brief minutes blame.
  For I must confess that I love thee in such sort
  As, thou being mine, mine is thy good report.

I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And leave me in the public eye to grow.
But why should I dare to question if my days are done,

The pierce of my breast,
