Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Her breath that traced the pain there
In my breast, that revived my friend:
Her tongue, which would have spoken the tale,
But sickened my friend, who then, in sleep,
Died in a different place.
"I never saw that you did painting grow
With sweetness or color change,
But that you yourself, being dead, did make
Those lips that Love used to nurse."
  And still others in that same plight
  With that same loss, which in me nearly killed
  In myself, almost by self-love.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Enhancing thy fleeting frame with ill fortune's sway?
Why bestow such riches on this fading edifice,
A monument destined to turn to dust?
Shall the inheritors of this vice,
Consume thy legacy, your very essence?
Then, soul, feed on the anguish of thy servant,
Let them fade, while you flourish and transcend.
Exchange the transient hours for eternal bliss,
Find solace within, while lacking without.
  Thus shall you feast on Death, who devours all souls,
  And when Death is vanquished, no more shall we know death's toll

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Alternating cures for my own heart's ills.
I have no desire to stay sick of your look,
Nor do I fear the day when my dear love shall fade,
When you, my dearest, return with gentle sighs,
A fresh start towards the morrow.
Yet, if you must depart, then leave me alone,
For I have no desire to stay,
Nor do I fear the day when your love shall resurvey
  The strength that once did keep me from half-lack.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
How can love's beauty be true, to whom eye it retires?
If I have seen a lovely face, dyed by tears,
What worth the world, that deems it otherwise?
If I have seen a false god, uncared for by love's fire,
What worth the world, that deems it otherwise?
If I have seen true kindness, my love warm,
Its virtue evident, its falsehood rare.
  But yet, love, none can make me believe
  That love works, though shadows mimic you.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steepen.
She let out a steady rain for three hours,
Three lovely showers plunged the Cupid's fire into the ground,
A famine had swept the land, and three banks of brookings
Three weeks had passed, yet the crops had not waned.
But when the waters retreated, the waters could not compare;
Their power was outpaced, and now the waters were
Three to the west, and Away from you.
  For in your sweet role, all things were restored,
  And yet you, my love, remain confined.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
In silent reverence of their beloved,
Died like flies, as the lovely vale below.
I saw the radiant light of day,
Yet unsleeping, my heart's contented plea,
As flint, when near to death, was drowned,
By my beloved's sweet, innocent touch,
The breath that from my friend's lungs did pour,
As if the waters kept the fire from consuming him.
  Yet still, I loved that heavenly fire,
  For worldly lights may fade, but not his moon.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And hang my praises on her intellectual tresses.
But since she thinks me gullible,
I hold her to be true, and not feigned,
Though she thinks me blessed, and indeed,
She thinks me a trifling lie,
To swear by great falsehoods quite apart.
And yet, to swear against the truth, I swear,
I swear by swear, and prove
A thousand and one feigned, falsellied lips.
  For I have sworn thee fair, and bound my deeds,
  To lie against the truth, so vile and unworthy.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And love that man well is not so rosy.
If Nature, sovereign mistress over wrack,
As thou art, keeps you not in chests,
Thyself, her art allayed, still is not so well.
But that she may keep you not,
Her craft, like a captive being,
Her allure, from time to time, as thou art.
  And, of thy sweet love, so rare,
  I will be the second.
