Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
To me, who languished because she loved not,
Her cold departure felt like a winter's fray.
Her heart, touched by remorse, then did show
A tender touch, a softer blow,
And taught her tongue, with a healing seal,
A kinder phrase, a tender appeal:
"I hate" turned into light,
Like dawn's first blush on the horizon's face,
Bringing hope, banishing the night,
Replacing despair with love's embrace.
  "I hate" she set aside,
  And saved my heart, with love, she tied.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When nature's ardent fire sings a mournful song?
Thou art too dear for my possessing,
And like enough thou know'st thy estimate.
Yet do thy worst to steal thyself away,
For I see thee striving to reach the heights;
Even so I fear the worst of this unrest
Will prove better than loss of thee.
  Pity me, then, and wish I were thee,
  As I swallow down the plague that pigs have stoked.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And needs no cold, no salutation to find;
It is as a bastard to his blessed mother
Who steals him away from her side,
Or dies of in poverty, whereupon his name dies with shame.
I have witnessed countless mornings, bright
And clear as any where else in the world,
But then, within that sun's shade,
My love fell a-doting, full of yearn,
And found no sweet delight in that bower.
  For nought in that realm she sees,
  Save breed, to whom her child doth give birth.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler nature to my body's treachery.
My spirit informs my flesh that it may claim

Rises at your mention and proclaims

I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,

That she loves those of you above.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my sinister one a woman wicked.
When at a smile I sometime part,
My sweet one's countenance fills the vacant space.
My lines, though precise, yet free,
To thee I send inadvertent essays.
Thy looking glass, thy shape in my hand,
Cannot settle the truth it shows,
Nor can my soul confirm the thing it doth say.
  If it be error, not mine,
  The crow itself doth proclaim it heretic.
