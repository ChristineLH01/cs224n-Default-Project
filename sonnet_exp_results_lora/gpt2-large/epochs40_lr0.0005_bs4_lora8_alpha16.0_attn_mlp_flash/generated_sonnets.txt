Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Those blue eyes that floated in front of my sight
As the conquests of the jungle wake;
Those blunt cheeks, which by, as by night,
Hath drawn my clear somenings,
As suspects in a forbidden mystery.
Who is it that says most, which can say
More, great or small, than this,
That you alone are you, and I none;
  Nor I to none in that you rave,
  That you yourself do it so alone.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Crowning the result with below thee alone?
All alone thy sport is, and therein spends
All thy sorrows and all thy glory.
How much more joy do I find in thee
Than when thou thyself dost give life to me,
For when that life thou dieest takes,
My life thou hast provided I hast to starve
And drown in debt. Then thou hast passed from me,
And I have post-Ordinary leisure found thee dead.
  But here thy purpose is to prove
  That thou post-Essential ill locusts thee elsewhere.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That thus the eloquence of thy praise
Hath added a size to my substance?
Did not the taller thing also carry a sense
Of your true worth, which in thy brain doth grow?
Kind they, but less true in their love,
To whom your praise doth like medicine give,
Making lame what kind of right might normally grow.
  As the incommitry of stars in main darkness
  Blind the blind, so my love blinds mine eye.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
And therefore dare to admit my love even while thou art gone,
For I may not love thee when I think on thee gone,
When thou art still around?
Thou, to whom I formerly committed crime,
Mayst thou outrage me with thy presence,
While I, receiving thy permission, do homage to thee,
Granted thee my sin not by thy faile,
But by thy faile permitting me to offend.
  Since I've offended thee, thou mayst pay the price
  Though I die and thee weep.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
Or else kind-hearted truth confounds me in thy will,
As though my soul were made of jelly by thee?
O, no. That lie is thine. The more I love, the more doth my love confirm
The more strong its steed with his steed.
In other words, love is thy billfolded armor,
Which armed with this armor can fight
Against awkward stones and weak sticks.
  But if my love be wounded, what thereby is he hurt?
  He takes no hurt when thou receivest it.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And two fronts have arisen: The lamb for which thou servedst,
And the dove for which thou chore'st.
The first loves the second less,
The second loves the first quite ill;
The first doth daily comfort the wretch with promise,
The second as if he were sick of lemons.
  Incapable of beginning, replete with all my parts,
  My soul's origin was Dartmoor's road,
  And, apt to originate, my body's location.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Making a dominion over nature.
The type of his female head was very fair,
Though not shorn so wise as his form;
His natural love was wonderfully resonant,
Hang on such a string as woman's wits fow;
And to his oriental pride added a rouge,
Which being foreign to his caste burnt so.
His beauty was seen in its newer part,
And beauty's ordinariness makes his mode weird.
  But beauty's unlimited pride struck him hard,
  And, sovereign, he ceased to be.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
In his deadly arsenal were aback
By his swift amputation, and tears were shed
All gilded with his living hue;
But heaven's free hand then restored him to life,
And to ladies lovers he presented himself as
Thy sweet love looks on thee now.
How many a happy hour have I seen
Use this perfect guide to laugh and make lewd feel
As thy sweet self mockingly examines thy rival;
  And yet this small moment doth scare the world,
  Because thou epically livestest in thine.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Whilst, for my sake, she hath bundled me up far from her place.
Yet if she represents my true thoughts,
The fabric of my love is badly done,
For modulation missing, strength replete with decay,
Is my mistress' style worsted and verge twain.
Doubting her sincerity, I examine her look,
And seeing negatives in her cheeks,
One on another's neck do declare
Her breeding most temperamental, her joints damaged,
  And much admiration for her fading fast.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Who in some mystery rides on my heart;
A dear friend to myself I hold,
Whose worth's unknown, although he lives in me.
And for the sake of love do I tumultuous leap
While shapes like to him mock my sight,
Dulling my nerves with their staccato shout;
Nor does he raise his voice in praise
When I, angry with ill, do wrong;
He merely whispers, 'Ah, maurosis!'
  But thou, to whom my spirits thine have lent,
  Much liketh them and much liketh me.
