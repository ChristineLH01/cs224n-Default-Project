Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
The prey of every vulgar pest?
Who allows thy warmth to drain
From his steel breast, wherewith he stands armed?
Or who dost thou send away free,
That homeless joy, which thyself departs,
As thy sweet self doth propagate within thee?
If any, let this thought enter thy brain
That thou in thy sadness mayst make a rhyme:
  "After my pleasure hath gone,
  The sorrow that brought it into me must come."

Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That thou in thy sum mayst our dear love show?
That people may say I love them that well know

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
If not to spurn your dearest love, right mentally.
For to do such a heinous crime
As disobeys mine own sense of right is no shame,
It is my duty as a parent to right act,
And to lose an intimate friend for conscience' sake
Is not affecting once more my true love,
O'erloading my bad with another's good.
  Therefore, love not hypocrite, be you blessed
  While others practise what you do wrong.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
In thought thy glass has bent and new goodness found.
Now all is done, love, and I forgive thee wholly;
All is restored, and I rivet anew your love.
So then thou wilt be none of so bitter a learn,
To love that well which thou didst vow to lose;
For to lose again thou art sworn to me,
And therefore to love thou must do some attaint.
  For truth stands against prejudice sharp,
  And tolerance explains hatred more.
