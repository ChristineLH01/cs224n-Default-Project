Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When from thyself hast thou thy sweet charge?
O, do not, love, so foolish beget,
When other types of life have given thee so blessed a use;
Bring forth more than thyself thou trusted dost deceive.
Go to other sweet ghosts of heart
And see what merit they find in thee;
They see but weak and scantily dressed,
And in their thoughts of love lavish praise.
  Then, love, thou image of my beauty shouldst say

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Making a famine where abundance lies,
A gilded tomb where beauty's treasure is.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Suggesting truths beyond my credence.
But when my love finds that she is made
Of cotton and wool and kerchiefs of vermilion,
I know the fraud which holds her breath;
I know the veiled shame which in her breast doth lie,
I know the fond anticipation
Which sets the tone for debate where I am.
  She hath not created me for her show,
  nor myself for her love, though I thine adored be.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
With shifting shadows brill'ring his shape,
With shifting shadows on his flaming face,
A woman's gentle heart, but not acquainted
With shifting change, as is false women's fashion;
Rough through woods seems his shape perfect,
But when from highmost pitch with divining wave
He turns toward the dusk, he stol'n from me.
My stronger love is as a spring
Whispers sweet things to the ear,
But when at sunset my love gives way,
Mine immediately declines.
  For no rosy event
  So disrespectful is final ruin.
