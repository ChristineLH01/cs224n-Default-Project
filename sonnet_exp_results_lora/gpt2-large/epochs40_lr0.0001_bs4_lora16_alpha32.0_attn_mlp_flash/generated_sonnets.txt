When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Happy without precedence to Tyane,
The humble of one craft, near the style of Kent.
But when my love loves to prove
That she is true, all things shake
That represent beauty, nature, and breeze;
I saith "Ah, truth hides a worse Cato,"
And teach her then to embrace imitation,
That teaches more, not less. "Thou gav'st this child this mountain dearer,
To show thyself clothed in fine clothes?"
  To love, to love with true inclinations stands,
  Which loves more than lovers know.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Whose pencil-thin hand my hand defaces,
Who holds my beautiful glass by its crystal face,
And lies deliciously folded on my heart;
Yet he in my love fears the worst of wrong,
Seems an enemy to tyrants' right,
And boasts of my excellence even to death.
