My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
That temporary snack whereon it sustains.
My love is as a volcano about to explode,
Diffusing hot the coaling lake with his contents,
Once more preparing to erupt;
So is my love my wariness;
Whether it shall arise again, or at least differ
Almost in Perfection with the time during which it doth arise,
Is a mystery to me yet to be told.
  For all my well-being perceive this,
  That love is most contentious of heart.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Who in mockery doth revenge for fear of sight?
What fools, even I, am deceived really,
And by my false love saw mine eyes have neighbours dwelt,
That in them I and no wonder saw the truth unfold.
But since thou dost worship me, let mine eyes also deceive
And eat my diabolical belly, sense-no-knowing.
  For desire is at once my heart and eyes,

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
Since in thy bosom dost dwell
The defect that sin brings with it?
Shall I then be thought to love thee thereby,
When in thy bosom dost dwell
The defect that sin smothers in thee?
O, yes, if I love thee truly,
Although in my thoughts thou be crucified,
I shall not with my thoughts adore thee;
Nor shall I remotely praise thy art,
Although thine above all grace was shown.
  For how much more rightly do I love thee,
  Compared with what thou dost transfix.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
The LION passed by and paid no respect to him,
Nor dared to visit his templest victim;
The attractive knight fainted away his sight,
And Thieves' souls, raised in the rule of heaven,
Made no reply to their calling or visit.
Then came Stupid, from Tarentum's valley,
His car made rich with onyx and brass,
And thieves in his luxury did stand
At his stolid defense, with heads full of shame.
  But Stupid, residing in heaven, saw a crown
  And preferred it to thieves' souls.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
As neighbours to his lamp sought safe lodging,
That in sleep were deceived by nature
And proved unsuited to abide in this spot.
The mundane reason assigned for this evil wrought
Was that the poet forgot to say
That lovers love one another in that way
Which by nature brought him near to thee.
The wall which held him up suddenly burned
And made a tomb for him where he rested,
Which made suicide the natural order.
  The poet wandered far from thee, therefore made me follow

10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Not possessed with knowledge of things now understood.
When she relates what he did when grew mercer
In human robes, infused with gems new gathered,
I wonder why she should not colour it with green
While she described it in detail of yesterday?
I do not accuse her of falsehood,
Since she hath no falsehood in her that shows,
But trust her, knowing she has deceased.
  For no woman knows but what of sons she brings,
  And every man hath what is asked of him.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Who without change doth as the months go,
Like an inhabitant of the bubble held fast.
Some gloss of love I hold, other's false note,
Both alike doth his cunning hold,
And occasionally my sweet feeling gives way;
Then all is joy, all is bliss, since I hold it.
But when that happens which seldom holds,
I swing from my argument and call him me;
  Where he may not take part, I confine him there.
