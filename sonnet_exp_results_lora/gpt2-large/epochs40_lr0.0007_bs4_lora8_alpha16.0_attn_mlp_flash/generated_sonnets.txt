Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Sapped with bonds of kindred love to befits?
O, lack of care for others' well being,
That thou mayst pleasure thyself in thy want;
No, thou art too pure to be married,
For thyself to be knew and judged so.
And for thyself to be knows he needs thee,
A truly bad man, to whom thou gav'st a home.
  For thyself to be loved he need not be known,
  Since that he grants thee so freely.

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Because in your hand the assault is divided.
O, treat not that instrument with skewers
Unless you are clever enough to know
What rather wounds do send forth.
To give blood to seem love is folly,
Since conscious wounds do betray their bearer;
To give life to seem death is madness.
So, true love, be a fool,
And your true house be a large desert
Which hides your true spirit, which lives in thee.
  Let that house fall to decay,
  Where graceful satiety may show her living flesh.

In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
Now for thyself mine own passion criers do come,

To prove thy love is fairest and most due.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Making a blaze on the roof of his house.
The roof of his house he beat with a mournful sigh,
And dumb with his mourning went, since he couldn't see
What golden and lovely time his child had gamed.
The gilded jelly on his cheek
When they warmed his wounds with steam arose,
A face very rudely made by his fondness.
His child was fond, and so went his father;
So his love was otters for love of man.
  But when trumpets sounded, and blew sage's face,
  The king was lion and was cheered

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Disdaining the treasures of his love, bore
The monument of his loving memory.
The dead president of his greatness calls
And lets his antique pen write
The lovely line that bestowed his life.
The dull muse then fills his verse with new thought,
And all her old facts are re-painted new,
Making room for new love in his rhyme.
  And whate'er she wails or praises or curses
  The rest, as her son would be if he lived,
  Let him live, and he's the better rest.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Like him that buys men's skins for cheap,
Who relentlessly throws money away,
Like him that buys and sells like him too;
Therefore my love is twain, and both are perforated.
Two loves are so, though in different accents;
But like him that buys and sells, I in each keep,
Seeking such relation as two excel.
  For Chapter II, written by myself,
  thou mightst my true love tell the story.
