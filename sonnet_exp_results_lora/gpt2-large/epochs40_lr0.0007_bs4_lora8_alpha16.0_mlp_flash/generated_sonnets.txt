Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Pitiful vacuum, that should than receive thee partake?
Return, forgetful looter, and straight redeem
In gentle numbers time so idly spent;
Sing to the ear that doth thy stolen love spy,

Rise, resty soul; my love's sweet face survey
If Time have any wrinkle graven there.
If any, be a satire to decay
And make Time's spoils despised everywhere.
  Give my love fame faster than Time wastes life;

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Savage as the eruption on the boughs;
For often is my love's fever newly settled,
A fatal one, that visits on vertues
And disturbs the conspire of woe.
If the release be lost of that patient,
The plague is his desire; and, so oft as he commits,
The bliss of that perfect cure is lost,
And his health becomes a miserable one.
  There lives more love in one of your fair eyes
  Than both your lovers could in praise devise.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In idleness the base rosy veins of his face.
After a fashion, therefore, his skin was rosy;
But his eyes were catgut and blacked with blood.
And, to insure his gloom, a vengeful canker ate him up
Within himself, whereupon his face took on a white.
This affair was confirmed by none other than Dian,
A creation so cruel and so hypocritical,
That to himself she imputed his crime.
  She fed him on her love, and he on her will;
  And yet him she prized and loved was yet another.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
By sympathy being tied to his offense,

For wounds are not cured which cannot heal.
His wounds are lasting, and therefore life no longer follows;

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Who in red armed soul's resistance wears his complexion straight;
Yet despair covers his hell with hideous blot,
And looks on him scornful when he sees him.
So my beloved aid, constancy, comes in one of these;
A radiant bell like most lily's White,
Whose thorns protrude buds in holy garden;
When seasons's gaudy summer wears his lusty apparel.
  For his color is white and for his love his love is red.
