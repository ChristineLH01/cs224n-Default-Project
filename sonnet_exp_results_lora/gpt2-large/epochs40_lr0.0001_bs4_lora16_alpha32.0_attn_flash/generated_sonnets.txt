Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Beauty in bold lips this vulgar perfidy
Whilst dignified in howl he lightly grew.
Two sweet dainties in two very contraries met,
Caught by two tails, and one swallowed up,
Releasing his brawn and beauty unconquered;
I was forced on both drunk to wonder,
For it pleased me but to see him forgot.
  This glutton and other vice did hang,
  For beauty hangeth but for vice and reason,
  Whereof both offense and offense are evil.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Bearing all close bondage as though afflicted?
Have I too much sinned? O, no, thy soul speaks
On 'twixt praise and blame, reflecting both sides;
Against my figures thou sicken, I myself against,
To choose on my part offends thy sweetness.
Why not fain would I banish that blight
Which by thy countenance swallows up more than it brings,
And distill thy bounty out of my parched veins,
Finding it vain to have art, art, but finding none,
Lifts up purer operation at my body's bottom?
 


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And must everywhere die, not visible,
But hid where blackworms eat its bone, where wires burn
In festering pores, where molds pour slime on the blood;
That living garment and shadow of my love
Lest to the common ransack all of us turned,
I like to think thou hast left out of all these.
In payment, though I bestow nothing more,
A sign of thine that I live is due;
It dies but when the leakiest stain more obtains.
  Truly this love is perfect happiness,
  And she often grows more and more


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That looks true where they tell me I look false?
I look, think I, for myself, when it lies
For my organs, which this love saith are blest,
Untroubled with discord among their parts.
Look, think I, when kings lie clothed in cloud
And queens in rich farress stock admiring,
Thy mayds wander in the parade unproportioned,
Woe to him who bade his rents depend
On distances borrowed from thy high estate.
  This want of communication I expect
  To care for with distress, bewailing my state.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot,
And never laugh and curse in thine absence,
If thou were to abide me, while I away?
But now I will swear the feeblest lie
That can accuse me of her love, to say
That thou didst a spy on me full long
And do not doubt her courtesy when I
Roughly arraign her faults that I espy,
For if I do not, she doth wrong me far
And forgets myself all the time she walks.
  Then do all else shame her once, now I swear,
  And I will not strive to repay her love


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
Or to perjure my fidelity to thee.
Tell me, thou my dearest, what height dost thou climb
To find favour with mortal hearts that reign
Slowly but surely, and which who is fittest
To appeal to equals t'oppose thine?
For thy friend, who dost fear nothing,
While thou art on civil terms canst not grow tall.
Beauteous love hath this inflexibility bred,
Which flatterers and princes call dulness;
  Ugly love keeps this vice, and bears his worth light.


6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Finding myself to amiss myself more than interest.
O, no but, so I may rightly say,
The blush no sinful night wakes up in my cheek;
Happy am I that this am it till my seed be found.
And yet when pride lays claim to opinion,
Whereto he thinks unfair what mine act hath done.
It steals and takes when my love calls to it,
And my precept becomes his doctrine.
  Why should I breathe a thought so great in his hold,
  To wit, "That he loved thee"? Lo, "He loves thee that perfect well."


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
To me this truce lost, to thee her punishment.
Whilst I was troubled by loss, thou adored me
And spoiled me in that I spent o'erthine;
By my treaty thou himsel'oted me remote,
And for thee me receiver exiled.
But when these offenses are made known,
Thy love for thee returns tenfold feebler,
Or else equal thy vassal to murders of heart.
  They were compound, thine, by both offenses,
  The one having poison, the other far below.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
Her beauty was aquamarine, not slight,
And she white on another's face is to this day.
Yet cannot any men make this fair display
Which the gifts of women do confound
And confound the eyes of even the most clear.
No face durst receive it save one,
Which by rare inheritance see'st this shame;
The comparison held, he her maid doth that day believe.
  For sweeter look'st not in holy see
  But in eyes which this vice takes from true.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
If 'twixt his hand and heart he interposed
Made innocence mirthful delight.
Nimble Dionysus his brawny brute tame,
Which fashions rich purple garlands in his hide,
Sacrificing joy and solace to morn,
Since pride headlong no longer buys much of joy,
When but supping on such feast he toears.
  These teeming multitude won,
  So merry, by the breaks of his neglige,
  But at last the rude cause led him out.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And yet in myself I saw no scruple.
But because she told me so, I was sure
That if truth stood against deception,
My love had wrought it all his due.
In truth he said so to me, 'Art thou all beautiful
That heaven stoos upon your breasts, and that waters
Your cheeks with dearer perfumes?
Bare I have seen but babes' faces strong,
And a babe's lips full of sweet essence;
Be sober, be gaol'd; I love not for love of
This love which so goes ill for me.'

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
That comes to meet me with open arms,
And bestows sweet reproof on my faults,
Giving well-defined aught as he giv'st,
Giving the details of wisdom and shame,
Giving the detail of Nature's favor
Which Nature deceptions masked from her creatures.
He's a delight too, so joyful my love,
Whilst I constantly in my mind play
How foolish I am to quarrel with thee.
  Or if I am dull or if my thoughts so,
  Lo, love's angel's music concends me still.
