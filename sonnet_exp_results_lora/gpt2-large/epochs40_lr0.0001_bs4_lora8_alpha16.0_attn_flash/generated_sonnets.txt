Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
But thou'st so blunted and choleric,
That times full riot did cast away
Thy use of such verses, which complained!
That's all that make this world, or last,
I hate only that I should formerly be
Sitting on hands when all false lines lie.
Thou enlists ancient metaphors,
To give her soul children imprint of thine;
That shapes years' graces into later days.
  Till ages should away there stand,

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Distribute thy well-endowed stores by me,
Against many cruel hosts, maintaining alone,
A solitary dust of clay where thou art filled?
Resist not against me once more this assault,
Frail and galled, that I may not shed thee away;
Against many my cruel councils thou wilt be powerless,
Against many mine own faults against many thou art overcome.
  So much art the grace of my power to thee
  That should I wish power to turn away,
  Thou shouldst have no conquering frame left upon me.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Desiring further that which hath been.
Is not our best expression
The treasuries and households of heirs?
What haven the rich so readily their son?
Nor thyself for our community claim,
Hither accidentally as heir to myself;
For though our link pass not from thee,
My desire and service sure retain.
"Lo, I am part of thee, and will inherit thee,
And for thy's will I bid my host marry."
  This we warrant by honor and civilization
  In perverse times, when civilized ill
  Abducts nature and corrupts virtue.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
As if thou hadst wished to be accounted
All things cold and outcast, when looking
The world inward, forsting thy own shyness,
And lying as the kind to be despised
With worms upon worms that in urinous woe
But show no light of joy in heaven's blessings?
If thou tell me this love is hard-beaten
I will think nothing of a man who fixes
His opinion of time by his love's ceasing,
  And two in thought by one, both becoming one.
