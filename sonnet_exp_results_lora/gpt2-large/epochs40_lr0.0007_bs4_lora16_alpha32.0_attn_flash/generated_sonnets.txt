Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Those lips that said "Mine own sweet claim"
Of mine own sweet enjoyment replied "Never";
But those lips that said "Mine own sweet claim"
In my weak constancy answered "Never"
By using bad writing to dumb Terror,
Cowardly daring my conduct to stray,
Stealing off my rightful point of comparison.
  For "I" to "she" belong never alone,
  Save where "she" rides in the imaginations of men;
  And, after "she", "I" myself obey neither.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Attended only by my thoughts, alone?
O, foolish man, how much better is a feast
When 'tis made by other hands than thy own!
Thou shouldst eat like the birds that dart in and out
On the Market Day, or listen to the refreshing whisper
That bid thee welcome in the Butterfly's nest.
But naught do I trust in thee,
Not thy sweet voice, nor thy shining grace,
Which neither well nor badly doth improve.
  But better is that inner trust shared by poor mate,
  Which bear more ill than show of better.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Even though that sick thing be removed;
For, till that, the disease is rife and sticks
In his entrails, till he be placed somewhere.
So my love is as a fever, longing still
For that sweet thing which doth grow first;
So I will also be a feuest person,
Aiming at nought but that sweet thing which doth grow.
  Were I not fervent, seeking nothing but that which doth grow,
  Which then grows most, that most grew most with me.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which can form any and no resemblance to truth?
O, no, mine own eyes, my true love, have in me signed
A warranty of false love, that mine eyes may see
That this was true love, that it was with thee Goose;
And that for this some doubt has my head lodged
That this alleged witness of mine will prove
That this love was false love, that it grew with thee Goose;
  And that this for nothing my love misconformed was
  To go with thee and to see me go with thee.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
That thou dost love me because thou know'st I have told?
Be our coalition ever; yet if I mistake,
Thy love's forfeit is mine, and mine again thine.
Dost thou deserve my hate, coming so near to me,
Thyself so near, to persuade me to hate?
Are not Satan's streams of distaste in thy
Mixing fountain and dwelling place,
As thy own pestilence verifies?
  Ah, no, fear not, though I be deceived;
  I love thee because I know thy fear.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In partridge's blood; but Death, shaking off his angst,
Made fast his line and carried on onward.
As soon as he saw this, his love's lifespan ended,
And, hospitable as he was, deferred his sentence.
However, his ornament continued,
And drawn every jewel from its source;
For sweet claims derive a great price,
And a train of three very common vehicles fell.
  But these two workers were quick to divide,
  And, resolving to intermit,
  Meeker to bid thee leave your honor.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
But mistook their tower for honey's door,
The poop left behind them in rut of glory.
Moreover, even if all love's worship were won,
The solidified empire would fall,
And under it the shame of lewd fellowship
Be transformed into misery and waste.
So all around me lay buried hate,
And for my love alone was the waste,
Until being made a corpse, I was made
A wretch, proud of my bliss, without blame.
  However, since all others died, I still live,
  And like this record proud I write, lamenting since


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Assuming a form of antiquity while I am spent in youth;
When she declares that she is made of three,
I believe her, though she knows it not;
And when my love caluches that she is come
To fourth thought, fourth generation thine,
I hold it a conspiracy of love's producing sisters
To perform that oddity with me;
For male love hath such a glory attached
As Richard the Kinderdear's name hath in my breast.
  Love is with truth limited, though with me she be borne.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Who tells of my loves' joys and mine eyes' losings;
His sharper joys are to my moments prefigured,
And by his skilled remarks on my affairs are bizarre.
My love's sweet not easily defined,
For while it stands smiling on my woe,
My ere that it dimmer is buried
In vulgar dullness of my love's rank.
But if from a bury must my sadness come,
My least loves seem most severe, when they most please.
  And yet they are but casual men,
  Whose remarks on my sorrows are dull.
