My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Convinced that it will found a cure
Same way as his predecessor.
The physician's art is so consumed
With his laboratory, so rushed with study
That it hinders his spirit, which art so bold
To try a thing which no invention could effect,
Creating vice in everything he touches.
O, let those wonderful compositions be
Which in their construction intend but one,
One true thing, one true thing to say,
Thy beauty, thy chastity, thy truthfulness,
Which in thy abundance tyrants would ban,
Having thy best in submission, just where thou art.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That my love itself for sagacity displayed?
Why spend a thought on such irrelevant things,
When thou mightst thine own heart make merry?
Why rave at the height of joy,
When wit in joy advances more quickly than eye,
  Being winged only by the winged butt?
  O, all these are woe and woe,
  Till every doubt and every woe prove me false.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Floating on the wind of change,
Whence all this power she bears of flesh and blood.
But if she be untrue, in my judgment,
My love is razed, and I bear the loss.
To be assailed by facts proves my love true,
And losing love, I gain another;
And losing her, my loss is my love's gain.
  For still the die must have priority,
  Even if my love know no swift stop.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
With heart as thick as mine own, big even for heart,
And with lungs as broad as those of my hero;
And my companion is said to be such a sweet beast
As whenever he through airy flights leaps
Sings delights of angels and men of fashion;
Were it not sinful for my muse to hold his tongue,
My sweet poet being proud of his sweetness,
I should be neglectful of his fascinations,
Which seemed so sweet to me still.
  To all ordinary creatures my love is associated,
  unless they be demons or saints.
