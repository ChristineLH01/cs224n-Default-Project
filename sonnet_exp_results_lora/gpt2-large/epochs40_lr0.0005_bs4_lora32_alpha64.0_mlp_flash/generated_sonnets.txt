Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
For wealth thou hast not enough, luxury nothing secured,
For blunting the fine point of seldom pleasure?
Against this backdrop thine eyes should have looked,
If thou hadst studied this lusty time of year
When grapes would blush and skins be white,
Ready for the pressing of summer's day,
Like jars of lager in the siren's breath.
  But do not so. I love thee in such sort
  As, thou being mine, mine is thy good report.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
For no medicine can prevent its danger.
O, therefore, love, be of thyself so wary

Bearing thy heart, which I will keep so chary
As tender nurse her babe from faring ill.
If thou wilt leave me, do not leave me last,

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That hath got so far from adjustment?
O, none, unless this miracle have might,

  For sweetest things turn sourest by their deeds;
