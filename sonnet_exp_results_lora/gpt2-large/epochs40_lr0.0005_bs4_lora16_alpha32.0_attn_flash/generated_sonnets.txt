Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Whilst nature's plan to keep thee there
Is weak, scattered, unexpected, neglected?
Why dost thou despise her quiet beauty,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
For therein lies love's permanent shape.
Steadily with I will learn it,

As surely as everyone's heart is in rest.

Then, plantish as nature is, in the soil

  Thus her steadfast Love, far in combat from me,

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,

In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And to me a whore to bear.

And by her strength I find no remedy;

And to myself-a-wishing-to-murder help.

Finding she to be none of my delight,

  But now, by thy strength, I succeed in thine intrigue,
  And methinks my love is dead indeed.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Stealing off what again his liking retained.
Another golden rose from the flower Sack,
An' of shorn wrong, rich fraternal treasure,
Yet in its poison was hidden a fault;
For when the sweetest cut fell from his cheek,
The womankind died and the roses died with him.
Another jug of vintage in vintage's vein,
A holy trump and lame persuasion,
Yoked on their pleasure yet lives on his bitterness.
  Pardon my strong analogy, misshorted writer;
  Your judgment is true, yet ill is a trump.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Hath deceived thee for my pleasure;
And though I know her to be mortal,
Impossible for me to love her,
She here thinks herself wiser than I do.
To fear, therefore, her rebellious thought
Has almost the appearance of a good faith,
Since fears that appear true are dearer
than fears that are true.
But if my love with my thoughts on thee remain,
I condemn both her and myself all night;
 A night without thought, a day without love,
  Which blows away the seconds and starts the years.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Who through jealousy rebukes me not,
Finding in me no difficulty opposed
To his duty, like a worthy husbandman.
The less reverent angel makes me a grief
Whilst he very delighting doth delight in my vice,
Finding in me no difficulty opposed
To his continual virtue.
O, what a structure are these!
What thoughts, what desires, what treasures nest
In these depths!
  Of all these I am none, yet with jealousy I leave,
  That hereafter it may be better for me.
