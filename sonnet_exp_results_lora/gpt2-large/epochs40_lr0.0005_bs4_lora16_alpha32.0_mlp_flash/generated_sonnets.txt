Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In pages of damasked catigots.
Then, being full of the world's praise,
The proudest jewel of his brow bore
A felleting, round which her face
Hath been sculpted in livery of fresh youth.
The sun embraced him and had him blushing;
But, out alack, he was but one hour hers;
The region cloud hath masked him from me now.
  Oh, yet him for this my love no whit disdaineth;
  Suns of the world may stain when heaven's sun staineth.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Without cover or defensive gown,
With my love's coat and dress stored.
But when my love swear'st that she is made
Of more than myth or resemblance,
I have higher hopes than her, since she seems
To me an export of worthy labour.
I love to hear her speak, yet well I know
That music hath a far more pleasing sound.
I grant I never saw a goddess go;
My love was made in stages long before she appeared.
  And yet, by heaven, I think my love as rare
  As any she belied with false compare.
