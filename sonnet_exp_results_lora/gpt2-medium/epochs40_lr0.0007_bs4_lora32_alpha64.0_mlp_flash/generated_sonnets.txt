Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Whilst my poor lips, which should that sorrow bring,
At the mouth of my most gracious doom,
At first in their wills compiling

A throned queen in their shade,

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When thou thyself dost indulge
In all-too-precious things such as gems,
Robbing old clocks and antiquing minutes,
Making old truths of beauty old,
That Time to come will look askance
At thee, poor satellite of this world.
O, change thy thought, that I may change my mind.
Roses have thorns, and silver fountains mud;
Clouds and eclipses stain both moon and sun,
And loathsome canker lives in sweetest bud.
  Use not false painting to make old words new,
  Since beauty must


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Craving that which it hath least;
Gentle sickness hath filled up the fever's notch,
And in its eager rage is worked a thousand repairs.
Hang on that which doth stand,
That patient does not shrink from this extremity,
But all in war with his fever doth rage,
Fighting to cure the ills that did not grow.
  Such is my love, to thee I so belong,
  That, sick of thee, I'll stay and die for thy sake.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee?
The world will tell the difference if I see
Thy sweet beloved name no more after myself purposed.

And scarce believe my countenance in sleep,
When in myself thou art all gone,
For I in thy sweet patch am still heart's and soul's treasure.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
That thou consum'st thyself in falsehood.
O, from what power hast thou this strong urge
To slay my love in pursuit of truth?
To make me intermixed in thy show,
That I must attend with mine own love to tell the whole tale.
To do this I defy, although I swear it to myself alone.
In nothing art thou black save in thy deeds,
And thence this slander as I think proceeds.
  But did my heart not give thee that gift,
  Then, if I love thee, I will acquaintance strangle and look strange.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Because you are mine. True, I am true; but you forsworn me,
My suitor being slain, my life hath end.
For what care I who calls me well or ill,
So you o'ergreen my bad, my good allow?
You are my all the world, and I must strive
To know my shames and praises from your tongue;
None else to me, nor I to none alive,
That my steeled sense or changes right or wrong.
  For I must ne'er love him whom you forsworn,
  Unless you take that extreme


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
For to thee I bring in my superessence
And salary of my sovereign's pleasure;
For to thee I hold service, and service no end.
Till whatsoever king in shining armor doth ride
And stol'n my soft flesh with iron sword and robe,
And all the spoils of my vanquished rage;
And of thee my life, being made of four,
Sinks down to death, oppressed with melancholy.
  Then, if thou wilt leave me, do not leave me last,
  But in the least keep mine love a close watch.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep her in her youth.
But beauty's honey did not soothe her grief;
Instead of roses she combed her hair,
And hung her beauty's tresses in stalls;
But trumped beauty's long since fulfilled hoard
And heavy wrought age had her dress pressed to a fester.
Aless'ring beauty's beauty she saw,
And, sleeping on her wailing grief,
Crawls to judgment, where she exhibits
Harsh, featureless, and downright rude.
  Then, should some adversary arise,
  He should make her roast with candied love.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
At his gentle nap humbly watch,
Feeding his imprisoned pride with all his might.
The painful warrior famoused for worth,
After a thousand victories once foiled,
Is from the book of honor razed quite,
And all the rest forgot for which he toiled.
  Then happy I, that love and am beloved
  Where I may not remove nor be removed.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
While others admired her beauty,
Coming from a gilded tomb;
But when I see her countenance filled
With rich gems and precious moments spent,
Then of thy worth my judgment doth judge
And she in me doth confidently contend.
Then can I be certain that she loves me
Whilst I, deceived for love of thee,
Do not so much as my false heart doth tell,
But in what I most love she doth deceive me too,
  And that she hath thee as a closet
  Which hides but her shining eyes.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
A moderate learner, but not acquainted
With shifting change, as is false advertising;
He is not tongue-tied, though tongue-tied languages grow;
He likes not luxury, though the buyer be.
Our angel was honest, but capricious;
For with his prompting came of you a charm
Which I new pay as if not paid before.
  But since your goodness imbued my love,
  Mine be thy love, and thy love's use their treasure.
