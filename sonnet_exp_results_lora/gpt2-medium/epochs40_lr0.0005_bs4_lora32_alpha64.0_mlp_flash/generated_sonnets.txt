Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
But now my heart, converted from other thoughts,
Hath breathed anew that love's most fervent sigh,
And, without a sweeter joy, mocks her rejection.
Her heart, touched by compassion's fire,
Taught her tongue to sing a sweeter choir
And, sadly, lost her heart to despair.
  But who among the dead has heard
  Love's sweet sorrow brings and sings?


1
Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When thyself so rich should repay all this wealth?
Profitless thee, so rich and proud,
And many a worthy squanderer, her own life
Became her own thriftless pet.
Then, if thy spirit be of use, why dost thou waste
So great a sum of sums yet canst not live?
For, having traffic with thyself alone,
Thou of thyself thy sweet self dost deceive.
  Show pity to the world, or this glutton be--
  Lest beauty's image should make ugly worms.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Craving that which doth not depart.
My reason doth persuue me thus:
My love is ardor 'gainst ardor's desire;
So my verse, ardor notwithstanding,
To thee I send hence :
"Love asserts that it is amended."
"Oh, but what 'gainst ardor' means
  Shall 'gainst love, by joining the sick;
  So 'gainst love, I shall 'gainst fever,
  And by a sick man's discharge I am cured."


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
So that my sense, which hath not adjusted to your feature?
If my sight is true, then love's deceit is more,
Its truth obscured by all men's abysm.
Why should my sense, which well knows the dance,
Interpretate the hurt your features inflict?
Or should my eyes, which see, declare the opposite,
To grace your face with their twisted vision?
  In truth and right, my view and my show,
  Leese but their homage; they are blind to love's allure.

5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And paint deceit where truth should lay?
Whence cometh this corruption of the good,
That in thy vices I find charm,
In thy transgressions, I see beauty stood,
And in thy chaos, a soothing balm?
Who hath shown thee how to twist my heart,
To cherish more the more I see amiss?
Though I should push thee far, depart,
My heart still clings, with love grown' miss.
  If thy unworthiness hath wrought this love,
  More worthy am I to be loved above.


6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I betray
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may strive
For victory in love; the flesh, now alive,
Rises at your name, its purpose to derive
From your affection, its reason to thrive.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, ever so near,
  To stand by you, to fall by your side, my dear.


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
For how should I love thee when I am estranged
From thee, my dearest, only ever below?
By my wilful deed and actions beseeching,
My loves seem to me estranged, love abandoned,
Thy heart's deceit implied, yet I abide,
Unless this wickedness in my heart is found:
  For thee I love her purely because she loves me,
  And through my actions I am bound to thee.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Made holy the shrine of their love-filled dream.
The waters kept the fire's essence, though, alive,
A bubbling, healing source for all ailments dear,
Yet, when my sweetheart's eyes met mine, Love's torch burned bright.
To seek relief, I entered the healing waters, that very night,
But found no solace, for Love's true balm was out of sight.
The source of Love's renewal lay where it had first been doused,
In my beloved's gaze, the eternal, burning house.
The bath could not compare, its power weak


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth, unwell,
To ride the waves of false, twisted, warped belief.
Yet, I do not love her for my stars,
Because I ride the waves of her twisted, warped view.
I ride the wave of her self-made truth,
Her twisted reality, she disdains,
And sees its truth, as she disdains mine.
But why does she not admit her wrong?
And why do I not confess my age?
For love thrives on feigned trust and belief,
And the aged in love dislike revealed years.
  So we deceive one another in our flaws


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
While ill spirits mock their foul deeds.
The false angel sees her true grace and mistrust,
And thus she alters her appearance,
Stealing his essence, to make him anew.
She may look good, but I fear,
My false heart knows, it is a vile alias,
To dwell in your good thoughts and dreams.
  So I'll mock you, and then be true,
  For I poison you, and now you stand.
