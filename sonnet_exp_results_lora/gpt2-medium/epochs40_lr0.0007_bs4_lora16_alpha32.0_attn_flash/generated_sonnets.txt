Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
In comparison, her tongue uttered
A sweeter sigh from her heart than those
That dried and stifled at my lips' sullen flame.
Then she, in thought, did fashion a
Temptation wherein love did begin anew
To kiss the rose, and in love said,
"Nay, Love, I hate you more than I did."
Her tongue she unfurled, touched
With tender touch, so far from comparing true
With mean thoughts that another's lips did sett.
  "O, 'tis thee, 'tis mine!" she whispered,
  Lamenting

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
And oppress those with whom thou hast drawn thy allegiance?
Speak, daughter, of domineering fortune,
And tell me how sweet life is when the partner doth command
His share in thy heart, with each and every thing gladdens.
Take heed, though I may wish it otherwise,
That thy heart does not consume yet thy income,
Rather, after death, keep it as a stewardship
For posterity's keep, and thyself divine.
  Preserve what is rightfully yours, and bind
  The miserly care of thee, sweet child.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
A starving existence, even in the stoutest of springs.
So my love doth begat crop after harvest's end,
As the harvest beseeches the poor, cringing
At the sweet treatings of the sick, their only shield.
Unsatisfied, 'neath the guise of sloth
My heart finds new life, once more driven by hope,
Placing my head beneath the sway of day.
  Even as the sun wanes, so my soul doth rise,
  Resurrecting once more, from horrors so hard.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
A tender heart that knows no pain;
Look upon my lover's eyes, as counsels they lay,
And say 'not my love may someday grow old.'
See how fond my soul is, now grown old,
In love's charm, so deep and endless,
An ardent spirit, full of wisdom and laugh.
So too, might you look upon my love with disdain,
Like the raven's hush, or the golden sun,
Cultivated by pain, or fallen from grace;
  And, if you love, seek guidance from that point.

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
She quitted her lover, thus making him her companion,
And thus was love distilled in love's first, glorious sight.
A naked man, in hunger he fed,
A cow milked by his passion's hunger fed,
A pheasant slaughtered by a passion so strong,
And so, I, on my wanderlust wander.
The world writhes with longing, every day,
An unbearably long wait, unvarnished yet true.
  Yet, in love's strange, unwavering grace,
  I find my treasure, my love, in bright hope.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Their own tender lips in a full glow,
Their hearts and tresses coiled around their suave,
With their own lovely lips saturated,
From which his breast dipped down in desultory freshen.
But lucky Hercules, by his skill summoned
To aid his lover's dreamlike sleep, fell into love,
As his free-loving spirit awoke he found his host,
A scion of good fortune standing tall.
  So he signed the lovers' kiss with his own heart,
  And sent them to sleep, his own soul content.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
His shadow sickened by the sun's warm glare.
My maleficent spirit wants to be plucked from me,
And in my sorrows all my hopes are crushed.
Ah, may my love depart from this turmoil,
For love's tender bosom supports my strand,
Through tears my husband gives his strength.
I fear lest my sorrow's shadow should steal the show,
Though unseen evils may wreak havoc.
  So I'll stand, though ill fortune may thwart,
  For love's sweet reason keeps my thoughts clear.
