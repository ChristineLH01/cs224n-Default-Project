Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
That voice uttered those words that drove me down
Like her snow-flake to this forgetful realm;
To me she wore such shrouds of pall,
And I, for deprived of love, did kneel
To forget her as though she'd fled.
Well, if she bore you down, that voice,
You could not but weep for my heart,
Lest sorrow set you free and paint your cheeks
A shade of shade like the nightingale's face.
  But if she marains you, then you have lost your grip
  And all I had was her love's own making

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When all my virtues are put in harmony,
And combined, they me thus helpless?
To whom do you lend your charm, that drags me along,
Adorning thy outer walls with fine art,
While robbing me of vision, all in single part?
Indeed, 'tis thee that gives my plight,
An ally to my corrupt heart, grows weak,
As if it knows, by some hidden affair,
That I, a tyrant, find solace elsewhere.
  Pity me, then, dear heart, know this truth:
  My loyalty lies with thee, so selfsame

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And by that greed, I am drowned.
Pity the patient, who under these cruel groms
Waits for release, though not granted his boon;
Yet, like a besieged bear,
His thirst is 12 years' worth,
And he bears the weight of love's balance.
  In love's fine sense, one cannot stomach
  More than a suckful bite at the first.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
So dearly mistaken in intelligence?
No skill have I to mark the gait of thy grace,

Who hath no face that reads warn
That thou art gentle, fair and true?"
To me, no grace hath my heart borrow,
Whose every form I frown, reviles
His truth by surface of all thy darling graces.
  And thus they my judgment should misuse,
  Of hate, of love, and all together have a part.

With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
It's but thee, from whence this vanity springs.
Who hath taught me to write such lovely praise,
To dignify thy worth with trifles new?

To trust thy truth more than thyself?

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest sorrow instil sorrow in thy sight;
And, unmindful of thine own gaze, creep
Into thine own soul, and therein dwells guilt.
If you must escape my guilt, carry my remorse back,
And take my guilt lightly; yet, love, observe
The severity of your affection, surpass
My guilt by swift and swift causes.
O, thus, thou sly sleuth; a true soul
Stands firm, despite rosy cheeks and wrinkles;
If caught, forgiveness does not send,
Nor find its name in print.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
By covenant by thy side;
Yet in deed thou art as pledged to me,
Thy deed my redeaser regrets not,
Making old wounds anew chaste trace.
That thou couldst sourly abandon is not my fault,
It is my nature, and desire,
That binds thee to me, like a bereft lover.
  For I have sworn by thine institution,
  To one whom thou duped, and still love thee so.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
The babe's eye, from whence the flame began,
Was dimmed, and the coolness ate.
A cure for this lamp-fire by heat did aid,
But when I sought to quench the glow of love's fire!
Love's fire heats the eye, but not the heart;
Celestial love's heat does not quench the flame's meet.
  Yet, if this be error, and my heart be untrue,
  No words were writ, and love's flame fell to ashes.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Came dancing by; one maiden, purest of eye,
Dressed no woman's part, but his own, and left
His fiery brand to languish awhile,
Her maiden hand the sovereign's, alive,
More blessed was the lover's deed,
For white night was there with his fire
When Love's own blood red locked the white veil.
A certain maiden maiden, so chaste, did confess
Her love to a stranger;
This was the fire that brought Love's fire to life,
  And I, dearly wedded, burned with fire that day.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my false one a raven with raven's wings.
The cruelest devil loves me best,
And he that caresses me most sad,
That spares me from my waking hours.
For why in myself do I think I'm made
That so? When in myself I see sweet beauty's
Beauty gone by moon, no wonder then
The vast world is made nigh.
  And thus they fare, that I in my reverse be
  To thee, sweet friendship, do rejoice with day.
