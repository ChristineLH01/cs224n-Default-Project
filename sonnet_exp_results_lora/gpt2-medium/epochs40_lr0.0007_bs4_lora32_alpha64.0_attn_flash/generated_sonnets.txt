My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And rouses the sanguine: then am I am cured
Of that which in me doth lie idle,
Savage and crazed, both by that which doth feed.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which observes yet the world's outside not seen?
If they see divers things, but in my mind
One single design imprinted on my breast,
Where heaven's goddesses and earth together conspire
To paint one single thought of mine in black and white?
O, no eyes! but pure passion doth stare,
Which often misinterpretes my song,
And leads me far from truth's blessed grace.
  Far from malice is love's look so hard,
  Which minds of men may mock and find no grace.

A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Into the bed of womanhood it bit,
And plunged itself into a well nearby,
A cave, deep and eternal, untouched by Time's hand.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And nothing in me worth teaching save what she believes.
But since she perceives me as false, she thinks otherwise,
And I, abashed, bend all my worth to accommodate
The appearances of my defects with praise;
This petty suspicion, which I ought to avoid,
Sums on me, and leaves me alone.
Yet, when this suspicion passes and my love looks
Well on the countenance of my flaws,
I begin to deify her;
  For pure reason does not love slander,
  And she for no reason whatsoever does deign.
