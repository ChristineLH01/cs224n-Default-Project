Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
That tongue that conveyed the message
Of distress to me that she knew not yet hear.
But lo, after the noise of two larks,
I heard another, a sweet one that sweetened my sorrow
And told me that he too would kiss me
When that I should be dead from want of speech;
That was the first time in my life that I heard speech,
Though not by that sweet note which loves so ill.
  But when that intrusion of tongues stopped,
  I found another, and found that he too sang
  To me that I would gladly listen again.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
As punishment for thy sins?
The earth is my home and I love it dearly,
But why dost thou chide me for doing so
In outward favor and doing so low a thing
In selfless love for thy benefit?
O, do not chide me thus: I have reason to pine
In thy well-deserving decline and do battle
Against thine invading grace, which thou hast so high.
  Thy counterattacks are brilliant and thy offense
  Fortifies me against thy hard blows and I gain rest.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That every thing under my sway is superstition?
What hath heaven's orbit calculated
That my fair heaven's pavement paid no rent
To this great foundation of love?
No more heaven, no more sun, still I think they are.
But me they are, and so I am;
Since no heaven can give me so fair
As my inferior heaven does reckon,
Or me inferior heaven forsooth.
  Now therefore methinks my thoughts are false,
  Being false thoughts, and thoughts unchaste;
  For being true, thy thoughts are equally true.

5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And thou the strongest lure, strong as the sea,
To keep my motion straight. Ah, but 'tis not so.
Truly from myself thou art the strongest,
And I soon find my weak spot tired,
Because thy strong will so overpower me
To leave poor me with a lie.
Give me thy strength, therefore, and I swear I'll do nothing
Save lie, with strong thought to thee;
Till I can think no more, yet swear it to my heart.
  In this matter I learn nothing but thee,
  Save that thy love for me is strong.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
When thou dost knock at my sweet sight,
Beauty's infant self hath that sweet habit set.
Priestess with her beauty she dispels,
But Fortune in her thrones chases her down,
And, mysteriously, my sweet love aids in this sad attempt.
Thou, gentle queen, wilt restrain my amorous moan,
The lute trained in bass and harmony unrestrained,
Though my bass refrain leave my hear.
  But Fortune blocks this, and forbids thee
  To play the bass solo no more.

I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
As fair as her mother's fair, but mock you for beauty.
For what wind could soch her fair shape into shape,

Consuming truth for truth, like a gilded couch;
  Since truth, built for truth, is no cheer
