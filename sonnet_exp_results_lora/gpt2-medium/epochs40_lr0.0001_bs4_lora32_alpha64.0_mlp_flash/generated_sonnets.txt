To me that languished for her sake;

Disarmed the robber of my dreams,

Against me, I called her a liar,

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Causing my poor health to groan and waste?
Shall such a dwelling house hereafter be
As that given to thee by Fortune's jaundiced art?
Him! What solid base element dost thou consume
That birth new life in thee, even as mine?
Quack medicine, why should poor health be now better
Or water for drinking water give life to thee?
  Unless you're devils, enchanting me,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
The evening sun, with his fervent blaze on the ground,
Awoke Love-dance, with eager might,
And shone his fierce brand upon the sleeping world.
Cupid, sadly, saw this and set his fire,
At the source of his love's efface, a counterfeit.
The true love, washed and cooled, rose once more,
A gentle flame, a beauty to outlast,
Yet, if this be false, no more life I know.
  Love-brand fires do not fade,
  They stand still, aflame, forever kindled.
