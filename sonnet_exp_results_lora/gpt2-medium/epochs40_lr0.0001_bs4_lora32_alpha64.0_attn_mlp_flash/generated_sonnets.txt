Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
If that be false, and you love me anew,
I cannot taste of death, for I am confined
To the sweet pleasure of your heart's embrace.
If she be true, and your soul doth part,
She hath tasted what love's sour breath doth great despise,
And banquets on the bitterness of a sport;
Since this was her intent, 'tis foul game to blame,
  Lifting her tongue to state what she disliked.
  But if you persevere, and I believe,
  Your love quenches thirst like the autumnal blossom.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Decorating thy outer walls so gaudily?
Who hath power to tear thee from this earthly plane,
That so self-made, self-piercing, governs
Every aspect of thy being, in every part?
Silent witness to thy rebellion, forgive
My transgression, so traitors dispraise;
In thy inaction, I now meditate,
While hating myself, unable to see
The helplessness of my sinful deeds.
  Yet, as angels, I fall into despair,
  Unable to find the peace that my soul requires.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Exchanging for a friend that much more friend.
So I, ensnared in this thrall, do call:
"If sickness fall not on thee, but on this fool,
I shall die of shame; and him life shall die
On this false testimony of mine."
This word, once prepared, still hath force
Even as the morning fire brings forth its blaze;
  'Tis not madness, but feigned cheer,
  That my heart should rush to this sweet deceit.

I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,

11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And like him I for climate dislikes extremes;
And like him I for walks do dislike ebb and flow,
Pitying and weeping till at last
I find myself in the goodness of peace.
Yet him I call to mind when I see
Deprived of joy, starved and vexed;
Infirm and poor, meek and keen are
Together, and I for despair in love.
  For him I hope there is hope, and him for me
  To shun is neither for love nor for fear.
