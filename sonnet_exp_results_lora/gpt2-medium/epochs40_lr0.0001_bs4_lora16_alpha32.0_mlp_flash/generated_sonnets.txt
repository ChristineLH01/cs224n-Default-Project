Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Obscured by shadows, while my deeds soar?
Why dost thou torment my heart so,
Concealing the light of day that shines so bright,
And in thy darkness, I find my way?
Then claim I my love, thy virtue, so welcome,
With gentle words, while intent to teach me how
To overcome the foe that corrupts my soul.
  Let my written word speak truth, without fear,
  For thou art cunning, and dost thy cruel spite ensue.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
For, being blind, they affirm no business at all?
If blind things do think and see,

At times swallowing up look and from view;
Observing dark parts, they easily become babes
And read with inverted sight the stars themselves.
  If thy sight is altered from what is seen,
  Mine too is because it by thy instrument lied.

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest guilt's shadow might blind your soul.
Confess your heart lies within one of gleaming stars;
And thus, Love, you will find a blissful reprieve,
Even amidst the storm's embrace.
Why twist and spin when your sight is clear?
Tell the truth, yet honestly perceive.
Honor what's sacred, let no blemish ever blight,
Even if it hurts you so deep.
  Forge a second self, kinder, more gracious,
  That compassion may merit love's rightful place.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And hate used as accuser of old.
I have seen sire him, so steadfast in love,
Yet falling from favor, unheard;
My daughter thinks him thine only daughter, valiant,

Making him daughter's chief begger.
Must he deserve sympathy, seeing woe he gives

  I hope not, for the sake of thy name, it is
  In hearts apart, love in hatred turns cold.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though I, in truth, conceive her to surpass.
Her love is kinder than feigned, though the true esteem,
Her beauty too, too, has my feigned shine;
For she bears the snare of hatching false scents,
Adorning false women's faces and tarnishing stones.
O, what a sanctuary for falsehood has chosen
To dwell in my heart, where its verdant air
Conserves my truth and my bad deeds speak!
  To shun this haven is to embrace the truth,
  For love thrives in falsehood, and so, I repeat.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Whose looks behold a sour disposition;
And in his price, ill counsel.
The stronger man ill refreshed expects refresh,
And sends him forward, more uplifted.
He wishes the glory of old to fare anew,
A man not so ancient nor so poor,
But keeps the recompense in circulation,
A highly polished treasure to lend.
This fantasy shows him well, and what effect
It imprints in me, I cannot unlearn.
  In one of his good acts, he embraced
  Another good, a love unconfined.
