Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Before, when she breathed, what could I say
But that I was her greatest and most precious jewel.
I found, or thought I found, no such lip;
In them her sweet right was crowned,
And in some of them my greatest grief lies.
I love her because I think she deserves;
And though I hate her, yet I love her because I know she suffers;
And though I hate her, yet I know she loves God and loves her dearly;
  And, by these views, I conceive a greater delight
  To know her mournful than her senseless laugh.

Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Where, throughout the day, I watch the clock for my true sight?
Neither the eye nor the soul
Of my body doth tell the story of my life;
So my name is writ in every book,
And I have eyes to reply to every book.
In some antique book is there more delight
Than in the breath that from my soul reeks;
And in some antique song is there more delight
Than in the breath that from my body doth sing.
  For breath hath eyes to reply, and for soul to approve.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
And for that sorrow which I then did feel
Thou shouldst depart, from me altogether canceled,
The solemn grounds of my love making no defense.
Then, if for my love thou wilt depart,
My life, being made of four, with two alone
Sinks down to death, oppressed with melancholy;
Until life's composition be recured
By those sad angels that weep hear
When thou from them returnest.
  Then may I forgive thy robb'ry, gentle thief,
  Since that thou stole thee all my poverty.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
That thou art the fairest and most precious jewel.
O, if for my sake thou wilt depart,
Leave me alone, but in my love's place stay
And play the coward to give my love another youth.

For truth proves thievish for a prize so dear,

  But do not so. I love thee in such sort
  As, thou being mine, mine is thy good report.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In her sinful veins the blood of his bride.
But when he rose from the bridal canopy,
His white morn answered with a frown;
A hooded murderer no whit so fair
As her sweet self, though yet awake,
Was a tattered mess of prime.
Finally his white morn gave him another shot,
And forthwith her white robes were stained with crimson;
But, for his whit, she bore him down alive,
And he, by sleeping, spoiled his beauty.
  More him she found, and yet he did boast
  That he was her great treasure.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Resembling strong youth in their statues,
Yet mortal looks adore his beauty still,
Attending on his golden pilgrimage.
But when in his sleep a vision presents him
Above the golden gaze,
The eyes, 'fore duteous, now converted are
From his low tract and look another way.
  'Tis him, indeed, that doth beauty's course grow
  Where terrestrial plants are wont, and him that doth draw.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unacquainted with truth's brevivest show,
Making old defenses for her when she scorns it;
And, for she loves truth so,
I will attend time's leisure with my love,
Making old defenses for her when she scorns it;
Then, if she denies my love,
My love's good report must not be so my praise.
  For she loves truth so, that she hath scanted all
  Where she can lay great esteem.
