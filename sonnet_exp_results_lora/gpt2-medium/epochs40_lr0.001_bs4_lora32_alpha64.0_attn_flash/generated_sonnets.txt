Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
For all such lips were but signs of love,
And such lips were but promises made to me.
But now that Otherere signs of love abate
My very heart, filled with heavy grief,
Appears to my very heart one that calmness affords.
My very soul doth say "This is not love,"
But "this is evidence of strong feeling,"
That no words of condolence
Can so strong my heart to move replies.
  Then is this mystery solved,
  When in my heart no words of condolence dare belong.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
For mine own good is bad enough
To make him a sheep for thy show?
O, no, my soul loves thee so; it is my wish
That my thoughts, 'tis well planted within thy mind,
To bear witness of thy true love.
Take heed, dear soul; my heart is my love,
And I must not pine away
Whilst thoughts of mine own true love do so overpower
That I, losing thy sweet self, faint in tears.
  But receive not my cry; my poor soul
  When thou my affections do strong overwhelm me.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
As when the worthless girths of the dead are cut,
Or that which in full length doth lie buried.
So my love is not shortened, for that shortenedness is
A hardship; but my love is shortened
To that which does more need my care take.
Therefore my love is not shortened, being made stronger
By that which it doth by need hold;
And that which is shortened is ill, not loved;
  For that which is well brings ill to its doom.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That is not in some fair guess, but in
Some fair made of fair things to be seen.
O, what fair beams hath my mind's lens borrowed,
That without bushel most recently laid,
Which might in my fair days have hinted,
That in my fair estimate lay?
Mine eyes, if they had eyes, 'tis fair in them to say
"This is my true love," etc.
  But God forbid they might say more,
  "That which in my sight lies most."

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
And then for my sake alone do I dwell,
Living in thy absence, hating myself for ever?
Look what a husband brings forth after his return
In that which he hath not left behind,
But, like an empty tomb, bears witness
To what is hidden there still.
O, what a happy wife do we wish
In that happy couple which never leaves us,
Wedded to our love, yet desiring new love;
  For why should we stay so long, when we can see
  Only one love after so many?

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Against his free will scoffed at his moan;
And, forlorn hope in a miserable death,
Winston's cruelty laid bare his justification,
And beauty's wont praise dryly executed;
Then, as all forlorn travelers
To their destinations find, mine eyes only to look were
Their ponderous brains to morn;
  For, when they grieve their part,
  The nightmare still makes them glad.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
While in truth I am wholly imbued with love.
Then would I my love be an untutored boy,
Who for his part of truth holds no love in his breast;
For if I in love did defraud him of sight,
His laughter would be an unthankful burden on me.
O, no, though I love her truthfully,
I do not think my love disparaged;
It is but my love that I think what is best.
  In this case my love is not false,
  It is my love that builds me up.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And, though imperfect, still loves me pretty well;
For, though imperfect, he still loves me so.
But the fair that suggests my worth lies with my sweet love,
Whose texture and tone are of fair and fair'd;
Not my fair, my angel tells me what I like;
So my love is made up of two lies.
In one I see my imperfect love plain,
In the other my true love well known.
  If this be done with my best reason,
  The best part is in the uttering of it.
