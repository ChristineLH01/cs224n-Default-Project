Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Nor are the kisses you so beloved shaking.
Do not shake them when your hand is shaken;
Shake them when you lift them from the ground.
Look on your beloved without any stain;
Whether you are blessed or not, you are true.
Even so, if you were false, I would not fail;
Even so, I would not write "you were false,"
But rather "your true acquaintance lies here,"
To show how far nature bent her knave to bend.
  To bend is easier than to tear; so let nature bend

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
Let nothing of my affection preserve me fond,
But for thy sake my dear child's life must seem dead.
A wife's grief is a new woe; her sorrow is a changed face;
Her loss is not her loss, but a new woe.
To lose a wife is to lose both husband and wife;
A loss of both must mean a loss of both,
And, therefore, grief is a grieve that loves nothing.
  In love of thee I find all my hate,
  And all my hate finds no patch.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Who doth call joy and frown alike;
And likewise the gourmand wine hath his sourness,
Which in me doth dwell and grows sour by nature.
I have love of both sorts, which heaven knows best,
But perfect harmony hath none but my dandy friend,
And though sweet words seemed all the better,
They were tenants of distaff and wretched effect.
I thank heaven that heaven hath design wrought
And that full satisfaction in my desire satisfied;
  For there is no better minister
  To pour sweet thanks on my unjust love.
