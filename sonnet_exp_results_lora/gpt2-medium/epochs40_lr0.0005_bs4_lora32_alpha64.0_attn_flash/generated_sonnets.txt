Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And thus, shamefaced now, she drank from
My sorrow's vial, like a poisoned worm,
Her pride bringing shame on the lowly,
And tormenting pity filled her lips with.
O, folly! she did not word correctly
To shame me where love shone so bright,
But hurt me where her love did persuade,
And taught her tongue, with false pride,
A borrowed phrase, as sorrow did afflict.
  But I, in my folly, did hear her utter,
  And compare that which my heart does speak.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Decimating the very foundation of thy virtue?
Who hath the power to destroy that immortal treasure
Which in thee doth nest, nest, and grow?
In thee, dost thou see thy weakness, enlarge,
A bigger tumor, a bigger healing remedy,
More powerful than sanguinary bleach?
Grant me the strength to doubt, in thee,
The strength to cure my sickness without bloodshed,
The void shall triumph, time's sovereign enmity.
  Crook, then, true friend, find thyself within,
  An inhabitant of time, who still lives in thee.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
With present feeling it drinks.
So I, a miser, in love's distress
Buy by future dues what was lent before,
Despising times that paid me such dues,
Enjoying what had been lent but once.
For every once in a while my love looks for
The sweetest of dross, rejoicing that which borrowed it;
And then, lost and angry, I hoard
The cash, hoping to find more at home.
  In love's sweet adventure, I find my way
  And in pain, my love's fever dances.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Where sin's shadow falls down on my face?
If eyes hath no vision, what worth it is,
That it calls on the veil to lift it above,
To gaze upon sin's wicked forms and lies?
If eyes, 'tis not truth that they gaze upon,

Adorned with wrinkles, they perceive wrongs wide,
Sunken under the gaze of all beauty's lamp.
  Yet my heart, unblocked, waits for thee to prove,
  Lest my eyes should find faults in love's false scheming scheme.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And hide what truth I should convey?
Whence cometh this want of truth,
That in thy vices I see no cure,
And in thy sins do I find sweet solace?
Who hath taught thee to make me sour by thy cap
And to inflame my heart with eager desire?
Who hath taught thee how to inflame my heart,
To love more fiercely when I am loathed?
  Sweet mind, observe not thy tread,
  For I thy insatiable heart cannot be quenched.


6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entwined with my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I betray
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My flesh reports to my spirit that it is mine,
A captain of love; the spirit doth protest,
"Not in him."
So, 'tis true, 'tis my flesh that informs
The spirit's desires, and forms bonds with thee.
  And yet, if you but persevere, you'll lose sight,
  For, like a seal, your heart's desires are sated.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
Now my love, though dead, will stand the same age,

Strangled worm; yet had I lived that way,

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
The very valley where his passion occurred
Were filled with mist, a perpetual day,
Or whenever daylight entered, he swept it away,
Impeding no matter where it did lay dormant.
But from above this point on the face of heaven,
A picture of him seen day by day,
And heaven itself, the holiest place,
Came to view his resting-place, searching for him,
And made him immortal, forever new.
  This song of love is borne, every night,
  To remind him where he goes to look and be done.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Had abandoned their station, uninfluenced,
To embrace a worthy companion's embrace.
One maiden, the fairest of souls,
Lifted by the gentle hand of beauty's chosen,
Her beauty unblemished, unblemished,
By true art's standards, she could not slay,
Unless art should take its place, perhaps.
  Then, by chance, my love's sleep came upon me,
  And behold, her heart was captivated by my fire.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And her beauty looks but fair enough for me.
But she denies that my unkindness
Is her wildness too fine for common earth;
That I am "forced," she adds,
To the deception of her husband's scrupulous tongue.
I cannot swear she is true, she counters,
Yet I do repeat her words, because of
Their mutual profit, a dozen fold spread.
  She loves me because I am her,
  And she loves me because I am hers, for she knows me she knows.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my terror pure, a flame;
In one thing, my good heart is blissed,
While in others my thoughts are entangled.
My terror's husband does envy his guest,
And to him I send this verse so sweet.
Papa Emmerich, be thou my love, sweetheart;
In weakness, my spirit doth laugh,
Until he perceives my plight.
  Or if he reads this, I shall be lame,
  And suffer greatly as mine.
