To me that languished for her sake;

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When every private hamlet is well-spent?
Why invest so much in thyself thy beauty's foundation,
A structure bound to crumble, fade, and fall?
For Nature's treasure is but a hoard,
Depleted, spent in her eternal fight.
Thou, so fond and so fond, hast added much more,
In thyself thyself thyself disgraced,
By wasting beauty's wealth, thy own sweet self.
  Yet, love, persist in thy spite, for now I know
  You love those whom thou dost destroy.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Resembling a sire who with his offspring doth nurse,

  Blessed are you whose birth affords
  Such richness, that you yourself may boast.

Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entwined in my wrongs.

I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Had I not known what religion was,
And found out vows yet ministered,

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And in his progress is creeping.
For when he descends from his spring,
My love's sweet picture remains,
Like a faded legend whose worth I cannot hide.
The spring shall offer a prize to all,
Whatever the elements in it are.
My love shall there dwell, free from blemish or flaw,
That every tongue, the soul's, shall sing,
Of love, its beauty, in truth it doth lie.
  So, I love him, for I believe in thee,
  And in that spirit, I find my soul's dove.
