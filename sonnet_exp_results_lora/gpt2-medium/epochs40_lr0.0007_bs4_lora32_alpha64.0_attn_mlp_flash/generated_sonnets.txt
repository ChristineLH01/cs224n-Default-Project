Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Yet then my judgment knew no reason why
My feelings should then be changed.
But when my changes were explained,
My changes were explained to my delight,
And my life, supposing it were new,
Received a second turn in my love's sweet sickle.
I was then sold as a babe;
Now sold as a beloved friend
And by a third brought low
By questioning whether my friend hath gone mad.
  No sooner had I ceased to hate,
  But then her sweet face filled up the page with hate,
  And thence this riot became a custom.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When in thy power all is cast away?
Grant, if thou wilt, thou art the wretch
Which in thy life works for evil,
And works even to make the world unloved,
Suff'ring creation for thyself to be thy show.
Look what an unprovident father child
Doth do inherit thy sweet appearance's cost,
And in tipsy rage doth thrust foul lines in beauty's brow,
Causing ugly beauty to be a father's son.
  But if thou live remembered not to be,
  Die single, miserable spite of thy posterity.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
As I will myself oppervune if I think of you.
Be where I may not be, and yet love knows no reason why
My deepest love should afterwards be nullified;
Therefore, like a lark at break of day arising
From sullen earth, I pine and sway with my drooping head,
Doing no lifting of limbs, nor adding weight to my show.
  But when your shadow eclipses, bright days come
  And frown on me with golden eyes, then I sorrow,
  Because I cannot true love to you pay.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And therefore my love may be false,
As she herself assures me.
She may be right, though I know it to be false;
She may be false, though I know it to be true.
Both her and I are liars, and both lie to my purpose,
Saying I thought my love was founded on truth.
She may be right, though I know it to be false;
And yet, by my lie, myself and our daughter
Eat grass, and I ill approve;
  For sweetest things turn sourest by their deeds.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And though in his violence often appears,
He is but the herald to my wakened fancy;
For though his strength is broken,
My love's not. For if it were, it might be said
"Because he loves me now, he loves you down;"
And in the strength of this thought my heart hath a cry,
"Sweet love, what a misfortune wouldst thou prove
If love were anything but fair."
  So then my love is not false, though my heart be
  To hear what its owner thinks true.
