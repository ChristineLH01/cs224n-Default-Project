To me that languished for her sake;

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
As greed's bloodthirsty appetite taketh away
The rancor of a seasoned breast.
So when this love dies, my reason no more,
Leaving thee baffled, I am left bereft,
In admiration of thy serene stealth,
And all my chivalrous virtues I bolster,
In praise of thee, my sweet excuse.
  Thus, I am alive, in love, steadfast and steady,
  And each time I ponder the passage,
  The love potion heals the poisoned heart.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
A traveler, passing by, in contemplation of love heard
A shout, which echoed to every ear:
"Thou hast made me love thee by unwavering slaying's hand,
And thus shalt I pervert my life's trend."
Then arose another thought, from whence came this noxious moan:
"Where did this passion for the sake of my own grace
Take root, from whence this healing grace to dispense?
  My love did joust with this scornful mockery,
  And now mine eyes, by my fire doth disengage."

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Sealed in wax and precious gems imbrued,
Aspiring love, thirsting to reach out and snatch.
I, for want of sleep, do then sleep afar,
Crying not for your own but for your lad's affection's well,
Replacing tender love's own, all friendship's passion quelled,
A saver, therefore, in devotion's every charge,
Where every good thing I lend garners her plight.
  Thus I am, and thus are, availing ill,
  For thee, my love, while he lapses, she herself lazes.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
For those who wear carnal ears
Who revere her in their hearts do not endow
Faith in her true voice, but converse,
Prove to me that she is not simply weak,
But has seized the truth, her conceit so dearly,
And dares not to challenge me with originality.
At times she puts my trust in youth's innocence,
Though she's taken my turn to betray,
I know better than she does her tongue holds.
  She smiles at my truest defects, her knowledge full,
  And then turns away in contempt, deceiving.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my troubled man is an affliction hateful;
So thus two in love life outplay bled side to side,
Like a faithful jester scattered far.
