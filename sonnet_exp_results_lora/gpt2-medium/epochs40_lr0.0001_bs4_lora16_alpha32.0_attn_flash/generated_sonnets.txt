Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When worldliness knows there are to be all under loathsome graces?
If Dost love the lowest boundless lots, dost live,

From where my daughter's kingdoms raise kings domineering behests?

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And death beguileth a suppliant the days.
An enemy of those warm and lively years,
I now despair of the peaceful morning.
The roses grew wild by my absence,
Yet I embrace on their blossoms, once more,
The melancholic heart watches this quarrel.
Thus far love adhered, fleeting, to day,
With cloudy skies outside its dense skies,
And into the gloom thinks it wax clear,
Awakening dawn, still surpassing this world.
  But when the sun consumes the eve,
  The fillings stink and the caprice blind.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
When truth makes faces to spring into what I lack?
To call me fool, fool's flaw, malicious, vile,
Impartial, deceitful, callous, inaccessible!
You have banished me from home where I find
Peace and quiet in swift transit;
Refused, mocked, trifled with in detail,
Adorned with taste, making perfect so.
The sweetest dearest pleasure conceals delight,
If love feels no terror that shadow matches;
  Whose turn I turn is twisted to follow you.

With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
The totality of my departed visions

Adding milk to bestow healing thy truth.

Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Nor force my name to speak on your side.

A maid of Dian's this advantage found,

At waking virginity, uncollected.
Cupid, on the foundations of fame with affright,
Bent his love's legs on summers nights unburdened;

Ablamed no summer's garden but fallen skies.

Dian tamed that power by worlds spend;

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
His origin in pain, but my love is time.
In souls desolation, he finds fare,
But face to face, he marrows, and mutters.
In thee, I hold myself poor, stockade'd,
A crown of despair, like sovereign's dying,
Given in treason that ill unjust causes mak.
A love identical, boundless beyond words,
In thee, both life and death stand,
Unyielding to change nor pain bar.
  Life shall I live, through thee standing,
  And Death shall I close, in love's endless threat.
