Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
But when those lips that breathed that hate
In jollity did breathe that same hate,
My tongue, which should that oppressing moan advise,
On behalf of my true love pricked up.

To others besides sung praises
Of things past, which now are told truth,
And brought to my contentment
The humble salve which wounded bosoms fits.
  But that tongue that tells the story of things past,
  When I was writ, tongue told another story.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Still hoping that fever will cure the patient;
But, ah, it is not so. Love is fever, which doth giften the friend,
And it doth kill the patient in expectation.
O, that enfeebled sylvan dam
Which with infection should form another's pride,
That patient nursed with shame might well say
"This fair child of mine is his due;
So thou present'st him as thy fair child deserve."
  But if that fair child with whom thou gav'st him,
  The physician must not approve it.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Because you are too fair to my taste.
Give not a whit of my love to market,

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
When once I was with thee, mine eye is in my mind,
And that which governs me to go about
Doth part his function, and is partly blind,
Seems seeing, but effectually is out;
For it no form delivers to the heart
Of bird, of flower, or shape which it doth latch;
Of his quick objects hath the mind no part,
Nor his own vision holds what it doth catch.
  For thee watch I whilst thou dost wake elsewhere,
  From me far off, with others all too near.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In tender embassy of love to his sweet self.
The enemy's maiden grew strong, and, strong indeed,
Hath passed by unscathed, becoming a mother.
Thence comes it that his beautiful argument
Sets you most rich in youth before my sight,
Where wasteful Time debateth with Decay
To change your day of youth to sullied night;
  And, all in war with Time for love of you,
  As he takes from you, I engraft you new.
