Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
That lovely inward sigh which falls
When I, contracted to go, cease trying to say
"Now my sweet love hath all too brief a date,"
Marking the time that did dishonor
By premature marriage and barren rage;
That hurtful "Now my love hath all too brief a date,"
Which time changes to negotiable leave
And leaves it hanging open to borrow again.
  Lo, in this sorry time, my love looks on thee
  As if by some unseen friend helping him to speak.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When every private star doth shine
And gives thee all the treasure of my heart?
If, heaven knows, I have ranged,
So low as thy sheermostmost pitch should lie,
The just satisfaction of my wrong,
The just cost of many a miser's debt,
Bearing thy star like a beggar's beggar,
To make some perfect nation proud,
Make some rich state famous,
And ten times proud of thy name.
  This were to be new made when thou reviewest
  This, my sweet boy, was thy humble birth.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Providing a kind of remedy, like a beggar fetched
From the streets, that heals the shame of shame.
Now stand ye upon the top of happy hours,
And the world is with you, or you are with us;
That this love was so poured out in this bad fashion
We are told, and the falsehood spreads
That this love was for love of you,
And you for love of us, and for that sake
We are shamed, and others in our joint.
  Then, were some blister on the world's cheek
  Less bitter than the owner's pride,


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That I, whom fortune of such triumph bars,
May ignorance of mine own worth hide?
O, none, unless this great cloud hang
Upon my nature's makeup,
Which ere my nature's action could make me go,
Be based on thy portrayal, on some fictitious name,
That whatsoever shadows on me I see,
To thy love no light do lie.
  And, for thy love, pardon me not
  Till I see thee again, in another life.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot to say
So to the worthy occasion, dear my love,
That thou, contracted to say one thing,
Cannot make any excuse, though mine eye saith it loves
The very subject whereupon it most calms.
O, change thy thought, that I may change my mind.
Shall hate be fairer lodged than gentle love?
Be as thy presence is, gracious and kind,
Or to thyself at least kind-hearted prove.
  Make thee another self for love of me,
  That beauty still may live in thine or thee.

7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
That to thy supereased mayst be sold.
For thou art so possessed with murd'rous hate
That 'gainst myself I anon chide thee,
"Wilt thou not haply say 'tis true
That I, a murd'rous husband, bear love's cross"?
To which I reply that love hath sometimes been false,
And that thou, in point of hate, hath wrought it disgrace.
  'Tis thee, myself, that forsworn love utters,
  For I, a murd'rous husband, bear th' offender's cross.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
But not so with me, whose true love is ambiguous.
He loves what he knows not to love,
And to this fair addition I tend to gravitate
The second angel of my malicious pride.
So I lose both friends, and am undone
By certain threats, which I try to resolve.
But there are many whom I never hate,
Who besides this lose by fortune alone.
  These lost-like-purchased loveliness include thee,
  And I by this produce increase,
  To happier hours and happier nights.
