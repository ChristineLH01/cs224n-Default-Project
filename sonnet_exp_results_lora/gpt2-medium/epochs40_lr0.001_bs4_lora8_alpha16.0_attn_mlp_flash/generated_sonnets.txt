Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Being made more strong by these than by others?
Be thou the fugitive slave of my desires,
Unmoved, remote from thy sweet self,
Unmoved through the gazes of hostile eyes,
Which do mine own pride to set high
And gaze on things hidden far off,
Where thou art not. The view of this makes me sad,
Because thou art so strongly built on me,
That I in thy sweet self dost lie,
Sunk in thy sweet smoke, as thy sweet self outstrives.
  This I do vow, and this shall ever be:

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
The very thing that brought me here?
Roses have thorns, and silver fountains mud;
Clouds and eclipses stain both moon and sun,
And loathsome canker lives in sweetest bud.
All men make faults, and even I in this,
Authorizing thy trespass with compare,
Thy trespass is not all my grief.
Thou canst not ev'ryday brag
That thou didst forsake me for some fault;
My name hath no such pride as thy crime.
  And yet, love, thou mayst do so.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
As you so lov'st me, lest you mislead.
Speak of my lameness and I straight will halt,
For 'gainst myself a disorderly rage;
Against myself I'll cling, even to love,
Which in thy sweet thoughts to love will tend.
I will not hide nor concealed love;
For it is not hid in my nature's course,
But I will prove thee astray,
By honest examination of truth in thy sweet mind.
  For to thee I bring this book,
  To prove thy innocence and me thine.

A maid of Dian's this advantage found,

A third, though it not extinguish, yet it gave hope
To every trifle under his disposal.

And they three lay still, each by his dead bolt;

A third appeared, and stopped his swift progress;

For He took no part in these faster parts.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Gilding their spirits with glossy envy,
One olive branch quite blushing shame,
And eagerness kissed the satchel to sell;
The other dead, apparently moved by his quick movement,
Aloft his lusty stare, on scent of burning,
As each one by his lustily rising did stand,
Bearing the burden of his sin,
Save that, moving with him to heaven,
My sinful lover obsequiously sat
On thy grave and kissed his body in my sight,
  For thee this trespass so preposterously proved.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though not exact equal.
For as the sun hath his eclipse endured,
My saucy thought, like his, is cast away
And carries with it some part of his flame.
Such is my love, and such is my hate,
That I an accessary needs must be
To that sweet Goddess which truth herself calls
To make me more stand well in thy sight.
  To keep myself afloat on dry water
  My bottles, though full of water, are not with me,
  But on my necks they are hid.
