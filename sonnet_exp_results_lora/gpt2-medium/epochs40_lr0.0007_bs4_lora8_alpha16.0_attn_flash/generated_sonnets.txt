Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Had I struck her tongue from her, my heart's shame had shown,
A flaming, fiery heart, crowing its plea.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
That spiritual ordinance watch over thy darkness?
Perhaps, says my troubled soul, thy living dark mistress best,
A lover's spouse, her essence bound in thy firmament,
Seeking darkness, to release thee alive.
Perhaps, soul, dost thou consider this befalling statute,
Upon dead men's tomb, the spirit's place laid bare,
When in soul's space, fear to dwell did lay,
Where conscience guided thy work, troubled offspring.
  Indeed, soul, thy sovereignty surpassed,
  More than any prison, if held would escape.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Seeking forth this if it to deceive thee.
And thou, by fever's starving fire,
Releaseeth forth that treasure which feasts thee;
No salve, warned from thee, still promises to wane,
Indeed, still purves of thy white portion's forge.
For what can remedy this love's plight,
Unless death itself acts?
  For lo, a certain surgeon's countenance prescribes,
  The cure of death's own disease; he grants thee neither potion.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Because love grants no license to my crime.
Thou deceitful cheiver, wield'sst
The thumb that wields the chain of law;
Love wields the sweet-potent wrench of mind,
Steal'st the key from thy grasp, and lets them retry.
If I could slay with ardor's touch,
My revenge's guise would be as sweet as love's breath.
  Yet still do I torment; my heart persists
  With furious, fervent rage, seeking revenge on thee.
