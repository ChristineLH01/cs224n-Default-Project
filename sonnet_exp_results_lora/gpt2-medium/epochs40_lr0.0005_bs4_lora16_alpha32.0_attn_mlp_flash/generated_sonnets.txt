Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
She summoned him from the night, and woke him,
A man of action, not of fortune's doom;
So she, in sleep, did rob him of his fortune,
And by doing so, his beauty fell.
This theft he did, and soon learned to curse,
For beauty's allure works on a double bond,
Two faces, a love-joy in harmony.
  But when his sleep-worn gaze slept again,
  his power dissolved, and the loss he founded.
