Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
The vacant leaves looked fair, but dearly were they washed,
Hiding their youth and their promise;
They were sweet and precious cookies indeed, but they looked false,
For glossy eyes as sweet as snow were in these.
I saw other roses watered, yet none knew well
Whether they were flowers bred or not;
And, for their virtue only is their show,
They are shamed by their smiles and their mournful moan.
  But when I saw another blessed mother do rewrite,
  All flaws were removed excepting of their smiles.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When, battling against thy might,
Thy best shield doth give me all thy might,
And, stout victim, I do defeat
By striking alone where thou dost stand?
Because, firmly in thy power,
My bolt cannot penetrate thine armor,
And, heavy victim, I do lose
Two saucy larks, both of whom gave thee grace.
  But thence I learn, and find the lesson true:
  Thy edge is so thin that steel can penetrate.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
That well that heals when well thou healest.
For love will that physician who cure
The sick and diseased mind
Which this sick physician must physically remove,
To leave for nothing all his care
And so irreversibly scars his creative memory.
  His cruel charger should not so much as graze
  His patient's bleeding neck, since his skill
  Is to cure not to kill.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That I in some strange shape converse
With shadows divined from thee?
No sense can I then to my sufferings find,
Pointing to my present state, or to future prospects,
Pointing to myself as I witness stand;
For such a wise demon doth play the painter
And turns fair and beautiful looking in his skill
To profit mankind and make beautiful shoes.
  For no man so fair doth live,
  That no eye so true his deeds be.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
To love him whom thou dost such docility post,
In deeming the world beyond my scope,
Consequently do I not come near him
That I may be counted among thine only friends,
And keep mine acquaintance distant from him.
O, if my love, though it beheaded what it was,
To bear the strong hurt of being cast away,
The poor dissolute left behind, should in my will accomplish
The petty spite of mine own desert,
  Receiving what was paid thee by me earlier.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And to render false what I thought true,
To leave for nothing all thy worth,
The precious merit of all thy gifts,
To leave for nothing all thy parts,
And to leave for nothing all thy parts of me,
That this my greatest lie, so esteemed
By others for my weakness,
On thy part would be Made stronger by my being frank,
And, all in war with myself alone,
Doing thy part, bring thee glory, power, and love;
  Or, to leave him that honors me most,
  Do this, love honors me least.

A maid of Dian's this advantage found,

The sad augurs forgot his grace,

9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
In submission to his delicious dregs,
At the expense of his sweet self-love did plead;
But, for his virtue, pride, and robbery
He robs himself of both. For, having traffic with his stars,
And having no leisure taken from his time,
He robs himself of all, and commits it all to waste.
  This thought is as a death, which cannot choose
  But weep to have that which it fears to lose.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
While thou dost lie in youth's rear,
And nothing my sweet girl dost praise.
For if I have read thy sweet face
When my love looked upon her worthy side,
Thy face hath not the power to make love groan,
Nor hath thy sweet heart the strength to complain.
O, therefore, love, be of thyself so wary
As I not for my love but for thee will,
Bearing thy heart, which I will keep so chary
As tender nurse her babe from faring ill.
  Presume not upon thy heart when mine is slain.



11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And in him I see a father;
My dearest love is but the son
Of one other, and in him I see such form
As his sweet form doth in my breast defy.
Such is my love's fair appearance,
That to his sweet self it doth dedicate
The earth and waters which he soaks up.
I love to him and look for his figures
And find them in thee, but, alas, they are vain;
  For, of many, he looks so fair.
