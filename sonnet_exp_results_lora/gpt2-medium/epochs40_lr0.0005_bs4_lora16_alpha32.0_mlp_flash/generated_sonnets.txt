Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Those wrinkles which traced my brow
To Hell did something more wicked disclose:
Swept black into Hell's embrace, as punishment stained
The loveliest of vases, a dreadful care.
These locks, which Beauty herself gave
To dread the worst of touch,
Did lead her in this vile direction,
Another unto her unhidden:
O, to suffer this, and yet live!
  Yet, still, I must suffer, and have no love
  To put blighted shadows where there was none.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Came singing to the fire, their vows fulfilled.
To this enchantment stood sacred Love's pavilion,
Its solemn interior adorned by artful sighs,
Yet, Love's true love, unyielding, did not join,
Nor did the solemn locks reveal their might.
A torch stained with years, Love's glow consumed,
A furnace burned, where the ember glowed,
Creating a noble blaze, albeit not so bright.
  The fire drew heat, lifting the sleeping down,
  But brought no cure to Love's true plight; it had no breath.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Uneducated in rudeness, rude in speech,
Unsavory in handling simple questions well.
Yet, I perceive her falsehoods, yet I believe,
To maintain the notion of my affection;
To keep alive the notion of my worth,
Defying the judgment of common opinion,
And doing what I think necessary,
At the peril of my self-defense.
  In this second act, truth on both sides hides,
  And I am left to my opinions alone.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And his love is ever so warm;
The false one oft seems to me unkind,
Though in his looks his true love doth prevail.
I grant I cannot love him less blessed
Than he who with public kindness greets,
And by the graces accord doth falsely show.
His looks are better but in my hope;
And 't worth more mine than his own desert.
  I may not evermore acknowledge him true,
  As I will him for my love and for my love his good report.
