Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
For what care I who calls me well or ill,
So you o'ergreen my bad, my good allow?
You are my all the world, and I must strive
To know my shames and praises from your tongue;
None else to me, nor I to none alive,
That my steeled sense or changes right or wrong.
  For all that your sound and that poor air
  Touch my bad, my good allow.


1
Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Whereto thy sweet self blush?
Profitless roots thou best of inheritance call,
Self-love quite contrary thou usest;
For how do thy sweet graces compare
Against thy harsh external spite?
O, blame me not if thou wilt leave me alone,
Haply I call upon thy help,
Even as on thy soft cheek my bones shall lie.
  Taint me with thy shame, and on my ruins shall lie,
  Complete ruin thou hast no right nor I to set.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Sinking down the treasure of their youth, which is still their source.
When that love whereof now it consumeth lies,
Like the swamp where Paradise doth grow,
The treasure of the widow's womb lies,
Hung with the trophies of my lovers gone,
Who all their parts of me to thee did give;
That poor consolation which enfeebles their lives
When I am away, working all day,
Suff'ring their earning age to buy more.
  To give away myself thou hast the strength of laws,
  Since why to love I can allege no cause.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
As to indicate my wailing lacked,
The cause of wailing being thy sweet self.
O, blame me not if thou wilt; I do not love
That burden so heavy a jollity offers.
Be as thy presence is, gracious and kind,

  Make thee another self for love of me,

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Though I guilty embrace it myself.
Name your love, and in it your history
Is writ more bright than in the cheek of your true friend.
So should that simple form of love which the reader pen
Of his divining soul against his will translate
Of sour weights to weights of praise,
Making his style admired everywhere.
O, him as for a map doth Nature store,
And not for his beauty show,
But for his virtue what she hath done well;
  For truly so bright is love that in his shade
  The learner doth no gross appearance deceive.


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
Two loves in one, how wilt thou bear that love ?
O, in what fault hath thy filching gone?
Both my loves are suspected of fraud,
And in their turning are seen to decay;
Thou art so possessed with murd'rous hate
That 'gainst myself and myself alone stand.
Thou art the lark of unhappy song,
Who stops the grate of widowed love,
And stops the grate of sound,
By which he sore hurt people's souls.
  For this sin there is no remedy,
  It is so grounded inward in me.

9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Toil, or lack of body, to travel;
The meadows, on the banks of the river,
Which soft the earth and rich earth replete,
Shall above the echoing noise make
The bier with thy heavy black jacket hang.
Another time mine eye was spy,
One solemn night my love looked on thee,
Another, nor love's picture in mind,
But wild conjecture of thy fear and pleasure;
  For thou spy'st but what thou see'st,
  The very terror of which thou art awed.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
As idle woman chained to some rondure.
For though she for women's sport have her,
The men's war is her duty,
And, proud of many, are champions of their state.
So I for love speak as if I were possessed by her,
Looking on truths as if they were my love,
And like her mockingly says so,
When I err, she fixes my tone;
  So I have thought myself as possessed,
  Being wooed of time, to love things gone.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
But love for love's face hides his worth;
So I am not admitted to that positive esteem.
O, what a happy title do I find,
Happy to have thy love, happy to die!
But what's so blessed-fair that fears no blot?
For if it be false, and upon me proved,
The hardest knife ill used destroys my love's beauty.
  Blessed are you whose worthiness gives scope,
  Being had, to triumph, being lacked, to hope.
