Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Utt'ring bare matter whereupon she might pour
The most salve that salve to wound her foe.
Yet fear not, love; yet in me
Might there still be some faint murmur that o'ersways
From thy soft lips;
Just look what thy poor inward-praised hand
Makes groan, when thou art indeed saluting me.
  To hear y'er own mournful murmur
  Sings hoarsely by the lips of others,
  Which many thanks to thee do bring forth.

Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When thy stars twire not, thy magnificent magnificence
Lest effaced towers should monumentize thee?

For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That my true essence might from thence be verified?
O, let them but copy my strokes,
Not admiring of my finer skills,
Since all my best is in their thinking eyes.
Book both my willfulness and errors down,
And on just proof surmise accumulate;
Bring me within the level of their frown,
But shoot not at me in their wakened minds,
  Since my writ does but show what they saw.
  They are mad that my rud'st absence astonishes
  And more that my rud'st absence affords.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
For trifles of purest love preserved.
Thou art as fair in knowledge as in hue,
Finding thy worth a limit past my praise,
And therefore art enforced to seek anew
Some fresher stamp of the time-bettering days.
In so I throw away all care
Of others' voices that my adder's sense
To critic and to flatterer stopped are.
Mark how with thy neglect I do dispense:
  Thy faults are not thyself so cruel,
  Since thou thyself dost so disgrace me.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
But, as the times provoke, he slept and died.
A tyrant built upon disorder,
Who builds up and ruins virtue's foundation,
Sinks beauty and lowers morals to disgrace,
And insults truth and finds fault in all his methods.
A false king pulled from honor's shade,
And languished in disgrace for shame and found it hard
To shout hymns at a built-up wreck.
  A true Christian once elected,
  But then began to follies and fell a-doting.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
In hope of more that sweet close,
Gilding pale streams with their glowing head,
And confounds dead silent song with mournful hymns.
Yet fear not, love. These same birds love to greet
That look on them with all their might,
Even so that their mournful hymns do not seem
To mourn for those that have lived.
  For fear of which, hear this, thou age unbred:
  Ere you were born was love with you broken.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
But as he walks he walks in shadow,
And my love's phantom doth mock him there.
For love is a spirit that bears impression,
And if that man's spirit with his dress doth show,
The counterfeit does look fair.
So my love's phantom doth mock me here,
That, like two lambs standing one stall,
He looks pale, and my tongue doth rehearse
That sweet love's phrase, "As ever thou shalt sing, so thou must cease."
  Yet so they mock him, and I think no ill
  But that they mock thee so
