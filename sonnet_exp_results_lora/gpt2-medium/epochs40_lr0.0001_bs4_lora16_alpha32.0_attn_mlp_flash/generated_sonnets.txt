Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Arsons thy sacred space with firing rage?
Throw Fire away with your blazing eyes,
Dissipate thy moisture in the cleansing waters;
Whoso turns from thee, no more shall they know,
To receive this exile, only they have spent.
Who will follow thee, if no trace of thee remains,
When all the world is desolate and empty,
And all that's valuable is but a grain of dust?
  Resist the urge to laze; sift salt among the ashes,
  And let your mourning cease at last.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
To do what is not my part, thy part, myself?
My reason, be it conscience, tells me
Thy own strength hath not pleased me;
On thy part, 'tis ignorant, concealed, untrue.
Both my will and my heart's inclination
Confess their part, and their rebuke admit;
Yet, on thy part, even love doubts
That thy great strength can quell its rage.
O, change thy mind, worthless heart; presently
I will be more of one, in love confined.
  Give me the strength, love; be the change I want.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Spare me the reproach of partiality showed;
Speak fair of me, yet love has sworn
To one who's suspected of no crime,
A silent heart, a gentle touch committed.
So, pardon my disobedience; I only ask
For the love that teaches me to trust anew.
For if I must yield to my heart's request,
  No heart's desire surpasses my reason.
  In one of yours, from whom you fled,
  I learned to love another, thus standing still.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
How often have I seen thy sweet countenance plead
Against many more favorable bowers,
Each knife showing more sure razor as he did,
Where were those buttons thy foot planted,—
Drawn out for trifles not worth worth,
And for thy cherry buds did you graze with.
Till I fixed thee immured and ignorant,
Mine eye might not to frown or her suck no more,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steepe.
Poisoned, in dreams his sweet dreamless glance did show,
His skin quenched by love-tide black and yellow.
So we awake, Poisoned at first by love, tasted love's desire,
Yet tasted anew love's forbidden sweet delight!
Oh, sweet poison, medicine that saved my life,
Retreating from lover's kiss, and coming to cure.
  For I, trapped in my lover's eyes,
  Despised as I, for love of my health.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Shall lion the sacred ground of their flower,
Each by his very being partly stricken deaf,
Although half-blind himself be His chief delight.
This Prometheus, risen early in the morning,
Births heir to all human kind,
Gave birth to such fair form as birds say,
Yet waste a flower that for all their new,
Too plain for garden shame,
Nor sown by eager hands who waste good.
  For poverty in his being many treasures hold,
  And wisdom so wherever he planted them.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though truth be but as a cloud, veiled from sun,
Like dreams, hidden from mortal sight.
But why does she not speak of her youth?
And why do I not see the sun as he rises,
To honor his youth, his beauty,
And celebrate his deeds, true and splendid?
No love I seek, 'tis hidden in her,
Unadorned, veiled, from my dull eyes.
  Hence, I hold her false, though she knows it's false,
  For I see her age, not age's beauty.
