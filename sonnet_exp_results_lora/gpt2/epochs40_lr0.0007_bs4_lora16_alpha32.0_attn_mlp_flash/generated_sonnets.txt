Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While I, thy creditor, steal thy strength from thy grace?
Why dost thou devise this injustice,
To poison my soul with injury and disgrace,
And do not redeem thy blight with fairest light?
For having wed thee with shadow's reign,
Redeem not a semblance of the day,
And make thy wrath first before me;
Who hath such power in me should I try to redeem,
To slay thee more than thou art known from me?
  To stand against me, she bestows such strength,
  She shall prevail, and I shall suffer.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
As feed on the pangs of fleeting pleasure.
Oh, if it be ill, why do you choke
Your heart, which grows wild and gives way?
It is that your heart that makes your heart grow
Intimacy, and that you feed on,
A hunger born of longing, and slaughter
The flesh, which needs no nourishment, for
It is the starving heart who feeds on
The sickness, and dies with longing for thee.
  If hungry, then you were of more ill,
  Eating with a meat that cost me much more.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
When I am from the good works at last?
If not so fair is my love's view,
And in my eye never's just eye dive,
Why should my words linger where they speak?
If body hath shape, what number is it,
How is my love rendered wise, if made from truth?
If not so fair, then love's new design sleeps,
More fair than before, more genuine.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
My bestow upon thee my due?
Who wills that thou love me therefore so pure
That I corrupt not to please thee?
Thy grace with such partial esteem
As me most of thy neighbor is based
To my content with others' content apart.
Why do I not say more about thee
When I am content to obey thee,
Of others who I do thine own well?
  O, none I mean to show thy sagacity
  But by thy magnanimous letting me show myself.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And show my worth to the universe unseen,
Transforming me to the dictates of Fortune,
Thou villain's touch, most cruel, ever true.
See, if I defy thy dictations,
My life shall feel free from mistake;
In thee, these prophecies I find pass,
And by a portion of my brain doth rest,
A truth based upon truth so bold.
  Thus shall love be my faithful soul, so bold as mine,
  To bear false and stupid deceits wherever.

Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest I should belittle your worth, even as I offend,

No need for praise stands between me and you,
For I am as you, and he is unbound,

My actions are guided by your own guiding star,
An honest guide through the endless journey
Of love, through your eyes, through fabric's maze.
  Choose what is right, and how you order,
  This choice I deem necessary, your judgment ours.

 I confess she is fair, and I think she is
