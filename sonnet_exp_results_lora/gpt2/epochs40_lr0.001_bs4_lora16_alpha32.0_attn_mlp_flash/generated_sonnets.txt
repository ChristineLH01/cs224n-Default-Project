Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Her heart, touched by remorse, did then cry:
"I love her because she roamed with hatred."
To me she turned away,
And found her heart no solace, her heart laid in despair,
And taught her tongue to sing a sweeter bar;
She added:
"I love her because she seduced me."
And sent her distress:
"I love her because she seduced."
And saved my soul with words:
"I love her because she seduced."
  "I do love her because she seduced."

2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
In ceaseless motion, the weight of ill,
Gilding the weight of ill, as if it knows
Her own worth, at last, lost in madness.
So I, enthralled, make her ill informed
By indulgent visits out of self-love alone,
She doth teach false skill,
And altogether marath'ring the truth,
Even to those who witness her wrong.
  She tells me she loves thee so deep,
  That I, in my madness, lose my own good,
  And lose to her love.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
How is my sight, which in its seeming sight is sought?
Neither sense nor wisdom taught to the blind,
So aimlessly pure is my view.
Neither skill nor beauty taught to see,
Nor seeing, nor observing, neither betray either.
Nor can true sight tell the vision given,
So aimless is my view, though it roam usually.
Nor can fair no spirit with his keen sight offend,
And make the view on window blind where it is seen.
  Yet sweet be thy image, which to thee I send,
  My love's power they read and their will.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest sorrow encourage my sins to reveal.
For who is unaware that pain so dear
Is not unworthy of love's own full weight?
Thus, sly philorinists, falsely convinced,
Have enchanted faithless love's sacred sight,
And fashioned false vows, as if blind,
In their own naive faith, unsprayed.
No love for others dwells within their hearts,
They live for their own shame, and thus they condemn.
  For, as they receive from sorrow, so they feel
  Guilty to suffer, and thus they condemn.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
Thy active eye hath told thy partner frown,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep the ocean's race,
Increasing its power to produce evergreen
In the starry of memory's splendor.
The virgin's eyes, once bright and gay,
Now hollow, while his power takes flight,
For romantic ink has traced its trail
To the holy places where people find their power.
For if your heart's love were stolen, or slain,
The sacred text of your love was quelled,
A vain hope for lovers to find their share.
  But I, in turn, sought to preserve you,
  In hopes that you would live and die.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
His skin clean, as thine own, by nature's wagging hand.
The rites of beauty's grace, once chaste now
Others view, too sacred, now turn to
The serving drunk, their own worth corrupted,
With truth's nymphs, who sent heart and follow,
One grief still, of another, with grief still stamped.
  Still, I bear this torment, for love's own woe,
  When my beloved reminds me of your own gluttony.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And like her herself, she knows well her days are past their prime.
So I grant she got a fleeting dream,
A dream conjured through my soul's vast canvas,
To imagine her past love and her days at bay,
The same thing I did to fold my tattered love,
And like her lover, I retell my tale,
At least her fictional name, plain as gospel.
  And I retell the tale, though my liasculihed days,
  To cherish her for I deem natural.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And love's semblance is his essence, pure and new;
So all my faults I undertake,
Gazing on darkness and longing's wicked play.
They glimpse the strength in my love's sweet view,
And then cast aside their bitter tears,
To celebrate thy true worth and joy.
They weep and then proclaim my sin,
Then I apologize, they persevere,
For I had lied, before true love uttered.
  Yet now their cries are marred, and my shame is grown,
  And now their rage is sated by sweet falsehood.
