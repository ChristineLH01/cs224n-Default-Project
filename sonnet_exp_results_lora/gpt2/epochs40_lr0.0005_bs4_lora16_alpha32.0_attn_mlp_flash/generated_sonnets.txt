Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And taught another to write the verse
Of old, with power, as mine own,
Bearing a poet's touch, "I'll wait for you, dear."
Mine lips, fearful of that sweet woman's book,
Were bought up by his own weight,
Which in from their countenance retained;
And thus forth they wrote of love,
Of poets' high-born joy.
  But me, fearful of losing you in longing,
  Spurned by your, scorned, all the rest, for my sake.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
My resolve mounting against thy challenge?
To keep my gaze open thou wither and comes back,
Yet in thy shadow, thy shadow retains me firm,
Embracing thy visage, a vivid display.
What secret secret will thy shadow keep,
When its outer shell doth ensnare thy visage?
No gentle deed can this sin of mine behold,
Can it serve, against thee I call thee,
To seek a purer self, in spirit and truth.

2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Yet slipping ill to an unbroken bliss.
Oh, but imagination, which instructs you to prepare
The sick with apparitions of mirth,
The sickness hath eased him from its sleep,
And cured him from its laced slumber.
Let him stay, dear heart, as thou restiest days,
A bankier of consolation than the sick's own cost;
In him, the invasions of life shall be said,
And recovery is promised, in heaven's book.
  In him, I find that fate hath held fast such bread,
  As death pays the mark of vengeance.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which reigns in fixed minute letters but hold?
What authority do mine eyes find, that they view in you?
If, however, their vision of thee hath adhered
To clouds and waves, or frolics and wrinkles fade;
Their view often beholds thy form in their sight,
Their eyes interposed, as the star did;
But my view, darting from sight,
Pushes back, the truth with ever-end shift.
  Fair is thy outward beauty, the best that in me,
  For his brightness thou mayst appear, without.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
My bodily predilection to thee draw?
Who wilt thou then that I in self make known?
For thee I see many false frowns, cruel eyes
Who do chastise thee for thy wicked deed;
Who is not so kind a husband God forbid
To take him form from thy isse?
Indeed, if thou art offending self, do not act on me
Where I ought sweetly wish upon thy head.
  Still, do thy harm; I love thee best
  And see thy goodness enough when kindness is shown.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
That thou dost good honour gave to thee,
And lead me astray with false command?
O, whence forbear, have I not a chance
To question my verity and perceive thy wont?
No man hath power to change what belongs
To thee. Thou'll dost cleanse my heart, and I may change
Thy selves thy best, you must be reviled.
  Yet, most am I prone to besiege
  That root where thy truest virtue lays.


6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my errors.
For that unnatural glow which makes my heart angry,
Is hid from the public's gaze,
Its pure, sincere glow so hard to see;
My soul is entangled, through my actions disguised.
No soul in whom this power dwell,
Can perceive the voluntary decree of right and good,
When ordered by your commanding hand.
  For, like a neglected spouse, my heart does embrace
  The duties of a loved heart laid bare.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
My eyes scattered, bereft, with thee.
Beholding thy gaze, this love hath delivered
And is hid in the mind of watch,
Thy reason from ever letting start.
Look why my heart abhors the declining night,
Before sunset, when fitter called,
When blest foliage hath already fallen on trees,
And thou amarest to flee hence.
  But, loathsome thou know'st thou thyself when I find,
  Because my love hath disguised itself as a dove,
  And like the thief who steals my heart's treasure.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And love's sweet semblance wise.
Two hearts in one, yet have moved,
One respect and other hate strew,
A shadow of deceit shrouded in tangled dance.
Yet love at times seemed innocent,
Save in the guise of thy cheek.
My seer conceive would have taught fair me how,
To stand tall, though shadowed hide.
Believe not, though my fiery heart is moved,
The sweetness of love's own power do equip.
  For if I lived beside thee,
  My love would be lost, was I not damned.
