Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Leading drain, as thou march'st from the source of all?
Why dost thou chose this temporal dwelling,
Which ever hath mentored his labour above?
For Nature's ransom hath not yet paid thee yet,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Leaving behind but the sick and bleeding.
In that case I am the sick and starving main,

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
For in our youth, we find love's sweet vest
That every villian likes to be real.
So that we love each other, though our deeds defy,
In love's own sense, I perceive her wrongs deeply.
But why does she not confess her wrongs?
And why do I not confess my age?
For love thrives on feigned trust and belief,
And we so should shun the wiles we deem too nasty.
  So we deceive one another in our flaws,
  And with sweet lies, we comfort our disgrace.
