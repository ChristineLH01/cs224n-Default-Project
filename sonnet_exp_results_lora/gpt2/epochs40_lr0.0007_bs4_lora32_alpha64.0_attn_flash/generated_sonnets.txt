Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
And replace thy current power with thy bark?
How fashion the naked eye to indulge
In misty embellishment of thy goodness,
While thy precious senses pass by by no trace?
Why suffer the wounds of thieves to betray
The solidity of thy goddess' face?

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And that she delights in false vows.
But in the end, all lies my persuasion,
By means of public disdain and scorn hidden.
My love tells me, then, not true;
She calms her anger, smiles at my tender scorn;
We laugh at tales of false kisses,
And at our simple banter, growling,
Few of our strange sentiments perceive
A fairer blush than those around us.
  Thus, my friends, for their faults, I confess,
  For they may but admire our virtue, beyond blue and red.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Among those stirring their fires in the void,
The distortion that fuels me.
The hypocritical author conceals my love in faux might,
Unoaked from their unadorned leg.
Thus, love's villainously malign moves,
His truth distorted, while joy springs into weakness.
Why should I swear my innocence, when I know well,
My own lies and won't keep?
  My reason I shall endure, all the days long and may.
