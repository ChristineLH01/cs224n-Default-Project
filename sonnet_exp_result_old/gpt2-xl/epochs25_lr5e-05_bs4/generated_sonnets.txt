Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Yet, Love, you knew the sentence'd be
To leave me cold and dry, bereft of breath.
But why should I reproach your harshness,
When I am guilty of cold-heartedness?
Myself guilty, I corrupt anew
The sacred bond between us,
Yet, to your reproach, I add
A few lines that say "Not so!"
  For your sake, I pen down
  A love-language, gentle and kind.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Returning no more to the source of infection.
Thou false diabolic in thy goodness does cast
A finer glow on thy bareness than on thyShow,
Which is more admired than the eye of brass,
Gilding the object whereupon it gazeth.
O, what a mansion have those vices got
Which for their habitation chose out thee,
Where beauty's veil doth cover every blot,
And all things turns to fair that eyes can see!
  Take heed, dear heart, of this large privilege;
  The hardest knife ill used doth lose his edge

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you lead me astray, I forsake
My nobler self to my body's treachery.

In love; the flesh, no longer a bore,
Rises at your name, its power to explore,
A triumphant trophy, yours to adore.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, always near,
  To rise and fall, for your love, my dear.

And for this theft my body does plead.

So then my body believes thee,
And heart in love's subordinate place holds his part,

Thy love is better than blindness itself.
  But why my love says so I cannot say,
  For I see clear to thy breaches and deeds.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though not quite so old as she seems to me.
Still, I think the day of her youth is past,
And her beauties likely more than false;

And in my love's dearest locks, find morrows.
But why should I not love her as I do thee?
Though she be false, I think my love true,
And so should thou, to love things nothing worth.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And in his presence my heart is drowned.
Yet my soul finds sweet pains in this despair,
And in his name, though dead, lives on in light.
For if it be stained with transgressions fresh,
The life it bears, though tainted, lives on in light.
My tragic love, thus entwined, stays my wayward heart,
Creating turmoil where none was intended.
  If it be reduced, 'tis no loss,
  For love's sweet memory never dies.
