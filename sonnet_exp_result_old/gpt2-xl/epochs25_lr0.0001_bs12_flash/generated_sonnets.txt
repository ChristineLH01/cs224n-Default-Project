Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Each day bring forth less and less increase?
Why bestow such riches on this fading edifice,
A monument destined to turn to dust?
Shall the inheritors of this vice,
Consume thy legacy, thy very essence?
Then, soul, feed on the anguish of thy servant,
Let them fade, while you flourish and transcend.
Exchange the transient hours for eternal bliss,
Find solace within, while lacking without.
  Thus shall you feast on Death, who devours all souls,
  And when Death is vanquished, no more shall we know death's toll.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my dearest love, a woman flawed far.
They struggle for my heart, as two tides do wash,
In love's odd grace, where two pursuers are.
My heart, once bound, now unbent,
For in its embrace, my soul finds its leave,
A partaker in love's sweet pleasure.
Love's cunning stratagem, with tears,
Works on my heart, like a flawed tide.
  My love, now partaking, partakes in love's sweet release,
  And vice versa, my heart favours the flawed.
