Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Whose motion seemed to me as fast
As strangling as those horrible reds
That on her head lay hidden,
In the place where those wasted died.
Ah, those same lips that Love's own touch had put
In touch with his ownuration,
Which Foods as poisoned lead are wont to do,
Weakened by that love which they view
As venom to the vices they are;
  And, Health, thou thyself dost kill

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Plotting against thy own existence?
Why invest so much in this uncertain path,
A path that lead'st to ruin and wrong?
To what end do you give your all,
For worms to come and claim their bequest as well?
Instead, nourish yourself on the servant's despair,
Let them wither while you amass treasures fair.
Exchange fleeting hours for eternal grace,
Find solace within, while wanting without.
  Embrace the feast of Death, who consumes all men,
  And when Death perishes, no more shall we die then.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Kindling a new passion in a healthier state,
Embracing the second burden of my heart's contentment,
More blessed than the first, more rare and more strong.
Now fortified with thy help, my love sesss
Her beauty, her devotion, and leads me near and dear,
Basking in love's soothing cream,
Rather than succumb to the ravages of decay.
O, what a joy it is to share this love affliction,
To love and live, and in that love, immerse.
  But what's so sacred that it fears no harm?

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I betray
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may strive

Rises at your name, its purpose to derive
From your affection, its reason to thrive.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, ever so near,
  To stand by you, to fall by your side, my dear.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though short my lifespan shows;
Yet, like two mornings happied,
He begins to pall in the East.
Two souls entwined, like ivy towers,
Resembling the summer's glow and the autumn's shade,
Yet one hour longer would show me true,
Than suffer through endless life's string.
O, that our farewell could be swift,
Like two swift messengers returned from thee,
Only to take our leave in the night.
  Yet, love, thy pleasure is only ever near,
  And that thou hast him near represents life's brief exchange.
