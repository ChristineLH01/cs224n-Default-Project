Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
So my love, till then very gentle,
Savage from love's savage rack.
But when that chill bough that hangeth dumb
Doth whisper "I love you," then jade lips
Make that lovely cry, "I love you."
For if that bell rang evermore
In solemn time of love's great day,
I knew then that love was killed;
For always high on the top of sweet Jane's head
Doth lean the dead askins' jollity,
That croaking voice saying "I love you."
  So I took courage, and so should you

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Defying the calamity that overtakes?
Why fight against the sap that doth root thee,
Expelling the sweetest boon from thy bosom?
Oh, know, sweet despair, that love is a cage,
An inviting cage, cruel and shallow,
That binds us down, robs us of our might,
And leaves us to our own crumbling hearts.
  Hers, by thy unrighteous judgment executed,
  Thine own heart ensnared her with certainty.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Exchanging holiness for uglyness, distilling scorn,
Sucking life's sweetness from the poisoned vitals.
My heart is full, then empty, starved, replete,
Consuming thee, thy beauty, and all's art,
Finding sweet pleasure in that which the heart doth crave;
But, alack, that blood is thine, and thou art all's debt,
So that now I thee, and then nothing else.
  For why should I live, being full of thee,
  When I have not thy love, nor thee to repay?

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
My own rank and deserts to thee?
Who hath denied thee that thou shouldst supply?
On whom dost thou frown that I do pursue?
Indeed, if thou dost scowl on me, I try
To supply thy defect with myself;
Thus I restore equality betwixt us,
And then thou see'st me forced to be bastard
And live with offenders all my life.
O, what a happy title do I find!
Thy fond heart thinks its own love loved me;
Thy bad brother lends me a hand, proud
To be preferred before thee.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
Yet twice is thy guilt, twice my pardon.
For who but I understoodth thy double grace
And took both of us for our legends?
Even for this my kingship and my love bound
Were twofold, for thou art my true name,
And I must not call thee by thy husbandry
But by thy christian name, and by thy grace forever.
  But since thy christian name is the deeper dye,
  I must use the poorer of two bad wigs.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
Auchenervere, the golden goddess of love,
Did leap from her balcony and smoke her foot,
To cool the flame that lavish was in Love's face.
But when the flames had climbed higher than life,
They burned his nasal passages out, so he grew lame.
And so the maid, in love's grief, smeared Love's brand with her breath,
And the injury mended no wound,
For Love's furnace burned with a virgin's grace.
  This prevented a war between lovers' eyes,
  Which might have made a sweet dental heir.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
If I could but attain to wisdom,
And grasp the reason for her love's flame.
But she knows my days are numbered,
And, wanting to prolong my days,
She meshes her lies with those of youth,
To let me live a few moments longer.
But why does she not confess her wrong?
And why do I not admit my age?
For love thrives on feigned trust and belief,
And the aged in love dislike revealed years.
  So we deceive one another in our flaws,
  And with sweet lies we comfort our disgrace.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Believed to be dead and left in ether;
My worse angel is a devil child born
From hell's own spawn, unrighteous pity.
The former loves me because she thinks me dead,
The latter loves me because she thinks me living;
Both loved me once, now they turn cold and dead.
This enmity does love strike me quite dead,
Another love I must bear, because I am.
  For if she dies, I am chilled still,
  And if she lives, then I am dead.
