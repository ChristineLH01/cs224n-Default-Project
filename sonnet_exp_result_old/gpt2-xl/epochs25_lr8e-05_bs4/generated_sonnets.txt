Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And there she that breathed that word anticipate
My worst of woe, even to the decline
Of my friend for whom she still harbored eye.
But why should I reproach her for my grief?
She was but blunt thou that she denuded,
And spared no pains to alleviate my woe;
That she alone didStrangle and scold,
Was enough to make me worse than still she lived.
  Yet, not she, but I, that lost thee,
  Lose her, and yet we must not be enemies.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
In thy own glass displayed thy darkest deeds?
Why invest so much in this temporal dwelling,
A structure bound to crumble, fade, and fall?
To what end do you give your all,
For worms to come and claim their bequest as well?
Instead, nourish yourself on the servant's despair,
Let them wither while thou amass treasures fair.
Exchange fleeting hours for eternal grace,
Find solace within, while wanting without.
  Embrace the feast of Death, who consumes all men,
  And when Death perishes, no more shall we die then.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which on myself this sly war in my heart sown?
If it be the lust of eyes, be most true,
Why do mine own eyes not suspect a thing gone,
Nor 'gainst myself do they chide,
When in their mournful sight I see
My heart in truth suffering?
O, 'tis love that makes mine eyes strange,
Mine own heart beside myself insane.
  For love hath wandered from mine eye,
  And now to myself it reigned.

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you lead me astray, I forsake
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul whispers to my flesh that it may soar
In love; the flesh, no longer a bore,
Rises at your name, its power to explore,
A triumphant trophy, yours to adore.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, always near,
  To rise and fall, for your love, my dear.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And for the love said to be new made,
Thy marital bed thou hast his marital garment.
Then can I not blame thee for my love,
Although thou art forsworn, and I am forced to be,
Two for two, love's spite and all.
In matters of vast capacity, we prove
Among many, one is insignificant, dear.
  In love's register, thou hast my love twice over,
  Since somber graves can sometime say more than fresh.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Fair in hue, and true in agreeing.
He lends me smile, and I give him back again,
In twice the charm that once was mine.
But why does he not pursue that excellence
Which he hath in himself?
Or why do I not challenge him to a race,
To prove me more fair than he is known?
For nothing so fair as I can claim,
Unless you would entrust me with your treasure.
  So, if he assents, I twist to prove
  The faithfulness and virtue of your love.
