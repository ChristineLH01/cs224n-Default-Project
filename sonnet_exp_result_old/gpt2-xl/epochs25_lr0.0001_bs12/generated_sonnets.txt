Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
So far for my love, nothing more could I do
Than sufferance, bearing the wanton,
The heavy bane of my heart, like a nurse
Who pinches close the instant he sees
That his appetite is full, teeth chattering,
At the sight of blood flowing from his own lips.
But when that desire was slain by a look,
Like a frustrated hunter, I had to shoot
At that which had so long held my part;
And thus was I glad, though at first my heart
Was full with desire, for then I had eat
The blood that had purpose

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Adorning thy exterior with such finesse,
While thy inner core doth suffer eclipse and defect?
Why invest so much in this temporal dwelling,
A structure bound to crumble, fade, and fall?
To what end do you give your all,
For worms to come and claim their bequest as well?
Instead, nourish thy core, make it grow,
A bulwark stronger than my tomb,
Roaming free, defeating darkness,
Applying famine to the lands where no flowers sprung.
  Ere you are gone, these lines shall live,
  And thine immortal

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Thyself thy foe, to thy sweet self too cruel.
O, benefit of ill! Now I find true
That better is by evil still made better;
And ruined love, when it is built anew,
Grows fairer than at first, more strong, far greater.
So I return rebuked to my content,
And gain by ills thrice more than I have spent;
  And this increase I pay
  For losing myself as a fool.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest I should Hauzeth thee and make my state notorious.
Whoso restrains me, let my name be façade,
No more shall I prey upon thy heart.
Do not thou straitly set me up to be thy conquest,
I'll haply overthrow thy design and live in retreat.
Grant not a debased grief, posthumous,
To haunt out thy living days like a terminal night.
  Since life is short, then let us each other die
  In love, leaving no offspring but the love we give.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unaware of life's deceitful arts.
Thus, she believes me gullible, unwise,
Though she knows full well my days are fleeting,
I play along, her falsehoods I endure,
And in this act, truth on both sides hides.
But why does she not admit her wrong?
And why do I not confess my age?
For love's most endearing trait is pretense,
And age in love dislikes the telling of years.
  So, in our faults, we find comfort in lies,
  And in our deceptions, we find sweet solace.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though not made from heaven's old gems,
And therefore does not flatter with glee,
Like inappropriate objects of beauty's lure.
My bad one is a creature of high estate,
Though not made from earth's rich soil,
Yet hath some substance of solace added,
Which sweets and touches fortify his cheek.
  So, both love and its despite are broad,
  And like two sharp swords they stab each other in the back.
