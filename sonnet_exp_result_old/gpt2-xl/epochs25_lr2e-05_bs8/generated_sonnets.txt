Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And in my heart there was hatred chilled
As if from a ruined vase, untouched.
"Were it not penalty enough," I said,
"To owe her love, learn to dislike
The very person who owes it?"
And then I remembered the bitter cost
Of love's short patience, born of dislike;
And, losing patience, I repented
And hated him anew, as if he'd done it.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While abundance everywhere abounds?
Among the rich abundance is not found,
Nor blessed hours to bask in pure delight;
Nor blessed bodies to bask in perfect health,
Nor names to graced live that then should die.
But thou, possessed by these same strange forces,
Burns with a fierce passion for growth,
And in thy own waste doth your soul find rest,
A famine born before true hunger came.
  Then be praised thy deeds be pure,
  When most men's deeds are but shadows shown.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Invading the calm of slumber with its alarms.
I've found a cure, yet love remains contrite,
For fever's sweet secretion is but cooling vinegar,
And Love, when unchecked, is as foul as canker.
In like manner, my love diseased is,
Hungrier than the ravens that peck at the dawn,
Gazing in vain on the celestial sphere;
Yet, in the face of this eternal truth,
My heart pledges him a steadfast nurse, pure and bright.
  To such a wight I send this missive,
  To cure his love

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which, though it sees, still loves to repeat the same?
If it err, not I, though it misconceives,
But love's cunning work, using my mind
To make of errors what Truth would overthrow.
O, sure, if it err, 'tis my love's doing,
And, loved more, more will be its consent;
So, frantic love, more will be its rage,
To trust in errors, to correct where Truth turns.
  If it err, 'tis my belief, and, believing,
  Love leads me in its errors to set it right.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest sorrow sternly enter your heart and knit
A greater guilt than hate has ever laid.
For, as you deceive me, I pardon your unfairness;
Though you've stained my love, yet I hold
My peace, knowing well the cost of false embrace.
Why should I reprove your unworthiness?
My love is well-known, and none
Can then question its worth or mar it.
  If you'd but remove this wicked purpose,
  My love would forevermore be kind.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though my years be yet unstirred by love's thrill.
Yet, 'tis my love that doth her falsehood spawn,
Creeping in her words, as plants grow
Within the shell of their parent;
So my age smiles upon her, unlooked
For wrinkles, though she wears them not unseen.
And, for her lies, I'll boast she is true,
A woman of consequence, no less,
Than those vain deceivers called prophets.
  So, if she be untrue, I lose not time,
  Since truth itself doth almost convince.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Hungry for love's sweet reward;
And my direst is a devil erst from hell,
Despair's companion and king of ill.
I have called my good angel love,
Whose looks were a conqueror once,
Now degraded and poor; and my direst,
Despair's ensnarer and murd'rous guide.
My good angel presides, and my direst
Doth ministry in evil days prove.
  So, either love or its contrary is mine,
  Since Paradox' better is my worst."
