Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
So others' voices seemed but kind,
Swept in me, their intended spite
Released by the appeal of my own heart.
Her eyes, which taught her tongue to sing
Apart, as if by themselves kept
From ordering what they should by rule do;
Her tongue, which fell short in telling the story
Of Love's sweet passion, rehearsed
The bitter vow, "I hate" transformed
By tongues that traced the lines of Desire's face.
  "I hate" from "I love" thus changed,
  Became a kind of song, words inadequate.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Creating a double strain on both my friend and me.
No penance serves to cleanse this impurity,
Entwined as it is with our love's embrace,
So deeply fixed is the vice,
Revealed no wound, whited sepulchers are
For Love's own fair visage to lie in.
I heal not with my thoughts, though they may strive
To bring my love's darker veil to light,
For Love's true remedy lies in self-discipline,
  In the cage of my own heart, where its weight is laid.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
To suppose what pleasure thine eyes have witnessed?
O, know, sweet love, I judge thy pleasure;
Thy eyes, though 'tis but a temporal guest,
Decide the worth of that which they see doth excel.
Then, if they should say my thoughts were swerving,
It was not my soul that wrought this accident,
But my eyes, trusting on their former trust,
Were forced to depart hence, their judgment final.
  For seeing is but a temporal guest
  Which stays for a while, then departeth.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler nature to my body's treachery.
My spirit informs my flesh that it may strive
For victory in love; the flesh, now alive,
Rises at your name, its purpose to derive
From your affection, its reason to thrive.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, ever so near,
  To stand by you, to fall by your side, my dear.

I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,

As truth does flatter, unspoken,

We find comfort in lies,
