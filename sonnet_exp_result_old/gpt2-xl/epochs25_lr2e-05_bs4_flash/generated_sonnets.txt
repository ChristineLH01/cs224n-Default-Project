Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Her heart, touched by remorse, then did show
A tender touch, a softer blow,
And taught her tongue a warmer phrase
For "I love" to utter.
"I love" from that moment did jump
From her heart to mine, and kept up
The conversation of our tongues;
At times, though often, it fell into slumber,
Like a former lover who stays home
To rest his case with words that rhyme.
  But when love's sweet prayer got the best of her,
  "I love" from her heart she tuned to curse.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
In hopes that the stronger will win the fight.
My love is as a bed from which noon falls,
Despair and all away departed,
Save for this hope, all belief gone,
That one or other of us shall see light.
Fruitless searches for truth and proof,
Now faith hath planted his everlasting crown,
And senses at pleasure mused are,

  If this be false, and I am proved untrue,
  No love laboratory can show me fairness.

Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my faults too deeply.
For, as you compel me to rewrite
My sanctity vows, promise me sweet success,
Preserve your affection, though your senses tell

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
In her hand, a potion to keep his lusty state,
Yet, as she twisted the flask, it took fire,
A venom steeped in sweet sleep, a fearful matter.

A purified take on Love's own brew,
A lethal dose to quell his ardors' peak,

Their darkest work, forever to rage.
  Yet, as she diluted the cure, he regain

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Were in their vibrating breasts motionless,
Gasping for breath, whilst his fiery brand ran.
The virtuous snow-white bird, true and dutiful,
One precious note short of heaven's broad gauge,
Was struck by the deity's pen, and fell dead;
The world mourned, but holy love no more did glow,
For sweet Mary had promised eternal rest.
  In anger the god sent to heaven's gate,
  To scold the world, and clean out its abandoned gate.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though experienced in affairs of state;
Thus, though she knows my prime is past,
I appear to her as youthful again,
And in this flattery I seem to succeed,
Although in my age a wiser man
Than I am could never in looks convert.
But since her love is false, and I know it,
I wound her with constant malice,
That she may think me back again,
Though in my state again a wiser man
Is found wanting.
