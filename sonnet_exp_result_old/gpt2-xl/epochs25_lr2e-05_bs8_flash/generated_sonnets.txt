Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And for a moment fate smiled, granting me grace,
And "I love" thence became a curse,
Like a mountain goat that murmurs at dawn.
"I love" from that hour did stamp
My heart with anguish, like the wretch who hunts
For love's delight, yet loses his stake;
"I love" from that hour did heels assemble
And mark my retreat, desolate,
Where love had taken a retreat.
  "I love" from that hour did I learn
  To cry out, "I love," every hour;

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When all thy neighbors proffer their gifts?
Nature's self-substantial clay, methinks,
Deserves better of thy subsistence than this poor sap,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Excusing not the bitterness that balm doth lend.
So am I, in love's anxiety, ever spinning,
And nearing the very end of my worrying,
When all my threads of conjecture are woven together,
And neatly fingered dread of losing you enters;
Then, sure of what I dread, I begin to fray,
Gaze on death's dark vast feature, and say,
"This calamity my love warned me of;
  To give my heart another youth, some portion,
  When life's brief agony's reached its bitter end."

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest sorrow provoke thee to stop thy riot.
Rather, be a transgressor, and I'll be thy hate,
Thy innermost self, from which thou wilt totallyhest
To harm no soul, whether friend or foe.
I'll bear thy transgressions, the very worst,
If thou refrain from willful wrongs toward me.
Oh, should thou resort to violence,
I'll join thy host, thy disgrace shall be mine;
  And besides, I'll quit this world, too,
  For love keeps fast my heart's and hand's friend.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
Yet twice love hath sworn me swift misconduct.
For to take thee away I once wrote law,
And to thee reputed lawful bond still I bear;
But reasons therefor I cannot find proved,
And so the lawgiver twice hath hidden his head.
Himself twice married, to two queens quite wrong,

But why she thought her child so dear thou know'st not.
  Then in intent I tried to prove my cause,
  But got no argument from thy humor.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
The violet in his bud both dear and fair,
But when it met the ardent fire it turned brown.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Darted in their streams to their ills, young and hard.
The holy sleepers their blushing shadows raised,
Concealed the sins of their eager eyes,
Still baiting the flame of love, which burned bright,
Others chided, yet they not chastised dared,
To lift the fire of love, from sleep's sweet dominion.
The blameless sleepers then did perceive,
The blissful state of love they were enslaved;
  And, willing captives, they underwent change,
  From slaves to lovers, as they were before.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though I be married wich believe not since hatching.
So all men swear by her, though they ne'er swear,
And truth itself in their Colts is sworne
To graces that to their cups are given.
But why her false breath so gladly doth blow,
Why loves' throats so quick to cruel breathing grow,
Why spends all his life in loves' sports
But Death himself his sweet hours to visit!
  Beauteous beauty saith through these lines:
  Hers is truth, but his whose breath well knows.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And in his weakness finds a weakness too.
His name is Hector, and he is fair myself,
But for love of me he turns pale and coughing.
Another is my poor precious self, proud and proud,
Now hounded by that love of mine which I have made so.
He too is sick of love, and wills me die,
But I will not leave him till I have tried him first.
  And thus, like two ambulances racing towards each other,
  I oppose my own heart and make him leap.
