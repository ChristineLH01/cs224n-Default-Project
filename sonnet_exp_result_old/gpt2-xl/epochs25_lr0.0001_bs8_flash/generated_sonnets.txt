Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Such loathing inward as outward
Was fixed and ever rolling in change.
Charming as a winter's bath
May seem to summer's cheeks, but to me
Summer's beauty was nothing novel.
Her cheeks were broad and bare, and full of youth,
Yet empty awaited the pledge of winter's care;
And as the basest clouds do blot the heaven,
So did her cheeks indirectly contain.
  But when they were filled with cheeks of steel,
  Mine eyes and voice were bereft, and she felt no blame in losing.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
In hopes that it may heal the heart at last.
My love is chief among diseases, rank below
Whereto th' inviting card of infection received;
It is the true womb in which it feasts,
Where vital power is pumped, and all diseased things filtered.
Yet it feasts upon this, that it may be cured;
That is, the well of love, which never can be drained.
  If this be error, and upon me proved,
  I never writ, nor no man ever loved.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
The virgin's gentle tears shed rare
A light on Chiron's harsh brand, which yet might rise.
The waters kept the brand's edge from foaming,
A barrier to the ocean's savage cell.
Yet, when this rose was touched by mortal foot,
The waters lost their hold, and the fiery brand
Expelled the heat, and regained their serene state.
  Yet, still, the prisoner's despair is heightened,
  For love's own commander, in aid of death, does stand.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Their eyes first to his cheek then to his breast,
With gentle hands they caught the fire,
And burnt him up to a cauldron full of anguish.
The fiery brand, by cool well water drenched,
Expelled the heat, and gained a healing potion,
But I, the captive of my lady's devotion,
Came to this well, in hope of my own healing,
  Discovering that love-fire warms the waters,
  But water cannot quench the lover's ardors.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
While in reality I am but nineteen years old.
For how can I then be ignorant
Of these facts, which she most assuredly shows?
And by what means do I suppose I am not taught
To swear by such holy and precious things?
Whence does my love derive this art,
To twist truth to make it seem false?
Though she knows my age, she loves me still,
And works upon my affection, promising me true.
  But why should I swear by false reasoning,
  When I know well that every word I say is false?
