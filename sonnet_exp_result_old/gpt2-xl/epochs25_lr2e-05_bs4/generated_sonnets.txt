Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And,, for her love, that heart did vow
To stay cold no longer than I could spy.
But, as Ipthes did grow on her head
And scales began to split her skin,
Her eyes, brimming with tears, did cry
"I love you, yet change your mind."
To me, who languished, they spoke
Like mourners calling out a slain comrade:
"He lives, yet she changes her mind."
  For Love's own heart had sworn her pledge,
  To stay as she was, after sorrow's reign.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While other mortals have strength to thine arms?
Profitless wretch, why dost thou use such strength,
To raise such trouble on thyself and sit
In glory before thy sins are weighed?
Even torturer, why dost thou use such might,
To make thyself sad and wide-awake look?
Hidden sin, why dost thou use such might,
To make thy name clearer in the blot of public?

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Precious as a treasure hidden in the earth.
Therefore love's hush is not his, though long awaiting,
Nor harsh reproach his, though merit commend.
He that harbors love in his heart does constrain
His heart to be all in all, to plus amount,
And that very sufficient, to love it turn.
But, out of sight, his secret self does speak,
Sland'ring him with his own disdain, extreme.
  For thee, I harbor love, concealed still,
  Assuming a seeming hush, though deep within me.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which should dispense true knowledge from my sight?
O, know, sweet love, my judgment is confined
Within the shadow of thy beauty's reign,
Which drowns out all my other thoughts.
Yet, love, understand this truth:
My eyes may believe, but their heart cannot respect,
The soul of thy beauty, which in their breast doth lie.
  For thou art so near, and their thoughts so fixed,
  That they whatever looks they step into the Plain,
  Top it with their interior sea.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though I be as old as she.
But when she fears my days are done,
And fears I may err in judgment,
She binds me more closely, more dear,
And I am more fool than before,
Ensnared in this bind of love's falsehood.
O, let my love when she prays me swear,
That she is true, though I cannot know it;
Then I trust her, though she may be false,
For true things love to hear believed.
  But if she be false, I lose my mind,
  And then I no longer love her.
