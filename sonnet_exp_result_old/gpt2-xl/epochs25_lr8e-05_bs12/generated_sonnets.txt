Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
So that sorrow did ensue
Where joy had all too short a date.
In sorrow's guise did she make her nest,
Where appetites bred before love's spring
Were given full scope, and acted as if by pleasure;
But, love, you were there, and made yourself
Much more to be desired than any woman's hand.
For how could any woman's breathing face boast
Of all those parts that in you are displayed?
  Unless this were beauty's resting-place,
  In slumber quite awa'er idleness dies.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Creating anew each blighted estate.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,

O, teach me, O heartless, to liken you,

  Then teach me to love you in that hue,
  Since thence I must derive all my woe.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And for the hurt thou hast given me thrice more.

  But do not so, love; I love thee in such sort
  As, thou being mine, mine is thy good report.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Instead worshiped his flaming brand,
Crazed by their devotion, now smoking dangerous.
The forthright lawyer, now awake, did vow,
To guard the lawful love-brand from lawful shame,
But Safety did not send him forth his burden,
For guileless Love, so well instructed,
Would strip him of his virtue, and put
Another in his place, even more true.
  So Safety, converted from the fool,
  set him on fire, wherewith he was put to shame.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my worse traitor is a woman in plight.
Both shunned, though in different ways,
By kings and common women deemed fair.
I may not ever love thee as thou dost sing,
Lest my better part should do thee shame,
And my bad one should do thee shameful wrong.
O, let me suffer, suffer I to remain,
That like two spirits ye may see
Which one do I love best, and that love
  Where my bad one doth lives.
