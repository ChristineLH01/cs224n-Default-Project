Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Such teeth saw I that Love's own touch did bestow
That taught them to bite and to withdraw.
"I wish they had eyelids" said I,
"to weep with fear when they saw my shame;
But they have no time to weep, since
Their blood is Love's own, and Love's face.
So, white as those tears are now,
Like the dawn they come and go,
Just weeping for love, that does't allow.
  And yet, if they could but cease,
  They'd see a better state to me."

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Swept into the shadows like to the pilaster's land?
The reason is thou art not conceived fair,
Within the embrace of thy outer light;
Thy beauty buds and blooms in debonair disgrace,
A blot on thy right hand, the sovereign disgrace.
So if thou survive this blighted age,
And shalt by fortune once more achieve art,
The sweetest use shall be thy oblation;
Thou thyself shalt use what thy body doth owe.
  There lives more life in one of thine eyes
  Than both thy husbandry and thyself can know


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Just to indulge my reproachful heart.
No joy is so sweet as that which maketh my heart complain,
Even if it be tainted with frailties mighty.
If it be pure, 'tis no wonder that it is sick;
How can joy be sweet if it be diseased?
If it be tainted, 'tis no wonder that it is well;
Why should I be sick if it be well?
  Then, whatsoever joy it doth give,
  Tread very carefully lest it sickens you too.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And in thy vices see thy truth displayed?
O, from whence art thou ensnared, that thou mayst lie
In every blessed meekness of thy will?
Though I should walk in thy right way,
As on thy stone phantoms do reside,
Thy sins should seem fairer than form could show;
For thou art so matched to thyself,
That thou thyself dost inform on thy glory.
  My love, to whom in thy aspect thou art dressed,
  Receives not thy being, but thy double falsehood.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest sorrow embolden my harshness,
And ill-gotten gains teach manners nigh efface.
For, as you benefit by my resentment,
Then I lose by your unfair gain;
And thus, in turn, I am hurt, repaired,
And all for naught, for love lies high.
What reason will my affection think me lame
When it sees fit to put me first,
It may condescend to call me by thy side,
But not from thence permit my claims to go.
  For it knows well my place, endures not lame
  To serve thy passion

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In a pool nearby, where it froze, solid.
The waters kept his lover's face ever fair,
But as the snow melts in summer's bloom,
His image rots, left in the night.
The waters could not wash away his shame,
Nor could they drown his love, enshrined
In a crystal mountain's land of fair recall.
So Dian plunged the brand into the well nearby,
Where Love's fire cooled, left no trace in vain.
  In this way, Love's fire was kept alive,
  Until the day when it should be donated.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Adorning their virtue with purest wiles,
Rightly did their ways introduceples chide,
Making fine art their virtue, adorning
Their thoughts with beauty's ornamentation.
But Mate, thy virtuous deeds do soothe my soul,
Enriching my nature with thy marvelous grace,
That every vice is fairer held than wrong,
By thy addition, mine own sins are eased,
And for a time, at least, I am whole.
  For nothing soothes my soul but thy,
  And yet thou dost create a but a tear.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Although I be forty-three winters old.
Thus, though she knows my prime has come and gone,
She looks upon me still uncraved,
And loves me, though my days be short,
For she knows well my nature does change.
But why does she not then believe me true,
When I swear her true love is truth?
Because I swear her false love is true,
She thinks me another youth, unloosed
From the chains of love, who roam free.
  Thus, in our faults, we find comfort in lies,
  And so, we soothe ourselves


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And quite contrite in love he seems to me;
But sweetest love hath no name, no holy bower,
And in its sweets most men miss their gauge.
My heavy love does depend upon my cheer,
And in my cheer I measure up to a storm;
So, either way, my love is double twain,
Or, in spite of myself, I believe
Heavier love does surround and enclose.
  So, if he be true, and I am false,
  In love I have found my solace and my sorrow.
