Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
The other, lips that tasted and mouthed
Like the counterfeit that they were,
Speaking not truth to honor what they had strayed.
How could my heart believe in such reproach,
Unless it were from lips that knew
The shame of their bad report?
And yet, since from such loveliest vices arose,
My heart was with the painted lie,
Like lead that finds its way to the well.
  For she that painted my heart so red
  Wound me with false colors, me to seek heaven's own.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Deprived of the light of thy soul's blaze?
If thy spirit be strong, increase thy fire,
Make acclamations to those who call on thee,
In time, when time has strayed and thee summoned,
See anew the beauty of thy days,
Recall the fervent prayer of thy youth,
That beauty graces every page,
In every breath that fills the soul.
  Grant me the strength, to face thy trials,
  And come to grips with this defiant curse.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
An infection that it will never outlast.
My reason doth compel my lips to speak
Of health and beauty, as if by starved seals,
In the presence of flesh, both sweet and sour,
Temptation's call, my purpose obscure.
But why should my flesh, so full of life,
Consume what food doth life store in it?
  I say, love, be contented, for life's sweetest treasure doth grow.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Revealed by eyes quite alien to all I know?
What shadows on my figure doth my soul perceive
That shadows under his soft light are laid?
Mark what ensnared love, that kills all right,
By looking upon me with twisted and unfriendly view.
O, knew I not heaven were so fair,
That it might ensnare my heart from right clear,
To love one so far from true that he doth stray?
  Love hath taught it to ensnare my sense,
  To look on shadows, that doth blind heaven's store.

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest sorrow seem a prelude to love's boldness.
Confess, love, your true value is concealed,
Truth's sweetest betrayer lies in your remorse.
So, love, rework your memory so it fits,
For if I betray thee, no love's fire shall fail,
And your true worth, in retrospect, I'll reveal.
  Just make sure I never, ever know thy name.


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
Why shouldst thou marry a double falsehood,
Making ceremony double all thy husbandry?
For in thy double falsehood I perceive
Thy beauty double, thy worth none;
By changing thy manners to thine own view
Stirred rascals to thy double truth.
  Yet in these double errors do I see
  The sweetness of thy double worth mixed.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
Thence sprung the curious story of love in birds,
In poets' strange rapture, love invoked their art,
And left behind only the guise of beauty's lesson.
This lovely mystery I, captivated by thine eyes,
Was sworn to find out, and found it not;
But curious ghosts, dancing in heaven's name,
Consulted my love's true love, and found a cause
To praise songs even though their fervent accents lacked.
  In this knowledge, I learned that true love cannot be painted,
  For love's flame remains before eyes, even in sleep

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,

Seduced him with false hearts, as the goddess Artemis did.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unschooled in life's craft;
Yet, she loves not truth so dearly,
That she thinks me of worth less,
That I am frequently her fool.
This she does so, that I may know
Truth's semblance not lies in her cheek,
Nor lies in my hearing,
Whate'er their source lies;
Yet, I do love her, though she knows it's
Gored by tongues, and laughs not at all.
  For she loves men, and nothing so;
  For she that endures this doth live.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And he walks as if from vast world;
The plain weeds love no rank renown,
And for him my sweet faith none so strong.
Alas, why his grace has some slight deviation,
Why so not my other love, so dear?
For why should he mar his visage when mine is green?
Why does he linger, dressed in rags,
Counting dead hours for jewels never dearer?
For why should poor health, so dear and new,
Steal life's chief treasure from his place?
  Death's gift, sweet thief, keeps him ever near.
