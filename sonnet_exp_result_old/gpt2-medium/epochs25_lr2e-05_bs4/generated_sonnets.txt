Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Moral courage then did not spring from lips so fair,
But strength from those lips that cost more.
Nailing worn beauty to a table,
Chasing shadows that whispered true,
I loathed those lips that spent so much gold;
A heart that grieves for what it once did win,
Would fain be chased even to the edge of earth.
  But, Love, your works end in truth,
  My love does for you end in truth's own zone.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
The treasure of my heart, which every man doth plunder.
Whence dost thou derive this peculiar grace,
That when I indulge in dalliance,
Thyself my drink, and I thy soil?
To what derive dost thou give this charm,
That to me, thy debtor, becomes my captain?
Indeed, it is thy art, in ill entertained
That doth make me the wretched I am;
To be at one with thee, my state doth confine.
  To part from thee I must suffer,
  By thy services, I am humbled.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
Myselfe and others whom you solde me?
No, but do act cruel to me
As for my crime in your resolves
Since in my pen being defaced yet
Can cause your ambassadors news of me.
More do I refute thy case
Then hinder my groaning flesh with like feigned pain
Or do kinder physic in my part
As something riper gentle more
Than writtly word thou outlastst me.
  All things well with me where thou livest
  Love but thine brings me all best things wrong.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
To view false as true when thou dost lie
And thou most impious clerk of my heart.
O, why hast thou this might to do
To lengthen my wretched plight, make me moan,
That I am by thy dearest spite made lame
And view wrong with false gladness?
Him that dost this task knows well
Whate'er thy pleasure or thy ruination base.
  O, let him prove an enemy of good will,
  To bear the cross of thy vices and annoy.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest guilt seem more acceptable than love's bliss.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may strive
For victory in love; the flesh, now alive,
Rises at your name, its purpose to derive
From your affection, its reason to thrive.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, ever so near,
  To stand by you, to fall by your side, my dear.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep;
A lusty beauty, in her hand did pace,
A master thief in slumber does behold.
The lily's purest fervor does wake Love's eternal fire,
And to her touch he lends his all-consuming fire,
Pouring his essence from heart to heart into her hand.
The rose's sweet scent doth make Love's soul glow,
And by the perfume doth Cupid sate his fierce fire;
  But all this the sun doth do, while Cupid slumberrs near.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Blasted their tresses to death with flaming torches,
And made pyramids of fresh-pricked hair,
To honor the dead goddess of his passion.
So the poet, in lust, injures his art,
And hits his muse on the wrong note,
Who, instead of healing, brings forth a new evil,
Chasing away Love's eternal fire.
  The fire cannot be quenched, for it is in vain,
  For fire burns both warm and cold, and both bare.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
But love knows no sentiment, so she works
The lie with passionate art,
Lying to us all, as frequently
As young lovers her breath attains age.
This I find replete with many a false comment,
False seeing, concealed and sweeted;
On holy pins and bracelets false love doth impute,
Naming me wrong to false looks.
  And yet love doth it oft, though she says it is no.
  For nothing is so true which she doth profess.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
A sweeter man than all the rest;
And that perfect good looks upon me,
His thoughts at heart never were false.
But thou, my sadness, mine assailed fair,
Out of such a land I part,
At thy sweet desire dull thy rude scope.
Oh, how much more fair thou shouldst be,
Mesmerizing thy spirit when I weep;
Bring me such cheer as that cherub lends,
A heavy anchor for my crumbling woe.
  Then may I love thee, and that sweet content,
  Whose sweet fire grants everything new.
