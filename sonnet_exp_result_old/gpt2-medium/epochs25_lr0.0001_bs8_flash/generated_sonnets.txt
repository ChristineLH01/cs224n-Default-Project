My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
My soul entwined in love's strange vice.
At times the fever doth beat so high,
Suff'ring the strength of my friend,
Then laughing with him, as if by fortune's part,
And telling him how to make his heart grow pale,
As if by fortune's command.
  Such is my love, as is being fed,
  By fortune's ravenous call, my heart's desire.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which dispels the blind and makes the fair chase?
If my sight be marred, why then my mind
Drinks up the poisoned remedies to cure it?
If my eyes be true, why do they err,
Seeking what no spirit doth tell,
And straying far from truth and right?
Why should my heart believe in scattered dreams,
When my sight is clear and my mind clear?
  Love knows it is a grievous burden to bear,
  The loss of eternal truth, when all is said and done.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And I, a sinner, did seal my love to thee.
But why should false vows be more credible,
When sweet lies can never be so sweet?
For to no falsehood I cling, being false in love,
Unless my love were poisoned, and then not so.
  Or if it be tainted, 'tis no great crime,
  For we whose hearts are tied do it wrong.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In her bosom's womb, where it grew strong.
The waters kept the fire's essence, though, alive,
A bubbling, healing source for all ailments dear.
Yet, when my sweetheart's eyes met mine, Love's face
Expelled the fire, for Love's true love was born.
Her eyes, though, Love's soul followed,
A haven for thoughts and dreams, a place to race.
  So I slept, free from Love's grasp,
  Unyielding to his scythe, my queen.
