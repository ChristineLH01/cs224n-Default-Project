Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
To me she wore the hue of a winter's night,
And lent it grace, which false lips could not pull.
But when my dear one opened her mouth to speak,
Her tongue touched the pitch of brass,
A tone that shook the heart of every pen;
Her heart, touched by this, ceased to print,
And I, to stop the flow, began to rehearse
The words that she had made herself.
  A lesson so sweet, I learned to stop,
  By heart's own power; but still, I did stop.

When every free power doth thrust in thy direction?
Grant me the pardon, dear heart, of foregoing

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And creating a second self, more zealous,
To sift through life's myriad to find the single case.
This thought ignites a war within my soul,
A fight to the death, to deny thy boon,
And snatch thee from my grasp, before it's too late.
  I've called upon thee countless times,
  And know it is your soul that calls, in vain.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And paint deceit where truth should lay?
Even so, couldst thou use my love as a bait,
To make me believe in thy false face,
The fairest thing in the world, conceived and planned.
Would it not wrong the soul of love, to abandon
The mortal coil that winds thee like a wanderer?
O, no, 'tis not enough that through thee I see;
My heart still has that gift, more than enough to sway.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entwined in my wrongs.
Conscience may err, though you confess,
And I may errant love advocate;
So, be assured, I love you as you are:
From each other's hearts, my crooked love scatters,
Though they may stray, in their twisted ways.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And solemn vows of many a solemn date.
For what right have I to call thee fair,
Unless my sight be false, and in it lies?
Or can my sweet love be truly loved,
When in our deeds I find but hatred,
And in our groans, only gladness find?
No, be true, my sweet love; yet in my spite,
Thy looks betray me oft to be untrue.
  For thee I often swear fair eyes do deceive,
  And in my madness do speak oft of thee.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Into a well nearby spring his blood came.
The waters kept the fire's essence, though, alive,
A bubbling, healing source for all ailments dear.
A cure for heart failure, raved the faithful,
Yet none could see to cure his face,
Whose veins, dyed by his own embrace,
Revealed a second youth, of purest red,
Beyond the pale of mortal age.
  A cure for rheumatism, likewise, sought,
  But found no reason to live, other than to grow stronger.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Came dancing by; one maiden fair,
With virtuous hand, did defile the king's heart,
By his fiery brand, which countless true souls had wrought.
The maiden, too, shamefaced, left the scene,
Her lover, heartless, unmoved,
And the lesson true hearts should avoid,
Like the fiery brand, by which innocence is burnt,
  Leaping into the burning bush.

Love's deceit is crowned with honor,

For I, a deceived spouse, confess
Love's truth, though she knows it not.
  Thus, in our errors, she thinks me true,

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And he with me in his sweet thoughts do contend;
The direst tempests alter his aspect,
And he with them alters his swift extremity.
Three winters' gusts have touched me first,
Three springs of life have I known,
Yet none have my love so kind and right;
Three scents of April in three Junes did reproach,
Since first I saw thee in my youth.
Alas, that beauty which my sweet heart doth bestow,
  Wound me with three loves, and thee alone abide.
