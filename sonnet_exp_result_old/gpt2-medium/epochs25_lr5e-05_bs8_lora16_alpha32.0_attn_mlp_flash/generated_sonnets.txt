Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
To enclose mine anguished painless life,
And sole share in solitude committed to suffer?
Consider my feeling scarce satisfied,
That as thou heavest not damage in loose possession,
The promise for my labor's closeness doth not sound,
So am I happy that breast shows no fear.
  But whether this defect shall live or die,
  Rest thou there miserable while worst enemies assail it.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
My ermine sceptre brings for atonement no sweet kiss

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep himself.
Wide his foetids rose a weeping maid, full of black,
Cunning smoky the woods wintry while her eyes ablaze;
And sweet hedges were eaten, and large wainscotts fell;
And loud alarms rocked bright round all like lament.
All that his wit did love beautiful charm,
His place burned at beauteous fashion;
And his land had all glossy speech made for fools' utter
That hoped a girl might stand beside their state
That in hate did shift their cause
Which thy verse unhappily speedeth with hearts bereft.
 


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
With rapine and all mortal end they work;
Some will labor less honest months,
Some more those wildest summers below
They roughened nature with prey most foolishly hunted,
While with slothfulness even life they transform
O'erspread them are plundered with breaks.
For all appetite bears all love and his true sweetness
Is sad ravishment, grown tetchy of greasy burden;
When on abused cloud's-shadow shakes beside him,
Then ravenry, sudden as love's arrival abroad,
Records him with all yoked companion gone.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Caught at her country's court, where all crime leads;
Or else, my deceit may be found
Wretch of madness, pride of cruelty never being overthrown.
To some things point she predestined her,
And opinions doth sway unjustly from birthday
And send into curious description sick lovers right beyond,
Being elders in flower, born to the true sun.
  The most loveless are ghostly false watches upon their eyes,
  Yet in their rosy weather security doth seems sleep.
