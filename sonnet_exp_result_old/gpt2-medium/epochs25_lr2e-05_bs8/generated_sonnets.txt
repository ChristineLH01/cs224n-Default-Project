Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Whose pale tresses, black as pitch-dark slate,
Or whiteness in some strange fashion they had possessed,
Were moved in that antique manner to kiss
Upon my other cheek, as a coal's blaze might rise;
And, lo, the echo of those sweet words did restart
That loathsome blood, which, slow and heated,
Dissipated as if by water and men!
  Alas, that Love's beauty ever touched so pale!
  Hers, no whitier; but theirs being black, still hath a note.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And when the fever's beating cease,
The cure is found in that which succumbed.
So I, in love's throes, drink down the cure,
Craving that which more outweighs the pain;
In my madness, I forget to weigh
The true worth of love, starved for blood.
  So I poison my health, robbing thee of my light,
  Lest sickness claim thee as its trophy.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Into a well nearby, to quench.
To prevent the thief from stealing the sweet water,
Dian secluded her own beauty,
By making two groves, one plain,
And one of purple, by art to make bold.
The first she clothed in purple,
The second in his love,
And poached their beauty, both fine,
So they turned into mire, where one lives
And many a pure soul were marred there.
  And thus, on waking, he fell to steal,
  A lady's beauty, his own vital flame grew.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Faithful and discreet, though oft lies askew;
His face affords charm, sweetly fair,
His manners kind, though outward might frown.
He knows my sorrows, my joys' confounds,
His fear only proves my guilt in part.
He bears these truths with quiet awe,
And I, a simple creature, believe him blind.
  And yet, sweet love, you've seen, what faith can love
  More truly than this: the silent truth.
