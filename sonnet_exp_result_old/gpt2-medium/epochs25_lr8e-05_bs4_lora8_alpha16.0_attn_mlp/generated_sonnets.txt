Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
So, hated no more by me in heaven
Nor all the world then I around see,
Nor above among fairing o'ers the thoughtless,

To her blessed Muse, or use in raiment woo
Nor praise from the distance, or pation held
From when some minister before thy ear doth sing.
  This speech let the kisses of the world baffle
  And time shall weary him where thou art.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Causing places all dreary, and fruits bitter,
Since heretofore fresh water with thee dost help?
Shall any barbarian's scorn say, "With this reckless pace
Wean weaker produce strong
And weightier produces void and places miserable,"
As ours calls boldness a continuance
And shows plainly still, ignorant of time or rest?
  Be no more infamous, my dear boy, since well paid;
  Be not accused of lesser crime;   Mine best is perfected wherein I live.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Applying physic to bread, and enduring hot summer's
Unlike winter's frost to dryness,
Curing without sepulcher no haply relief,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
Dian was always merry, even in that sleep;
Then grew a great majesty between thee and me.
But thou must I so defame out of lust,
That eyes as glad and fair as women's shall see me
Since words else could not well express.
Let words give them fair rank, but no strokes art
To evil my beginning hero, fair Dian.
  For thou know'st how three against one,
  All alike to succeed one who began him.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
If so made and honest, such a dreamer deceive.
A child of ours is sworn to add slights;
These truths equally equal seem me foolish for to change.
Yet in every whiche if I will change,
In spite of nothing can Fortune most infirm;
So I've had contrary to wonder cast,
In numerous princes she breaks full our renown.
  Is it, which thou desirest amend,
  Or everything that lies in shape withstands me,

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And knows his needs to repeat.
My sooth, taken on fools can renew
And amuse wiser hours.
But when his pattern change, like his brother,
Bearing my dear wretch at his side,
My most part on me, with true Love stead'ning,
Love boldly compliments, and shows reams
Of little matters urged on me dumb gaping.
  His rascally nephews do their trick;
  'Tis not jealousy, but hate that saps bold leads.
