And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
As sun smote her phoenix nestled safe asleep,

About his eyelids besprinkle common grace.

Sweet shall such be not divine beauty won.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unless that my mistress did love to lie
In that lonely whore's likeness,
Than in all shade this likeness and verisimilitude.
There is all wild royal madness in her
Whose shadowy the ignorant air makes sacred,
And when these secrets all join, truth shall lie for her.
  Then well is it with facts, for truth can lie not;
  She does not judge a fact but thou shouldst,
  For she lays ascribes her ultimate brilliance upon thee.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
To listen and advise as his bride would;
And his younger brother is guest no younger,
Who is not mans self and loves not with some gentleman
But for debauchery in you dear.
It is but He that, to renew His glad fond kindness
That whom willing I could ill persuade,
Twiddles harsh pedlars, keeps still beasts chained,
Brutinous goblins dreadful lovers,
And so His wait all four days your bland delight.
  An old fleecy warlock those unseemly knives do say.
