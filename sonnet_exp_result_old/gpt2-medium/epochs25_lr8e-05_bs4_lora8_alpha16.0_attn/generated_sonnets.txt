Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Is this mine natural happiness? O, the wretched love
More freely sprung from mortals we save:

Men in middle age cursed to excess spend
All thy pleasanties at unnecessary use

For that which longer nurseth the disease,

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Whose ideals do own brave things justify.
His dashing light astounds my awake.
Pour forth sweet showers of sunshine, he shows
Rows and rows of heaven built on pomp;
He depends on mighty masses which credit
With his tales unteach his talk.
Foremost use consider sweet alms that my thoughts starve
Of dole and doctrine how I sound as main.
I depart, relying still upon smells that fires roam,
To preach where the hew is latent only:
