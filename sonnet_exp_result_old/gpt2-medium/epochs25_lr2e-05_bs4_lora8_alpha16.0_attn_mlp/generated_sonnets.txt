Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Sisters gave without her loved all away;
Friends, bearing with lonesome dearest ones,
Blinded by summer child sweet and unfeigned lived;
But with my love no diamond shall cut it short.
What jewel could praise my daughter's eye,
Tongued after the latest diffusion,
Where taught the vanrey design doth grow!
Wo'er still sorrow she suffered of mind,
Young tender years, gentle hands or sworn enemies,
Or bewailed sad lost days at like reward.
O, sigh of grieve of love (slain years receive)

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Or partake of the ten hypocrisies old,
Before thy statues were elected heavenly heroes
That engraved lessons and service upon heads
As praise to such dumb weal show?
Why did men so lazy disdain to reign in justice
And enjoy rank instead of being;
And spends the fruit of this end I lose,
This I repent and my posterity suffer
To bear that shame upon the cheeks of God?
O, my friend, neither great dangers nor perils hang
Nor pain are safe till these make greatest use;
  Then steal these advantages, like sights fair,
  Deny entreat

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Afraid no sooner of dying, than alive again.
Were that every one of books were written for him,
Book may but ill the hour seem urgent,
Defilling how scarce he must contented degree
To write, gently so while he contends
That nought but constant praise doth live.
For heaven hath heaven an eye brimming and pondering,

3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Having darts sight for mouth of hitness,
The warfare of lenses with o'erbuilt aries
Which follow death's path to produce decay,
Or tell His tale (to him our time is spent)
A wanderer's play to o'erground-trouble,
And since so sweet of face I am wont,
To enjoy my lovers drunk by filthier condition,
Returned up to dispute departed guest.
  "Fie upon your blessed messenger be," quoth green light,
  "thine agreement only abused rudely."

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
Meself, when thee be as an id that wills
Nothing from himself?
Spend fullfull tears of honest love the punishment paid
And weary beggars change their posture
In worldly snow to dance on the hallowed ground;
When there is country and death, tide does storm and
Life in earth and space threatens cost or threatens freeze;
And gave wild love to me so shallow that I scarce tasted
The thought of withdrawal from thee.
  I sweet and false slayer o'erstitch against thee,
  But can neither so loss myself being left undone.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
That I love the lens. If such render thee base
With anchor's open but suspend thine eyes to my heart,
Have none in thy loose time stronger than I am.
Nor dare I sound thy wing without endeavor,
And make replies just what wavering thrills thine ears
To sway which voides thee through luxury could not aim.
  Whose mouths do sing ye higher praises,
  Yet shadows through appearance admit your reversion,
  And plants' sweet fruits in thorns' door do shut.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
You might indeed instruct my dear tongue more
Than education merely suggest.
It modifies here a corrupted tongue's prejudice
Against your love's sharpest accents.
Bold clear that displeases not: fare can tell
Whither they happen with clever language even
The tightest tenor brought by pleasure
Rolled up acrid to erst in dolorous eyes.
Then my sweet age doth praise, seen through poets holier minds,
Gods may wonder how they could dull with age me
That beauty upon your choicest brow.

7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
Hast thou so bad a course when thou wroughtest thy likings
To animate us without mingling my love;
Is it another because thou this woeful anniversary has denied
The love by withdrawing affection fully disallowed?
Begone, thou tarn; my woe disguising thee so.
  Make sweet love thy wane pain, tears to pain keep it groan.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
A busy lawyer witness trying to match
Truth with falsehood; indeed,
Shall I then be alone conversing on
His law, or his falsehood? Shall
I learn that my love is jealous, and some shadow
Of interest pressed on him? If this be so,
Alas, dear O, beware of the latter note:
This man, mad with age, fear is foremost
And steals love's thraldom from each other's cup,

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And that man with me to my danger hopes
For a blessed reward this strange poor day.
A treasure is scarce what cost I it, fair or fair—
By luck or stubborn born of wrong,
Crueltty not malice to make money do,
And where even toward that doth he partake
Faith he hath his judgement deemed as his merit,
Fore, love free and thrice praised,
In pennies borrowed, or still out free.
  O, sweet of spirits, love and hope remaining
  In this state wherein so many do pervert,
  Do this that you well may
