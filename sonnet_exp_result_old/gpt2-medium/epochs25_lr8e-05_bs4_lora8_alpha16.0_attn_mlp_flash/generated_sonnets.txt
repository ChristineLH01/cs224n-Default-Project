My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And preferring death yet lives,
Resembling myself dead, more mortal than over;
Knowing that my true worth lives but by air,
In heaven where all mortal being lives before.
This world liveth that which it pricks,
And thence not wealth except food brings food;
And my crime is of heaven, being found such,
In heaven being as in the nature of clay,
For that is born neither above nor below.
  This hole none my enemies can pierce,
  Therefore in my crime there is no end.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
