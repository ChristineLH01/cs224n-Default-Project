Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Crowning my love, she set my heart to sleep,
And hid my sadness from sight.
Yet, in that hour, her glance revealed
My deepest sorrow to hear,
And brought me to my knees,
As she, inspired by my woe,
Crowned my heart, and I fell to pray.
But why then did she not intervene?
For love's subtle touch brings out the best
Of ill, in those lips that lie.
  So I sleep, in love's sweet ambush,
  And when my soul wakes, no longer cries out.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When every private estate serves thee well?
Grant me the pardon: my love well knows her virtue,
Her walls are heavily built with virtuous traits,
Yet, thou defiant, manipulative bore;
She, builder, bestows art's treasure on thee,
And thee, mere emissary, bringest poverty and rage.
Wilt thou not rather say, "Upon my love's heavy burden lies,"
To make my love more self-sufficient, allow
My needy subject to flourish, and myself proud?
  O, no, thou sly Deceiver; love knows it well
  To burden me


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
The thrift of the heart, which doth iniquity seem.
My reason, t' anticipate what will surely be,
Alternating between the two loves,
One love that doth love forbid,
The other, in hopes of the former's denial.
  Thus, in dreams, I watch while thou dost lie in my sight,
  And while my thoughts, in thy breast, entertain the dream.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which at such tyrants sightless keeps?
If my thoughts be windows, which see, yet cannot judge,
What worth the world, that deems it otherwise?
If not so fair, then love's craft,
Its workings veiled, as if not known,
Shall be profaned, and no praise given.
  Love's cunning scheme, with tears, my sight is barred,
  Lest piercing eyes should find what it sees.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
My own soul, which in thy breast doth lie?
Who turns from thee that I should call a friend?
On whom dost thou frown that I do adore?
If thou dost scowl on me, do I not smile at thee,
And in that frown tenderest thou my heart to stray?
What worth do I in myself discern
That I should hate thee when in myself I find
Some virtue in that thou dost hate me?
  O, love, remember I loved thee when I count
  As I do now, but that count is not so.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And to trample under foot my own dear name?
Wilt thou, O tyranny, claim I am blind
To view the truth as thou dost thine own part?
Since when hath my seeing been struck off,
By thee, my sense is barred?
O, no, 'tis true: yet this cunning trick
In my love's weakness hath it so got
That I almost believe thy falsehoods,
Though I should know it were false, yet acknowledge it was thy.
  For thee, I am bound to thee, and
  To turn from thee is to love me anew.


6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler nature to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may claim
Victory in love; the flesh, no longer tame,
Rises at your mention and proclaims
You as its prize, its conquest and its aim.
  And thus, in pride, it serves you, ever near,
  To stand by you, to fall with you, my dear.


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
So I may know thou no more alive.
But take heed, dear heart, of this treacherous allure;
A fleeting shadow may stain thy everlasting fair,
And when the stain is washed away,
The pearl of love's worth in tatters.
So should the time come when I behold thee clad
In vibrant morning light,
After sunset thou no more shalt live;
  I have sworn thee true, love, once sworn,
  To die for my love, so deep.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Came dancing by; one maiden fair, so fair,
Lifted the fire, which countless true hearts had fed,
The monarch of desire, she, in sleep, did quell,
By her innocent touch, the fiery glow,
Of his passion, by cool well water drenched,
Expelled the heat, and gained a healing potion,
A cure for hearts by Love's own fire afflicted,
Yet I, the captive of my lady's devotion,
Came to this well, in hope of my own healing,
  Discovering that Love's fire warms the waters,



10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though not of your sacred secrets.
But you, whose stars I follow,
Whose worth I cannot thy fair estimation enshrine,
And in what I value, I see a multitude of flaws;
Some praise her virtue, others her grace,
Some praise her beauty, yet acknowledge thee false;
And in these superficial allusions, love's fallow ground
Grows deep, where verity is wanting.
  For we who love understand not what is meant,
  But only what is hidden in thee.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And in him my sickness is hidden;
But as the spring thorns are wont to gather
At the foot of lofty mountaintops,
My sick love, which should bear his disgrace,
Hath buried itself in thee, its sweet plight.
The gentlest breeze of love alights
Upon my sorrow's naked breast,
Offending night and day with his sweet sigh,
Transforming frailties to virtues quite new.
  And yet, so great is love's inconstant rage,
  That he will not e'er outlive my darkest hour.
