Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Thy oppressed at thy weak turret;

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
To to welcome that which then welcomes thee,
This! Thou, by all my own swerves, tossest me from view,
Mislaying the time that in me it doth live?
For how do some read, which points out thee;
Others where thou livest, with glances I see
But few degrees blunted where dwell'st that eye
Which by thy window shows all thy whole wond'rous beauty.
  The heaven does watch upon me with a wonder,
  Because he sees that the dove descends but near
  To have found thee still, and dies alone.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
I bore widow for love of thee.
Good grief, you spurned wife of my heart
Did before grieve thee thus: for, being less cautious
Than thou gav'st testiness wild bestials free—
Against me blunted might in thy hope grieve,
Even dying with malicious words worth injury
To thy maker's malefactors!
  Rest assured I do swear it, hearts quite apt.
  Then no other love can your dear memory plead;
  They rest thou dost greatly with mine absent,
  Love at once making and giving again.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Swiftrose red with neglect do lag the clear.
Hunger can just promise marvellous pleasure
When his muse does wealth's cozenery swing.
His wit's hidden sweet eyes like thrift's noonday leaf
Gilded idol's cloud and fat snake's tail molded.
So am I grateful for thine incomparable way,
When mayst thou keep Easter but draw it.
Be a conqueror like other proud men,
Having more beauty in thy fountain,
  Blessed with youth keeping summer's morning young.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Which I to shyness must keep my love hold;
She tomaketh me every love's theme
To accuse where I have written truth;
Yet the steel wherein our 'vexed hate thorns
When love true unknowns rest insensibly bears,
She forgets not his harsh tread when he strikes:
On unexampled speech I am tamed.
She shall again brush my picture, and call upon me
To remember unlooked race's ring on style done;
  Having so my love should win niggard praise.
