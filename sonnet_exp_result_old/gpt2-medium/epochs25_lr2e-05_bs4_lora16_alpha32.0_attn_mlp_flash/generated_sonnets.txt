Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And these for beauty's temple
Dost state my finery under their strains.
So save your suckling soul become old
Whether my tinier cheeks be perceived,
Or fresh and burrest edges looked after.
I have been satiated, and after in again,
When in love at court my compositions ranked;
Whereon wax, heart or soul, I grew tasteless,
Bewiling my servitude in you.
  Our wit's tale lies, 'tis the character of lovers,
  And happily in need plays glad victory all day long.

Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
That with this wasting creature my bowels delight,
For bitter powder cast where thy meek pleasure reigns.
So, with how much all as poison am I sensitive,
With every heart I think loaded with interest,
Even that same love which lives upon thee,
From mouth thou great teacher to thy dead pupils.
  For, being all again, whether first or again,
  Through lack of thy shadow too slovenly doth hang.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
Yet confession at the gate pleads the same cause;

And mean to hate comforts in thy part;

That my own good to thee best must at cause.

9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
With loving behalf withstand against vile venom;
Thus many precious love charms become true friends
Humbly's lovely hour before they outstrip;
Hierachy love, slow of quick, with gravity seizeth
That vantage on time which yielding must give;
Such darts so strained to mastery
Render those compliments lasting with many a world;
  Their bubbles for moss done and kisses's dry made
  Are all twigs shaken alive to taste in times brave.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And yet truth singeth in honey.
Thus must the calling bright hades bear,
Even the jealousy of thy age lay,
Whilst she might muse while she myself blest
Thy strong motherhood and beauty imputed;
And then, for frequent moans, she partaketh
Though love by delay make a longer bond,
As when jesters hall believe and hate each other.
  And none but the sister aged should confess
  All joys that in peals of heart she heard.
