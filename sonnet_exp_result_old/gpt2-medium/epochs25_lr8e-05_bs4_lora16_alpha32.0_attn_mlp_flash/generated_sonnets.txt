Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
That thy wild grief abroad th' own shame embrace?
There is love, and love seeks place in thee,
May, I say, she in pride provide.
For if many waters miscarry she refines thee,
These too that press on matter bear on thee.
Thus, in motion that brings thee from time to time,
Each thread finds reason where his ancestors lie.
  He whose use is great by nature hunts at thy goal,
  Feeding on a thousand injuries of thyself.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
That is the sweet promise of long-cherished use.
Inveigled love, hovered on that which it refuseth,
Steals a little of that which it gains,
And in its soil sets a terminus rich:
The common source for wretched sedation is waste,
And loathsome sickness, poor and sick child of shame,
What's waste must from hence grow rich and painful.
  From this sorrow I devise store of flowers and lusty muse,
  Which I most justify in recompense, knowing fear of losing
  My object in disease.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Faced with no acquaintance foreign,
Made the stranger by misunderstanding you.
She has nothing need but admiration and hates no predicate,
Making nothing ne'er said of any worth,
Whereas she both inspires pride and prejudice,
Slander and praise alike. This shrewder beauteousness
In my praise makes my love even worse,
Whereas for her being dumb she is better served
By an actual truth rather than by idle jeers;
  And who better companion could she have, if not me,
  On account of my love to such an infidel as is.
