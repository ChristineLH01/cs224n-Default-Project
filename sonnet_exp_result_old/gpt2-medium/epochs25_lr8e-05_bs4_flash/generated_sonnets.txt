Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
They choosed out the finest of breath
And sealed it with a kind of seal that there
Existed in the stranger's hand, as if by some instinct
The inferior part of me to master.
The first impression that hand made
Upon my mallet's edge was such,
That I did not dare to thrust my lips there,
But held them back with my hand;
Thus, perfected in that moment,
I did not further anger
My Maker, but kept her company
  To work the wood that she would not break.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When every private may do as he pleases?
Grant, if thou wilt, thou art beloved of many,
But that thou none lov'st is most evident;
For thy beauty's honor is thine,
By thy loving heart, thy sweet favor spread.
Then do thy worst to thine own self,
And best of all, thy love to others.
  Yet, love, persist in evil, for I see
  The truth: you love those whom I despise.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And leaves behind but the semblance of health.
The cure is this, my love's prolonged fever,
That doth loosen the grip of the fit,
Guiding love towards the purest cert.
In myself, this fever is eased,
By striving towards that which is best,
That is, to the true, which doth therein lie.
  Thus, I return, refreshed and am as I was,
  In love, the fever doth keep me from death.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Since everything in me is clouded and dyed?
O, let me be obsequious in my look,
Though outward I resemble a mournful god;
In my heart, no looks so gracious,
As that of thee, which blinds even my sight.
If I could write the heaven's face,
The clouds would hide your beauty, and heaven's name
From my tongue, which would be too vulgar.
  But since my eyes are such, and love such,
  In every blessed shape, there is your image displayed.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And play the fool, to spite my dear mind!
O, take heed, dear heart, of this seductive mask
That doth deceit in beauty make beautiful still;
It is the gallantry of base pride,
Which on thy part doth lend light,
And puts glory in thy rotten dream.
  Beware, sweet heart, of this grand disguise;
  A sharpened sword, when blunted, shall still rise.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
And brave the tyrant Time to make right
The wrong done by his unyielding hand.
So you, my lovely sleep-walker, must wait
While those fair breasts, so desired,
At length become the dwelling of one;
Then to your side will come another thief,
More fit for his part, being your own,
Than having sleep with your sweet self removed.
  So shall the rightful thief be, staying near,
  To steal your heart, which yet lives and fears no harm.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though not acquainted with truth, yet admiring;
And although she knows my truest love lies,
I perceive her pretense is strengthened,
Though she knows it is false, yet I believe her,
For she loves those whom she thinks are false.
But why does she not admit her wrong?
And why do I not confess my age?
For love is more precious than all gold,
And aged in love dislikes the telling of years.
  So, in our errors, we find solace in lies,
  And so, we soothe ourselves with false caresses.
