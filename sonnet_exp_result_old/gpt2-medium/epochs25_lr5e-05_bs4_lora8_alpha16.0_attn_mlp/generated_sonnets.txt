Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Every mans poet worked
To illustrate nature's being in love and hers in woe
With lines so bounteous and skillful,
Made of flowers so free from wrong.
With Venus's often boastful clay
My longed lips knew, gladdened my heart;
For, beloved mistress, magnificence wins,
And tongues have their pictures droll.
  They shade not their meaning from that ill ground,
  But are virtuous to return to thee, with their boughs
  To breathe glad words in parting.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Eternal triumphing on earth, true Majesty's castle?
Chilly if growing warmer winter have bred
The tender tree thin with blood, stiff wit of veins,
With snowfalls freeze, or ice with moose's ferocity,
Having a large fat head, above breast of matter,
Therefore thou greet'st cool cold where thou shal'st brag.
Behold, what words at steeds' banks, haunts of paree!
Their seas with oars open opposite thee,
Or thin leaves with strings outcloth are fast
To glad moors' poor yields,


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
That still by continual turn remains old.
So thy wert and life to love make once dead,
Those chief images unmentioned, thy age wasted.
Seldom thou aid art my praise nor aid
Or solace in all time falseness,
And, thus dreading doing again, seldom do I write.
For this reason my painters ceaselessly paint
The skins of lamentable hearts when woes cost their dye.
  Their slow and void countenance if all hearts paint,
  Then thee praise does in truth exceed.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That tho' bright does not change with night?
Are those jewels that I recall blessed,
At odds as the beat of heaven,
Or gems that see no wear but in time's mood?
A queen such as they, with thy good husband's eye,
Was nigh become a winter slave.
O be so rash! if many privy eyes follow,
O, poor tho' beauty look'st starly with queen.
  Come, indulge me at mine inquiry,
  She, thy rose-bush, in fall.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thy strength
Whilst raining down rain upon me,
Coulting if thou dost help leave me to live?
Hath all so, so cogitates even in my half
Against sinner, and swearers though true art
Against punishing,
The ruins but paintings fair which thou best visible;
Save such as thou saw'st wrought and spent,
In that hide decent attend, exhibiting, more lovely array
Than thy fair best to exhibit were taken.
  Or do I make such rapine of thee,
  Though lucky life with children ever more dismal hours.

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest no token of love any neglect ought.
No serious man drives too quick a lover's sleight
Upon that sure safeguard which every sound gives
And that stalked fragrance and teeming eye such.
I desire, my true love, tallies such love that creeping
Lives within the foot of every quick-paced fortune.
What wert thou that's young and saucy brought up?
Then come not near my breast, nor nip my rents
Before diligence offures perfect judgment.
  Then sweet was thy transgressor yet to hew--

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,

In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn;
But of love thou gav'st much pride a mistake,

Given, "hoping thou dost reverence it not;"
It is my excuse therefore that thou devuest scorn,

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
While his hand was under the young queen,
Driving himself to attempt her, forthwith
Cheered against him been; and her eyes
Then lost his change till it grew on him again.
Then rude and thoughtful feeling moved,
Tempted both to feign to begin,
But still rage that child was roused
Which now seeks hate more nobly than affrighted love.
  These facts deserve gentle metaphors,
  That thoughts, inspired other lead by hatred.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Brewing unrestous waste to unwaste,
Beat his breast-cleaning and morbid scope,
Serving his soul with ravening knife,
Till day is his crown & new flowers those of age
May shall everywhere triumph;
Whilst blissful heaven yawns still till death ne'er eve;
Then might the sleep of his youth quicken again.
  Of ills till then thou wert thy maid,
  Like love well bred doth scarce need avail.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Which durst not fairly go forth about
When all the happy assembled noble dies,
Blacking about tender ne'er trod, thus making two lie
For I do swear, for the constant force keeps
Resistance what he feared in women's privy places.
There grieves my penitent lamentations past happy old days;
No sign of any weeping perspire,
And event thou but intent writing doth not time cease.
  Peace and sweetness allow that melancholy truth
  On lonely watches keep no dead peace low.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Whose praise grows enhanced by feigned self-shouldering,
Whereas love has he constructed;
Being molded to surpass time's end,
Like incipient spring let this head grow:
From age I scent the direction which spring tend
To bear poor stale tomb, instead of welcome flower.
  I need no pencil to construe thy renown;
  In writing on my rectitude, I shall give thee strong intelligence.
  Sweet mine argu'd officer conquers liberty's crown,
  Casting my love from the skin into Rome.
