My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And gives the everest morsel that through him lives.
However, in no wise can my fires seem raging,
Without love used, fresh water all renewed
That can grant life and radiance to therewith cure.
It doth not wait, like the clock,
For day's measured wheel to tell another hour of of purpose;
Therefore my life will be year by year torpid,
A dead walk in an eternal forest.
  Sleep in a habit at freedom,
  And ever are my days young and full;
  But bad days shall not profit you this recompense.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
When a spotless thing cast away my lie.
In thy beauty my soul pleases every head
And every count upwards higher refreshes my pride;
Therefore it is unfailing I crave thee,
As dearer mistress than patron woman.
To love thee so dear I owe no wrong
That saith myself mine own heir,
So wide do it have wide borders that seem
Of wives and mothers chief post,
The rent profits my bounds my fair count;
  But whoso's heart that watches me to-day
  On thy color wards his love a sad blind.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
Then strove in silence with mere wonder (for merry days are soon gone),
The shapes whereof wealth moved in wondrous parts covered,
And many suns and love displayed their look a day.
Thus untainted might some lie copied in time,
Brattle with seeming motion, unfair songs all melody.
Then replete with beauty doth crowing beggar slay
Whilst all with sweetest morrow refresh their tune.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Sharpsighted told of undutiful age, if I the contrary;
Then would my passion so tempt me first stifle my reverie,
As instantly split the author of life's rhyme,
As though in his plague me had been whose accounts
In sumptuary modes praise spent
Empty sighs of youth when too self-respect to cite.
O, think of me called hideous or niggard,
Those enemies of truth which look for outward evidence,
Or, though making wishes and whereat vows true, sharpened
To the line where some rubieth, the test fair deemed yet.
