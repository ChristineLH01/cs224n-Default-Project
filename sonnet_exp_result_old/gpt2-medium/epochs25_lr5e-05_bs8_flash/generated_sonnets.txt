Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Their soft touches, I took to heart,
As contagions to my heart's own nest.
Might I have saved my soul, I pray,
If not for those lips that Love's own hand had graced?
Were I to fall into despair, I'd turn around,
And find love again, in sluttish haste,
 Lest my heart should be full of hate,
And all my dreams of thee, swiftly to dismiss.
  Yet, fearing none but thy pure heart's embrace,
  I'll bear that burden, even if it brings pain and shame.


1
Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Being filled with such forces, why dost thou defy
The counsel of thy own vital self,
Rebellious, impetuous, and full of woe?
Grant me the strength, love, to face thy scorn,
And grant me the fortitude, to endure
The march of time, bearing down on me,
The grinding of this bitterest stone,
An ever-present reminder of thy disdain.
  Grant me the strength, to face thy scorn,
  And then to heal thy wound, I'll graft new life.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
The very thing that wards off the very thing.
My love is so troubled with this that I
Cannot think nor say a word to dispel
The dread which every heart in me doth feel,
Neglecting thee who doth soothe my heart's deep
With all his might, healing all ill thoughts.
  For if my love were cleansed by thy gift,
  The sickness would fade and die away.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which better can love give than reflection's shop?
If not better, then not to love,
For then nothing love can do better,
And nothing clear argument against robs
To make acquaintance with heaven's voice.
O me, dear love, what can be my ball
Which on your love no wrinkles doth ensue,
Shall beauty's shadow hold out against the storm,
Or fain the hapless thing hold out against the rain?
  Love knows no beauty bettered whose gauge is better.
  None so fair whom heaven hath endowed with thee.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
Myself, and to thy service bound do take
My part of thy society to bring?
Whate'er thy pleasure be, thy will or my will be,
That I am by thee bound to that
Which thou dost compel me to give.
O, what a wretched subject am I to be,
When all my thoughts are in one place
And all my strength is by thy aid!
Then thank me when I forget thee, being so near,
For I then begin again, nothing so poor as this,
And that by thy help is made better still.

5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
To frame my love's face against the sun,
A tyrant over myself, proud and vain?
Who hath taught thee to ensnare my heart
Within the thrall of thy false sight,
To cherish more the more I see amiss?
Though I should flee, turn from thee,
My heart still yearns, with love's decree,
To stand by thee, to fall by thy side,
  And suffer thee to take control of my heart.


6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
As you do, you betray me, for I
Spare you love, for I love you so;
And, in losing me, you both gain
My deepest love, which I cannot force to rehearse.
So, cease this torment; I have found
A refuge in your sweet worth,
Which, when needed, will lift me from my place.
  Seek what is best, that best I wish for thee?
  That is, your love, my foundation, fire.


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
As bewailed betwixt thy sweet heart and head.
But why thy vows should prove no straying,
Spiteful eyes nor minded heart couldst ever suspect,
For roving eyes and gaping hate didst intrust,
A prey to every vice, wild and straying mad.
O, cruel be thy trespass, for evermore mine,
Whate'er thy pleasure or crime thy heart doth indulge,
Thy flesh lovingly thy soul dost join,
And in this thou welfare'st confined,
Or, at the least, dead, as the dog.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Into a well nearby she plunged the brand,
Killing the maiden within the well.
The well was then filled with murky water,
A deadly poison bubbling nearby,
A cure for lovers' pains, only to waste,
But Cupid's love was not finished, for love drew
From his own essence and dyed his hand,
A lustre that lasted all night and buried him.
  So I, poisoned by my own deeds, do plague
  Another, more merciless, with a just spite.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Made as fast as they could the gift of gaze,
Gaze in love's eternal course, unburdened.
Some swift-winged dove, soaring in sweetest air,
Lifted the heavy olive from the sheaf,
That eternal flame by love's own cooling might
Could never be quenched, for love's own breath
Embellishes all precious things, fair as any chick's egg.
  Lo, in love's eternal course, all eyes are invited,
  Just as when the dove lands on the olive.


10
When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though not much better acquainted with truth lived.
But when she tells me she is adorned
With every truth in the world, from head to foot,
I cannot help but praise her,
Though I find not such as she thinks I know.
And for I perceive her in these thoughts,
I do maintain that she loves not true,
And even her telling lies to please me.
  For she knows I think not better of her,
  But only so as I with her approve.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And he from me loves that which thou dost disdain.
The gentle wish I held in those black orbs,
That e'er seemed mine to desire,
Now my poor love hath found out his due;
He, so unjustly deceived, is askew
At the very sight of me unkind.
  For thee, I painted this love double;
  For thee, this wish I to show, true.
