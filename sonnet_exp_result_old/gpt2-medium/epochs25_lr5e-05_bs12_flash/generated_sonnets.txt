Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Tears of anguish, grieved by love's own doing,
Could not the heart of love so kindly bestow,
Like a dove's sweet embrace, outshine the sun.

And bind up this sorrow with gloom,
Like mourners we bid before the storm.
  But if the spirit of love refines,

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When every private canker doth thrive
And thrives even in thy filth and mire?
Even so the serpent, as he his hours grow strong
And wriggles and shows his strength in his decay,
Do I, like thee, seek new life, grown old,
For worms, like thee, find no welcome there.
  Look to the record, fair dame;
  This bloody battle held true as long as Time did lend.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And being teeming with new life, I bewail the loss,
Craving that which hath been before,
Past life's counterfeit, purer, more to offer.
My love is such, an early bird in heaven's gate,
A former child of hope, now faithlessly gone,
Lost in thoughts of thee, beyond all compare.
  For thee, I travel with anxiety,
  In mind's thrall, locked in thought's throes.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which looks on falsehood, and marth it as it were alive?
If not so fair a child of theirs are you,
What son of theirs holdeth your favor dear,
That even his glass bears your face's imprint?
O, let me see your face, and I'll tell you my story--
It's the work of eyes, no skill can it read,
But love's perspective it bestows, which grosseth the picture.
  In youth my dear eyes were twain, clear as any eyes in heaven;
  But heaven for love ruined 'twixt them and you.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest guilt of love engrafted on my part show.
For, as you fondly recount my misdeed,
I betray thee, myself, in my treachery.
Ponder then if my guilt should ever bring
Such a toll that my soul would turn from life?
O, let me confess, even now,
That love is too young to know what guilt is;
Yet who knows not love is born of love.
Then, gentle lover, urge not my omission
To bear the weight of love's accusation,
Lest I should absolve thy guilt, justly seen.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
As clerk vows false deeds, alter oaths new,
And steal testament to date.
Against thy double innocence I did lie,
And found reason to suspect second time,
Despite evident lie, suspecting of love;
But heaven alone exempts me
From such double mistrust, capricious and capricious.
  To thy high virtue dare I bring my complaint,
  Not to thy heavenly sight, but to thy deserts.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unschooled in life's deceitful art.
Yet, love's cunning scheme does keep
My silence with solemnity, though I know it's
A falsehood, sweet Lucrezia, born of spite.
For she loves not truth, that she may strive
To make me seem less good than I am,
And that she may have me believe she knows
The world conceals love's true worth.
  And yet, she loves those she best knows,
  For true love thrives in quietness and fear.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And 'gainst him I often fell in love with thought;
So one day his sweet grace taught me how
To move swiftly from troubled state to cheerful ease,
Whilst on other days my melancholy fells
In care and laid waste of both body and mind.
For when that fiery cross
Which daily offers trial to my love,
Or curious wanders into my sweet thoughts
Where once was content, now far from grounded am
Deserves my dearest joy to share his parts.
  So then my blessed little help is in distress,
  Where other love's spirits sicken and die.
