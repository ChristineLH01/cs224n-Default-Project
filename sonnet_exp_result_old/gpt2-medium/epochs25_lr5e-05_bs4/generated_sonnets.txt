Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Though my unlistening tongue did state
That love had made "I spurn," yet "I do choose"
Made that appeal by kindling desire.
"I spurn" meant "I will not," and "I love"
Made that appeal by kindling desire;
But neither tongue nor thought could so much as utter
That hurt to me, being mute, dear.
  Since each did speak thus, I thought it best
  To let their words speak for themselves.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While thy outer shell shines with such brilliance?
Waste thy fury on this fading edifice,
A monument destined to turn to dust,
A bard's worst work, auteous waste,
Too dated, dulled by decay,
And unworthy of thy living star.
Take pity, friend, and wish me well,
As I swallow the remedy for my pain,
No matter how harsh the flavor may seem.
  Pity me, then, and know this verity:
  Your pity's power can heal my injury.


2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
My love's strange policy it seems to me:
Whence dost thou derive this wicked might,
To make me love that which doth not belong,
And force my dearest to be my own style,
Whilst robbing thee of what thou dost confound?
O, blame me not if I no more live!
Thou benefit'st myself much more than I,
Being at one with that which 'gainst me lies.
  For that thou teachest how to make me love
  The things against which I am strongly bent.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Since in love's own conceit all distinctions lie?
O, be silent, dear heart, and I will tell you
That eyes are windows, and love's face hath
Some resemblance to the evening glory;
But that in itself is not so,
Unless it be by your help, which I intend.
For my part, being prevailed upon to speak,
Of thee, dear love, is all my despair.
  For my sake alone is my despair come,
  And that is this, and this with thee.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you lead me astray, I forsake
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul whispers to my flesh that it may soar
In love; the flesh, no longer a bore,
Rises at your name, its power to explore,
A triumphant trophy, yours to adore.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, always near,
  To rise and fall, for your love, my dear.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
As was sworn before thy face, unguarded.
But why should I blush to see thee in a dream,
When I am attainted of all my transgressions?
For I have sworn by thine unseen breast,
By thy love, thy truth, thy faithfulness so rare;
Yet in my madness, I forgot this oath,
And deep-rooted in my sin, I gave thee false sight.
  For I have sworn thee fair, false heart, in lies,
  To swear against my deeds, before thine eyes.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep;
Dian chanced upon the sight, and plunged into a well,
Awakening Love, from which it had been blind.
The waters kept Love's blood at bay, but water did wake,
A cure for Love's pain, though it came from afar.
But what saved Love from this ultimate woe was Love's own making,
A skill that could not be bound by Time's swift decree.
  In combing through the wounds of his lover's death,
  I found the fountain of Love's release.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
And for my sake believe her false tongue,
Though she knows full well my days are past their prime.
And yet, love, do you not defy my belief,
Sland'ring my sense with a preposterous argument,
A quest for strength in love's own hollow?
O, neither should she, nor I, if we must depart,
Stained by our love's own deep-rooted guilt.
  For we live in this union, and she in us lies,
  As lilies we scar with our fables, who heed not the scythe.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And he in me lies in decay,
Neglected by love's dearest remedy.
The nobler angel in me is slain,
A gentle reminder of love's bequest,
In him life's sweet legacy remains.
Divided, both from me, they bear this pain,
In each other's grasp, my heart they mourn,
For no clear reason thus they find:
  One, my love, by death hath claimed his share,
  The other, my heart, by love hath shed its light.
