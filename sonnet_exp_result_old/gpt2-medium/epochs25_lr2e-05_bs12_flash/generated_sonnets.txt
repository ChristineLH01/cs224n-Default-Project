Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Then she, changing the subject, found joy
To sing a sweeter melody than my unkind bite
And blessed hymns were more poor than my ampler lark
Despite the gracious warming of my tongue.
Then did Love rank among gentle women,
A woman fair by instinct due
Of kind to be; she skillfully fashions
Sensuality with aesthetic charm extreme,
Like honest art she stays—
Yet none might liken her lark the best;
  In me she had finesse; in her loveliness,
  True alchemy she wrought.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Brought disgrace and disgrace far and near,
And made his deadly art
Thy false image become the whole earth,
She who by him held captive his birth
Or tried to erase his kind, true self.
Herself, he struck in jealousy, killed,
A simile of himself, both fair and true,
And left her to weep and despise,
While all the world raised a moan to dance.
  Such was Love's madness, so great,
  That by Time and World he took flight as being lost,
  Only to come back full grown with strife still.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Or half-human shape, that sees better in clear light.
My guide loves her better when she speaks truth,
Though she knows that I am gullible,
And that I must trust her for advice;
But when I am stymied by shrewd turns, I abandon
My purpose, and trust her again,
For she has seen truth in me, and knows
My worth as such.
  I have sworn by this love, though I know it's untrue,
