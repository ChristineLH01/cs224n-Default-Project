Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Whose sweet love, fresh as a spring,
Shall outlive this sad death, outliving
The hundred lifetimes that tyrants brand.
Though they may falter, still, outlive
This cruel universe that keeps us in its grip.
O, what a gift, to share this love
That so deeply knows our deepest needs,
To cherish her, to keep her warm,
Despite the years, to stand as women.
  But what's so steadfast that fears no shift?

Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When such power dost thy duty impel?
Grant me the strength, to face thy defect,
The captive's solitude, in liberty's cell,
With forbearance, I'll endure, each ebb and swell,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And, sick of this state, doth resort to strange cures.
At times the taste of this feasting is chased,
By the thought of health, which nourishes the soul;
Yet, as the slave of body, he eats the ill,
And Death himself being made sick by this,
Rejects the true remedy, his friend's cure.
  Thus, in excess of sweet delight I droppeth,
  Iniquity second to ill.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
And what good or ill is it that sees?
If no man well can speak of thee,
Why, then love, be thy monument.
Lest my saucy eyes deceive with false vision,
Make my soul mourn in thy shade,
Sap from eye to eye, with sorrow painted.
  How much more blest would be my sight
  If my lovely thoughts could truly glow.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest guilt's dark shadow mar your visage.
Confess, if you are able, that you've tasted
The sweetest guilt, the sourest pleasure;
Let love's advocate raise her hand,
And I may absolve you of love's deceit.
Who's so blind that thinks only on his face?
Or who, so enraptured, allows
His charm to waver in the light of day?
  In either case, know this: Your love is strained, and it knows
  That strain gives its power to waver in the light.

In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
As I before have sworn confounded, thou forsworn

And double steal thy beauty;
  And in thy sweet crime I find buried
  A testament of double crime.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And him I love, as is his wont,
For him I fasten all my affections in goose;
And him I esteem as my dearest hope,
Whose good sense I seldom test and believe,
But doth my wrong, and hath me like to strive.
For him I follow, to the grave,
Where other ghosts of mine alter their state,
And him I partly believe, partly believe false,
That he me loves me so, and hath me depart.
  This love I do so, partly believing,
  Because of thee, my good man death.
