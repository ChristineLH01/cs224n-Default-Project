Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
In rotten skill and slow wound; thy pride is more wretched
Than that I am, poor dust, and I soil it,—
And yet highest false pomp swears thou thoughtest
Of blood which am no more than worthless, a guest
Of nothing mortal to me but, heat, and strangeness,
Gripped and sad cover from being real things, eyes unseen.
Such terror do I in love ever dread,
For hand can produce no more luck than fever does give
Whilst hair retains all that's lent, the son of his dear sleep,

2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Feeding upon his consuming with envy,
Each meate of his to divide.
At times, which I shamely by art to subvert;
Whence love's marrow is starved and loses life,
Till he brings his fiend about thence,
So that her in uppity is choked
Outward and absorbed a fool!
  He drew venereal sores from my body
  That wragged the greater-poured lily
  Upon their cheeks, before their flower faileth;
  Thun while beauty night by night makes me sick.


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Having nearly the art of observing opposites?
Ah, but blessed liberty! I love not to describe yet,
Miserable elegance, that doth deride behold.
As beauty has no impertinent state,
For near as it descries to perfection,
It decays and degenerates here, depreciating time.
Today another is after, tomorrow another.
  Time already has weary allotted, wishing yet my contrast;
  These trumpets divide the ignorance with knowledges.

But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn.
What believe thou said should take habit,

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep,
Accursed fallen pride trembling gave it.
In supper enchanted tender beached
There quick robbed eagle hung on this shore,
Lest prey should rob gilded dead mettle,
Because lovers of such need had pendled
Should prove thy diligence repaid by son and child,
Or refuse thy friendness by jealousy.
For this kind doth shady some fall
Against peace and love with love of its source,
And light ensues in darkness scorn of none;
  But nought of himself remains new, as then while
  Whate'er else dies tops with condition still wax.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Can weep for milk with poor old time's tab
After the damnation received for fraud;
Within this, to him belong so much holy power,
That sacred degree was holy bread, born young,
The fruit of such expiration's warfare.
No, hated to him so far as to give pain;
He never so cheap as to withhold any point:
Lady Calydon, on whom benefactor law that sold

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Time have given her borders that rest in degrees.
One has twenty chalk marks on his head,
Knowing him so truth, and yet again false--
Timid which never is taken, when wisdom
In other men's hands affords me heirs.
Others would groan, how fair their weapons are,
But bold olives for such were made--
Yet I drink not against dread to deal its shortage.
  My Muse is therefore swift to with love—
  To lament that truth which not my will,
  She cannot execute malice, though men do equal fear.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
That do like us bears fresh guests home,
And straight walks without a firm station
To look upon which such voices only give end.
So for myself in grief makes full retreat,
And wide wooing guest even ends all.
