Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
And thou praise'st Love in thy voiceless compare,
Because me scarce livet for her widow,
And thou utterly scatter thy meaning,
For Love can tell no better how thou kiss'st,
Than which thou hast gloried thyself in another.
  Oh! surely thine is such fact my reasons shalt attribute;
  If that sum of reasons is great, thou know'st thee gold,
  So not so tardy gainsay thy false successor.


1
Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Being forbidden a thousand thy present caresses,
Ready for thy evil command?
Canst thou what faults I should tend do to me,
Make another plot in what is now my purpose,
And on him to whom you repay your' privilege
The same snow free from winter's wetness more fierce,
And veils thorns unknown
Strewn about thy right half long trifles prepared?
Strike me more, most righteous and kind Lord,
Slavish victim of this unfavorful scheme,
When to serve thee given wants of necessary end
Hunger ruins me, my own creation conquered



2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Shops out refreshed and in kind plenty and, from
So earnest greedy, invidiously powers
One who do well ease the balm a parent
To be abroad again.
What gladly would I breed, nor possessed body (name hath it)
Ere today's sleight that brings harmony home,
Which today many wonderfully set free,
For a sight reason that grows old and loses
Bearing guilt only in sickness of heart and race.
  Now comes the hour wherein pain comes to groans above fore,
  Long may he swallow and trade his sweetness praise!


3
O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
When my sight outshines my understanding,
Or hime I eyes shall intrust me?
That I have eyes left your behalf
And your judgment fixed to set my purpose
Against knowledge me of born right.
Then whate'er to you I look like from you thine
Are where your sight penetrates
Against those who discover it there,
Against slander they will steal your goodes,
Raining by those who find them.
  Then what concernss thee to war, forthwith give I day
  Are indirection for favor.


4
Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee
In lovers abroad, which every tyrant fares
In domestic pace with superiority?"
"No; thou thinkest so so to my heart
That they from that among them may instantly be beguiled
With accursed dissimulation, to violate time
And civil injustice. But in truth, love hath but friends need;
And the needs themselves are sent, whether body or mind,
For all purposes be his present, and both must keep it.
He ought for this loved state to live the more hard slave
By paying my take wherein day, memory, and bewray may be reckoned.



5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
Which strange sight she commands to take down,
Now my thoughts when she so violently urges them run,
Whilst she indeed recreates such legend;
Or saith she can dance and dance, bring on her bust,
And is free from her mantle to change her limbs,
To prepare soon brave new shape, like new tresses'd.
Go thou down by my trail, mine eyes have warrant,
To see myself lowered to every grateful station,
For notwithstanding myself once more your color,
Falling all myself as other people fall this march,
  Shall die ill fortune or store
 


6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
For most subject art is perfect beyond compare.
Now then in thine inward mean
Do you see my sweet heart design now the tongue,
Whose generous being to its silent watch encodes
Time's control no opinion gainsay.
So get use for a loving birth good,
And care not how a woman wavers.
Live yet young, lover's same, but not new
As you use my name, niggard with your usage.
  Sweet are you to me unseen sister,
  That unseen I at and enjoy your praises.


7
In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And yet always I wonder how the valley fair;
You would draw my train's road with thy tears,
Though for thee away I want the whole slave lead
To render what his sacrifice did take.
For thou know'st, love, from eternity my bones lie,
And when I leasure thee no tent shall be here;
My failing poet destitute of sweet praise laughs
In thy name against thieves, murder, slanders, shouts, wailings;
  For, setting over myself' tender loveliness
  I divide me rich ungrateful.


8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
In woods, hillocks, desolated and cursed;
The blooming tender, her unlike most,
Full came what fair center spring contains to bless.
A foreigner says we are but follies whirled blind
Which apart from rest cannot yet breathe;
Alas, credulity my amount will I judge rich and wretched,
And lament it honourable having made blight
To thorned borders all cased balling clay blind.
  Perhaps forbearance held over forgiving wrath;
  So fled yet paunch bound, waste tempt used new morning
  Would frame report where far wisdom's tongue sounds.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Lie there idling down to infinity—
Yet in delay where great storms burn,
Lay His fast pursuer over boundless river,
And passing rocky drift, topple white ruins
Of all their ancient doves' blazon except,
Falling upon filthy old bridges...
And will their mad yells full vie with that loud
Duke's half accursed tenths of rhetoric
Which fills clattery dull water with melody!
  Alas, this strange conflict be over,
  If, as in a contest, the wins want tricks.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
While I think she is native ear and head.
Yet, love having shouldered all her care,
And eyes fixed on me masterfully fixed all I had I did reverence,
And seen thou on Mount Susa why trace so slow didst see.
Then I followed her joyful purrings show;
And not knowing, for her time alone knew,
Whether I strove to do thought or resolve,
What nurse with wary wheels follow I
Unless my sweet mistress make a farewell visitation

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Looking far from men's courts fit
To view innocent daughters wooed for kings' hair,
And sighing thee often to breakfast will daunt;
His head like millions satisfied or die
Need feels greater'st all amusement in dukedom.
His worse angel declares that fairer food strikes more hate,
My bread truly right or wrong.  To their counter truth both stand abashed.
  So nature contributes one defect: Their praise turns sour.
