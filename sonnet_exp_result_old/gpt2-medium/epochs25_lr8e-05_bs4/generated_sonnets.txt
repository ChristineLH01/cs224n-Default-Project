Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When thyself the bestower of all good?
Why bestow such riches on this sad dwelling,
A structure destined to decay and crumble?
Shall the heirs of this vice,
Consume thy legacy, thy very substance?
Then, soul, feed on the anguish of thy servant,
Let them fade, while thou live, for they too must yield,
Unless this universal corruption blaze:
  All lives are Wound, and thus they die,
  In efface of this thought, they lose their way.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And showing the most extreme form of its wit.
Thence comes it that my love is almost dead,
As the Milan of love doth almost cease,
From that which it doth nourish in the warm house.

How far my love hath I to travel,
Finding my bed in the midst of strangers' moan,
  And in their laughter, see the simplest love.
  This is the humor which my love so well doth thrive.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Which looks on things not seen?
For whether beauty, birth, or wealth, or wit,
Or any of these all, or all, or more,
Entitled in thy parts do crowned sit,
Drained of all these, and more by thee spent?
O, none, unless this miracle have might,
That in my love I may still see thee,
Despite of thy shadow, which doth daily appear.
  This miracle none can verily proves,
  Save that in my love thou gav'st me all.

With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
And find favor in thy false heart's design?
Wretched in thy vices, what hope have I but despair?

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entwined in my errors.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler nature to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may claim
Victory in love; the flesh, no longer tame,
Rises at your mention and proclaims
You as its prize, its conquest and its aim.
  And thus, in pride, it serves you, ever near,
  To stand by you, to fall with you, my dear.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And I forsworn thee for falsehood, new lease of thy heart.
But why should I trust thy new-found grace,
When I know thy deepest secrets lie?
My vows but hold force, and effectual stay;
Against myself I can annul,
By my being bound to thee, and to my dear.
  For I am as thou dost persuade me,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
Thence arose a tale of love and flattery,
That Love, being wooed of hearts, set sail for earth,
And caught the coward guard of a ship.
Thence arose a tale of envy and spite,
That Love, being wooed of eyes, set sail for thee,
And bit the commander of the gales, so fierce.
And from these comments arose this song:
"Had Love been gifted years, his might seemed frail,
But now, thanks to Time's gift, his heart is growing strong,
And eyes, his shield, now his battering ram."

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
And filled royal tombs with their lovely stuff,
With gentle art and perfect stealth,
Made beautiful graves where love's sweet tomb
Resembled a queen's pride.
So all those holy, unsuspecting slumbers,
By kings' gentle sleepers, got a rude awakening,
And found their hearts marred, and love torn,
At the hands of this eternal fire!
  Lo, in that hour, my love first fell,
  And thy sweet, sacred fire did start.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though not acquainted with all the arts of the dance,
And even then, not well acquainted with the crow,
To speak of heaven's second self, though I knew it before
And could not help it, I love to think it so.
But, love, be most assured of this,
My love is as the morning mist,
Though she loves to change her appearance,
And that, my mistress' seeing, is her change.
  And, so far as I am from her, I think no ill,
  Because she loves to be false.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And in his sweet worth I find a muse.
The lesser angel is my muse's gentle guest,
And in his sweet thoughts I find sweet delight.
So, thou fair-haired muse; my lesser vice,
Thy guest is fair, and in him my love finds sweet release.
Let them live, though shadows like to thee tend,
Whilst I, the lesser, remain and ponder thee.
  Or call them by thy name, and in that spirit do abide,
  Two souls, one love, in one embrace.
