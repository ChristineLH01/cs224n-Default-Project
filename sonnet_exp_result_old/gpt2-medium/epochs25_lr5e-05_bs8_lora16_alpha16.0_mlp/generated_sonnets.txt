Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
"Death be the recompense of thy fleeting sighs
Like life's tenderest foality paid with hard wages.
  What love doth she am worthy to hold up
  Unto a burning gall for wait for thy rock?"
  Thy utmost words shall deign to stay alive
  With the voice that, if not changed, bereaveth time
  Of saying that love now's not warmer, and sing not ceaselessly
  Nor desires might either continue very tame,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Champing his appetite whose weakness thou view'st
From far way off, by the contrary possessing
The means of winning colds that combatent heat eat
Unto health by hourly reduce;
And--if their relationship are adverse
That high has it who seeks to sojudge
Against thy caution but takes thee not
I ceaseless endeavor on my stay
To ice with a love blot, leaving daylight gone.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
And sees in rough parts learned face lacking not?
O that my mind could find grace instead!
What me would I trade my will with beauty?
To choose that, for the sight with which she's gay,
Does could sustain no immortality.
  Or to desire the laud wherewithore I conspire
  In wax some dense awful work of my thinking doth paper soar,
   Believing that in true day time there's not sight so fat and wax soft,

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot thee,
Sons of Heaven I loved in the heat of my life,
Wherefore also do mortals trod from me from years' begun?
But speak thou at least for a beginning, lovely!
Honour me not with praise, whatever the world fillth up
To your weakness, but take them some due away,
For that in a righteous sin could exist a better right
Then could I not thrive whilst up rich on their love's end,
Which ought would teach thee and mean learn thy worth.
This I cannot pay in wasting space or elsewhere,
Therefore have sometimes decided for something.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Like swash-rims to drow on foul ground,
So giving my love the grade of love fair;
'Twill serve to make man's mystery glorious;
I prefer flesh and I howl like a lion.
And darkness bids me not speak but fear forbidden.
Expose thine honest heart to music,
And its sound shall none durst read.   O bring the world's realities brief
Through infinite number to an one and simple keep;
  There spend the portends, and there swallow   What traces your pain in your tomb.
