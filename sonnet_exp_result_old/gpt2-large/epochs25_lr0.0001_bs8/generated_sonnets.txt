Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Those lips that told me "I forgive"
To lips that craved to be lips,
Savoring fresh passion from their sour vade.
So I, converted, turn away,
Unmoved, fearing their unyielding tongue,
Will ransom my heart with ransom borrowed;
In remonstration, I pay the debt,
And save my soul with love reborn.
  But if they bite, I've lost my honor,
  And love hath paid the debt in kind.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While my bare requirement is met with thee?
O, that heart, which nature gave birth to conceive,
To dwell within thy breast's lowliest root,
Would burn with a passion to cope thy defects,
And fail to drop dead where they should live!
Thou art tempted too much; shouldst strive
To settle the score with thyself alone.
Let not thy heart be graced with trends
That it knows not yet to belong to thee.
  Make thee another self for love of me,
  That beauty still may live in thine or thee.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
And that which by therapies is at length
Revealed by sincere physicians' reports.
Yet, my love, thou art near death,
And, like a willing patient, thou goest
To the very brink of loathe,
How much more blessed if, like a swimming pig,
Thou downstream doth live thy life to advert.
  If this be false, and I am proved
  By physicians' reports, there can be no blame.

Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my sins.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though she knows full well I am vulgar with Fortune's manners.
But why does she not admit her wrong?
And why do I not confess my age?
For love's sweetest habit is to feign trust,
And age in love abhors the telling of years.
So we deceive one another in our flaws,
And with sweet lies, we comfort our disgrace.
  So we laugh and die, and hope our days are done,
  In love's sweet deceit, we keep ourselves warm.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
His face with love etched is as white as marble;
But my tormentor's face is ember yellow,
With torches of discord blazed, it gleams.
My sinister muse does lure my virtuous guard,
And seeks to taint his essence, pure and bright.
She whispers softly, sowing discord,
Enticing him with her twisted, warped delight.
I ponder if my virtuous one may fall,
Seduced by shadows, losing light's embrace.
  Alas, he may not, but I fear the worst.
  Fearful contemplation! What prospect sees me thus?
