Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
To me she was but second husband,
A friend, a mother, a delight.
Yet when my beloved moved on,
Her parting was something bitter,

With false romance the love I most killed.

Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Thy grapes to the tyrant Barb, thy pride
Obscured by the wealth of thy State?

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
Whose images are in my seeing so Tweedled and gamed?
If any wonder at my feeling, let them inquire
Where my sense or heart's dictations lie;
No fear can love's eye provoke,
Unless it be biased and double-dealt.
Be thus advised, my love:, Hawks have their spots,
And birds their nests; so is love's glass,
Garbage left when birds are fermented.
  But wond'ring heaven, I wonder why
  My eyes are not of like brass or stone.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my sins.
For, as you deceive me, I betray
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may strive
For victory in love; the flesh, now alive,
Rises at your name, its purpose to derive
From your affection, its reason to thrive.
  And thus, in pride, it serves, ever so near,
  To stand by you, to fall with you, my dear.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And pledgement of strangers turned cold.
For to thy love I succumb, whom thou art not bound
In any constrained sense, staying firm
When I am by thee perjured;
And for my sake even thou art to be assailed
By that same thief whom thou at thy tables
Sworn to before his crime.
  But here's the joy: my love is true,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
But when I, his beloved, saw his eyes fell,
I took my leave, her benefit swiftly lost.
Thence came spring's invasion, blighted olive bark,
And thirsty earth, craving liquids, forlornly crawled.
Doubting the wretched earth could render
Her services, I ventured to warm her own,
But found, to my surprise, she did burn
Her brand, producing a bath.
  A cure for lovers' pains, I found,
  But water cannot make one love compare.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
His memory ever near, did take his part,
In solemn processes of love's eternal fire.
But when my sweetheart's eyes met mine,
The fire within them burned with a matchless flame,
And love's sweet exchange, so fearful, was done,
With a death that silenced the night.
  For I had slept in love's sweet guise,
  And now, in dreams, I was lost, listened to music.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unschooled in life's shared scams and arts.
But why does she not confess her wrongs?
And why do I not admit my age?
For love thrives on feigned trust and belief,
And the aged in love dislike revealed years.
So we deceive one another in our flaws,
And with sweet lies, we comfort our disgrace.
  And thus, in our faults, we find sweet solace,
  In lies, we hold each other's every part.
