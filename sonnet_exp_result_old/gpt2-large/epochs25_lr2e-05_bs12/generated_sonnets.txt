Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
She painted his face with gentle chrystalline
And set commersions on his visage fair.
He grew a beard, a change from his former state,
And lost his hair, for which 'twas formerly proud.
Then sorcerers found a potion to banish
And cure his beauty; but I saw a woe
Which afflicted him more than death's tax.
For of hues potions, some best were,
Some cures were worse than death's supposed cartel.
  Then was his beauty more and more admired,
  And love again had its habitation.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Their sacred vows, day by day, succumbed to temptation.

Came bouncing back to its buds, thus risking life and sight,
But the bud that opening came too late, burned up, and gave him shame;
The sincere love that instantly went out;
For love's true ardor runs deep, and in its place,
Humiliation and despair ensue, blunting the blow.
  Still, the rose did not lose its gentle stripe,
  For love's sweet power does find a way out.

That like two spirits do suggest me still.
