Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
To me she sold her self-worth for dirt,
Her beauty for worthless scorn,
Herself she sold for pauper's heaven.
Her feet, the pleasure she brought, they crushed
My joy, their heel turned down low;
In them, those divisive tongues laid
A scepter that whispered "I spurn."
To me, she exchanged her youth for her end,
Her bitter sweetness for false music,
An improved sou with added shame.
  In them, those bad thoughts mounted,
  Still undermining love, remorselessly.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Infatuated with falsehood, making lame haste.
But why should I believe her sweet words,
When I know well she is full of fouler plume?
Or should I believe her false appearance,
When I find in my beauty much beneath the book?
No, let her be deceived thus; thus, I swear,
She loves me, and none well knows it so well
As I, whose wild heart speaks so boldly.
  So, she is false, and I am true,
  Together, we falsify not truth's sweet art.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Whose allure almost brings me to tears.
My darker muse, on my foible mad,
Gives birth to fearful thoughts, like a fever.
Thy beautiful child, like a sick person,
Comes to my fancy, with a fleshly ruck,
That stings like an unslackened dove,
More keen than any knife thought to carve.
And for this sin there is no remedy,
My love's eye for my torment doth reign.
  Pity me, then, and know this truth:
  Your compassion is more than your hate.
