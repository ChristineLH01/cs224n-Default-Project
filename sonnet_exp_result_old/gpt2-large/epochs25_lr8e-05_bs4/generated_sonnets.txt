Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Adorning thy outer walls so gaudily?
Why lov'st thou such wealth, which thou receiv'st not from thyself,
Nor give it away, having no use,
But rather pour it on others' shoulders?
For if thou wouldst keep what thou receiv'st not given,
What use is there left but to inherit?
  But if thou use what thou ow'st, sin lingers alone,
  And ye of more deserve thine just title.

Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That it may possibly appear elsewhere?

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And his visage shadows like a mother goose.
The other love is more like a ghostly form,
Darker than the nightingale, more cold,
Stealing unseen from thy sweet self.
These verses I love, yet strangely they flow,
One love in sadness, another in light;
Yet, like prayers divine, they answer my need,
One love for thee, and another for myself.
  So, like tsundere children, I tie thee down,
  And one love, but two souls do confirm.
