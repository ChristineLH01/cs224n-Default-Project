Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
To me she stood as the captive's mother
Whose heart was captive to his own display.
"I hate" she breathed, "because she loved not me then;
I forgive, though she swore by this league,
That love hath sworn by things pagan."
"I forgive, though she told me so," I told her,
"And I think her change of heart was my loss;
But love's changed his mind, and now 'tis all on me."
  So I go on loving her, despite her change,
  For I see her change is but a thin veil.

A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
To green his beautiful brow she shook,
And dried the withering hair from his face,
A cure for all ills but the melancholy head.

The gentle art of love-regard,
Nor seek a cure for my own sorrow,
For I was captured by my own heart's desire.
  And the dear abode of my soul's delight,
  Was right where my body was slain.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unversed in all the riches of her heart.
But why does she not speak of her love's wealth,
The room where it is kept, well hidden,
The pillar that supports its height, question?
And why do I not pray to have my will admitted,
As ancients claimed, one of many,
Hidden power in each of our hearts?
  For love answers not "I am made of enough,"
  But "I am loved."


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my heavier sire a sire quite wrong.
The power in my love is so strong,
That it lags behind my implants' learning,
And fails to gain full mastery over them.
Yet, in the end, my love shall win out,
Although my sire and I may seem to fight;
For love is strong, and shall win out even
With my best self quite contrary.
  If my love should fall short, 'tis my delight
  To have my rival's love too.
