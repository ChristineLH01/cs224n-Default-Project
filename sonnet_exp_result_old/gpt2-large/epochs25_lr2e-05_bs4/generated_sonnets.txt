Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While thy outer walls edify and grow?
Why maintain a parasitic existence,
Whilst thy inner core doth suffer in suppression?
Why invest so much in this mortal shell,
A structure destined to decay and crumble?
Shall the world repay thee for its debt,
A oppressed beggar who doth consume thy grace?
Then should the world shun thee, for it is thine,
And thou, a debtor, must shoulder the shame.
  Seek thou to reclaim what was lent,
  By maladministration and thy own transgressions.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
In vices sated, avaricious scum feeds.
Therefore, I wait whilst that sweet-tempered cure
What scanty alleviation may my longing bring,
For time's parcel, though present, in me still lies.
Then may I boast, "Now I see the end is near,
When this malady shall subsist no more,
And passion's flame increase with those diminished stays,
NorDeath, which waits for love, inherit the command."
  But then, love, remember this:
  Death dooms the idea of love that lives in thee.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my transgressions.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler nature to my body's treachery.
My spirit informs my flesh that it may strive
For victory in love; the flesh, no longer tame,
Rises at your mention and proclaims
You as its prize, its conquest and its aim.
  And thus, in pride, it serves you, ever near,
  To stand by you, to fall with you, my dear.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And still love finds fault in me, pledged at bay.
For promise thou the loveliest of pledges,
And yet in my maturity don't abide
The straying of mine eye from thy fair countenance;
So then mine uncalled-for jealousy is prove
To thee and thee alone; a prove
Of love, and thy true love indeed amaze.
  Then elsewhere in my nature do love show
  That he that delights in me must seem untrue.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
But when Love's warm expanse was half-crowned,
The cream had run, and Love's essence was o'eridden.
The remnants of his joy ran down,
To dogs, whose useful stubbornness
Lest their master should stray from their turns.
The flames of love that Jing characterized,
She took, and thus her own essence was lost.
  Yet, by some miracle, her love-kindling flame
  Continued despite this extinguishing hand.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unschooled in life's ways, and full of scorn.
Yet, I trust her, and abide,
Even as dearest pieces of truth are cast,
In your affection, I am firmly glued,
Revealed by your love, as purest heart.
And thus, I am left to endure,
The cruel fruit of her unwise trust,
As she delves into my shortcomings,
Truth's enemy, in my eyes, lives.
  In this plight, I find my lesson learned,
  Love does grow weary of a lie, and true hearts fight.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though sad as a gazelle caught in the ruff;
My darker phantom stays masked like a lamb,
Though my best friend calls him like a wiz.
My sinister muse does take delight
In what my benevolent lover does forsake,
And takes his joy away with her twist.
My virtuous guard thus does stand immobilized,
While my wicked one, huge and clumsy,
Steals joy from my heart, with almost guiltless glee.
  Yet, as he welcomes her, I mourn my plight,
  And in my weeping, I voice my plea for love.
