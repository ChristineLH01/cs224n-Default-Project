Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When thy outer shell is as fair
As any mother's child, though not so bright
As those gold candles fixed in heaven's air?
Profitless wretch, why dost thou waste away
In worry that thy outward shines so?

Thou of thyself thy sweet self dost deceive,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my errors.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler nature to my body's treachery.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unwise to trust a goddess of trust.
But why should I not glory in her rise,
And named after me, though she never lived near?
And why should I not proudly claim the love I bear,
When I know well her deceit?
For love's sweet tyranny is to claim
The truest honor, when I refute her right.
  And thus, in pride, I will maintain
  The love I bear, though she storms way.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though not so bright in show, yet such pure and kind.
The nobler spirit clad in light and valor,
The darker phantom cloaked in melancholy's woe.
My sinister muse does lure my virtuous guard,
And seeks to taint his essence, pure and bright.
She whispers softly, sowing discord,
Enticing him with her twisted, warped delight.
I ponder if my virtuous one may fall,
Seduced by shadows, losing light's embrace.
Divided, both from me, they stand enthralled,
In each other's darkness, they embrace this race.
