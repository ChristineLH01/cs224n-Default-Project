Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Yet who's so dumb that cannot write
What ought to grumble when I rebuke?
Or who's so dumb that cannot whisper
That love hath taught them to invent
Elaborate phrases to enhance their show?
No, let them whine that I have changed my mind,
And they will find their cause,
For I have sworn to one, forever tied,
And that love is such, to me,

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Obscuring thy sins in the shadows of thy sky?
Why invest so much in this temporal dwelling,
A structure bound to crumble, fade, and fall?
To what end do you give your all,
For worms to come and claim their bequest as well?
Instead, nourish yourself on the servant's despair,
Let them wither while you amass treasures fair.
Exchange fleeting hours for eternal grace,
Find solace within, while wanting without.
  Embrace the feast of Death, who consumes all men,
  And when Death perishes, no more shall we die

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Making sullen the very beings contracted.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
For, as you deceive me, I abandon
My nobler nature to my body's treachery.
My spirit informs my flesh that it may claim
Victory in love; the flesh, no longer tame,
Rises at your mention and proclaims
You as its prize, its conquest and its aim.
  And thus, in pride, it serves you, ever near,
  To stand by you, to fall with you, my dear.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
And due forfeiture I should pay,
Yet twice be crucified with thine error.
For to thy gentle heart I lend mind,
Thine adversary, my defense, takes form,
And a just plea against me they find.
Such civil strife in my heart is born,
  That I must two wrest a heavenly grace:
  Or else this my correction would be all my loss.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though she knows full well my days are brief,
I'll live my life in her falsehoods,
And let her believe me uncared for by all unfathered.
But why does she not admit her wrong?
And why do I not confess my age?
For love's most endearing trait is pretense,
And age in love dislikes the telling of years.
So we deceive one another in our flaws,
And with sweet lies, we comfort our disgrace.
  For I have sworn by your profound kindness,
  And by your beauty's wit, which knows no age.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though less bright than my deathless Moon;
But like her, he laughs at my foibles,
And she, in pity, says "This man loves me best."
Then, despairing of love, I send them both away,
My better spirit going mad, and my mad lover
Both laughing at me, that very night.
No love, though mutual, can so preposterously last,
As that which springs from thy inward frown.
  Or call it hope, that thrives in thy ground,
  And sounds an alarm when thou shalt depart.
