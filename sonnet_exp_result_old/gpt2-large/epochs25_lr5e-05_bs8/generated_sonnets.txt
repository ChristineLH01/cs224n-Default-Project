Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
To me she tied a fate unvaried,
That shall engage my muted moan
When I by quiet devotion meet.
She cast me forth in need of drying,
A wretched thing thrown from her sight,
Carried away on the swift stream of time;
No love toward me did she then show,
But pain, repeated and prolonged, tickled
My soul and made me want to sing.
  Yet, at the dance of her eyes,
  My heart, once full of your love, was satiated.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When beauty's pile stands multitude?
Profitless wanderer, where art thou seeking
For delight when thy comrade's bliss is?
What worth do those belated marks give,
That thou shouldst in thy vile fashion reprise
The part allotted to thee in kind?
O, none, unless this miracle have might,
That richly thou and thy sweet self may seem
Much more blessed still, when the dead are dead.
  Let this sad fact register then,
  That thou mayst find inspiration in some song.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Making maladies worse by their very being.
In vain I try the salve of thy wit,
The gentlest of beings, to heal my wound,
Which I have not only by thy written word,
But by the sweet repose of thy love, too,
Have expired this very day.
O, what a blessed cure for my grief
Had thy sweet health, which so sadly fell,
But that your worth, although so favorable,
  Do not soothe the wound, but send me to bed.

With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest remorse lead me to err, tragic.
For, as you deceive me, I betray
My nobler self to my body's treachery.
My soul informs my flesh that it may claim
Victory in love; the flesh, no longer tame,
Rises at your mention and proclaims
You as its prize, its conquest and its aim.
  And thus, in pride, it serves you, ever near,
  To stand by you, to fall with you, my dear.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
Theophilus the mimetic kept it glowing still,
But when Clymene the oracle saw it,
Her tears were more red than before,
And loving Aphrodite she shook the brand.
The living fire of love, so honored,
Lifted by a breeze from heaven's own breast,
Rises today, tomorrow drops away,
But still, every day, his memory runs.
  So too, my love, let your brand be still,
  While today's drops of love are still warm.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unschooled in life's ways, credulous of youth's tales.
Yet, I did treasure her story, and believe,
To keep her image of me as young,
Unaware of the world's cunning stratagems.
That she might think me naive, unknowing still,
She gave me false teeth, and false cheeks,
To keep her impression of me as boy,
Unaware of the world's cunning stratagems.
  And yet, I am taught wrong, and wronged still,
  By the beauty of her falsehood, my ignorance.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though not so bright in his facing as my sorrow;
His worth and his beauty in my view hold
Two strangers in their dancing grasp.
The nobler love, so far from censure,
Deserves my keenest admiration,
For it faces death, yet smells of life.
The darker love, which in its woe
Summer homes with caution, doth speak
Of me, and though it trails far,
I find its spirit still fast.
  For love, like a mother goose, I fled
  From this, and you alone know.
