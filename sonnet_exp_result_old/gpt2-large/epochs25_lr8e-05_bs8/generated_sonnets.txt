Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Those small flats that graced her cheek,
And all those gentle nips that poked
At my exposed heart, whispering insults.
Her tresses black, the hue of mourning,
Taught her to tie her smooth head
In the fashion of the gilded age;
And like the mourning dove, she sat in shade,
Her sweet gaze, like the serene bay,
Bleak with loss, yet full of grace.
  Such souls as these, in sadness black,

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And my darker phantom a woman cloaked in light.
When flattery's veil obscures thy visage,

My darkened spirit does call to thy gentle soul,
Seeking to ensnare thee in her twisted dance.
She offers her allure, a tempting bar,
To sway thee from thy course, and join her trance.
