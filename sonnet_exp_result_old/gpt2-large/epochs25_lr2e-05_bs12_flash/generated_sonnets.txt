Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
While my riches multiply and increase?
Why hoard thy apparel's value, tax free,

Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unschooled in life's ways, full of vow and chance.
But why should I reproach her oft,
When I find she loves me more deeply?
My love may be false, but I have sworn
She loves me for her truth, and not for my dust;
Thus, I am not suspected, though she be untrue.
Ah, could I but cease, and understand,
The worth of a lady's love is her upfront,
Her faithfulness repaid in kind, higher than gold;
  She lifts me higher than me, so that I am low.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And his partner in sorrow is as black as pitch.
This knell I pause to make, whose soft breath
Sings or talks with my heart's inner melody;
Whoever hears this, though shadows may ensue,
Believes me truly alive, though darkened there.
Such love was never vile or cruel,
Or base in its enrollment;
Rather, it mattered not how dear the stain,
Whose multitude forms the mountain's crown.
  For if worthiness or low estate bred
  In evil's stead, then I was born fair, bright.
