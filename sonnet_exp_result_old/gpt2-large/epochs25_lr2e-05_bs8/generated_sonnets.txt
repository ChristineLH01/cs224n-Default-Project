O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,
Which have no correspondence with true sight!
Or if they have, where is my judgment fled,
That looks after the most perfect jeweled sight?
If deemed fair be the focus of love's false sight,

To make the dullest jewel look worth more than air;
And much more precious is this, that man

Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my transgression.

Rises at your mention and proclaims

  To stand by you, to fall with you, my dear.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep.
She quenched the flame with water, but not quite ease,
For pride of beauty her purpose lacked.
The lad quenched the fire with brandy,
But still the water could not put it out;
So the brand captured him and drove him
To seek a different drink, deeper in peace.
A lesser man might have shrugged his shoulders,
But Leadbeater walked beneath the fire,
His courage yet bold, no longer shaken.
  And still, the fire beat fast, burning his spirit,
  And his love, forevermore, a distant flare.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Unschooled in life's ways, who've sold my heart.
Thus, I am fettered by a love so great,
That I may not contradicted give space,
To child the truth, although it may bend and turn.
But why should I verify a lie,
When I may live in liberty where I may?
My love knows this, and knows her truth well,
So I abide, though she doth deceive me.
  But what's so virtuous that she believes me
  To be untrue, when I find she's true?
