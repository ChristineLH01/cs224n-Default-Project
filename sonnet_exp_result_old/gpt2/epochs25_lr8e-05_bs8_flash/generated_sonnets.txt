Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
At that instant, neither whispered kiss nor my name
Revealed my heart, either for or against.

Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Seeking release from all bonds bound?
Why dost thou cling to these walls, walled against?

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Enhancing health with that which brings despair.
Thus does my love keep its winterish nurse's hand
And keeps its summer sun in its nest,
Innocence keen and keen, as if by a jewel.
No grief can this grief evermore alter,
Nor in that sorrow assuage the plea,
That love itself must bear the cross of love's
Reveling birth, that proves us two again.
  Thus, I fear, he who lavishly spends
  No grief and still lives in hope.

O me, what eyes hath love put in my head,

His beauty, though my seeing have surmounted.
How should my seeing be blunter than his,
To make clear the form of his picture,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
Upon the blossoms of the two kindred tribes.
But neither could for his consent take leave,
The sacred waters which together gave life to Love's name;
So, without assistance or aught, Love's fire grew weak,
And died a virgin in his fire.
The waters could not give him up, for Love's true rose was
A liquid when heated; nor blood Love gained, for it was
A substance that breathed life into the lungs and new.
  But water could not soothe the fire that grew weak,
  Unless it burnt with a forge's fire.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
One maiden fair wore the visage of a maiden,
And countless bards did their vows swear.
So too, the humble jewel of youth, when worn by hand,
Could not the sting of innocence from his breast
Be deemed a crime, since he wore no more than a cloak,
Or if he wore no more than a coarse wire,
The world would flee, in shame and dismay,
To take him as one such as you are.
  Thus, thou youth's most precious jewel, now stolen,
  Sold for nothing, for which you have no rights.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
When in truth I have sworn her innocence,
And then to falsehood I plead my good name;
And yet, love, you deceive with a curse,
A league of feelings, I once was,
When near to falsehood I admitted,
Although love's fire admitted not heat;
And in this we see the truth through woe,
And to falsehood my love fells,
Like false women in love's riot.
  But here's the charm, the sweetness,
