Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Denying the constancy of thy bright face?
For base clay works wonders, and thine eyes
Bestow a divine spark to mar the imitation's face,
Adorning ugliness with adorning walls of gold.
Yet, store thy charms in thine own well,
Thine unused treasure, so cherished, shall bear
The sting of love, once it has been doused,
A lesson true heart needs no embellishment.
  So, store thy charms in this well,
  Then share this well with others, amen.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot
My key to grant thee double life?
Who turns from thee that I should call a friend?
On whom dost thou frown that I do pursue?
On thy part, sweet thief, when I steal sweets
From thy treasure chest which thou dost bring forth.
What virtue do I see in me
That I should spurn when thou art gone,
When I have obeyed thee to the end,
To take thy form in myself before thee?
  O none, but this I know: that love
  Deserves no name in me.


5
O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
To trust in thy celestial nature,
As thou art mine, to trust even in thine own part?
If thou wouldst use what old methinks mine eye bestows,
To paint thy beauty anew, with new truth,
The old beauty will be forgot, as beauty's decease
Will vanish, of sweet sense and folly.
  Hers, by thy beauty seeming false, taught me thus:
  Thy unused beauty, when taught,
  Will teach me how to live in it.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Were marred by passion's instilled hold,
Their vows now marred, as well.
For to my loving heart, whose heart is bound,
My love now works as a charm,
Her gaze so fitting, like mourners in their midst,
At those who, not bound by devotion,
Cannot claim their love displayed.
  For to her heart's sanctuary, their love remains,
  Unyielding, never shaken by fear or punishment.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
Though she knows no fancy, such munificent boasting.
But methinks she hath no tongue, and I am unwoke,
So base as a moored boat,
Scarce and faint with my love's swells,
Like fools who believe their vows so willingly.
Then do thy worst to hate her, for she hath but one hue
Which proud women can change and wrinkle;
  I in love but increase in happy hour.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And love that bends to circumstance is replete.
Though he in turn may err, never truly err,
I hold the better for his due;
Unless this wicked carnal grace
Enslaves the true spirit of my soul.
I forbid one false sin from you today,
Which by your law you shall not remove;
And for that sin in me I do forbid,
First my love, then you, my most truly true heart's decease.
  For you I have imprisoned with thee,
  Two hearts in one, and thus I'll keep as bond.
