For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
In hope that his own flesh may grow strong and new.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though false o'er whom all hearts err.
My love to thee, to myself I confide,
For thou dost keep a watchman's face,
To watch my dreams from far-off;
To watch him from where I waken, I say,
To know where my heart is at variance.
I lie with that watchman, knowing well the truth,
But therein lies hope, a heart so steadfast,
That in my dreams my heart might reproach.
  For such falsehoods love imparts,
