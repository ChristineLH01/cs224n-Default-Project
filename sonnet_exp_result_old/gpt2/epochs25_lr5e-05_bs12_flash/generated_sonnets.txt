Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
As minor sire alters his prime, time's cruel potion?
The noble heart, abandoned by its sovereign,
Feeds on the discontent of its captive,
In vain, turns against the lawful lords' decree.
Nay, 'tis not love, when fortified and well-versed,
That lags behind the times, doth it forsake its lead,
Or turns its gaze from truth to grace,
For love's sweet memory dwells where truth itself bends.
  Thus, in this vice, discern the ill,
  And render virtue, even if it serves not for you.

O, from what power hast thou this powerful might
With insufficiency my heart to sway?
To make me give the lie to my true sight,
To make some copy seem worth more,
To make me proud of manners which I did not profess.
Yet, being deceived by my looks,
My dear nurse confided in me
That she saw how to make virtue grow bolder
In her breast that makes my manners weak and weak.
And thus thy false accuser proved her virtue
Neglecting well the reason of my being
To come to her power, and steal my school.
  For to trust in such slanderer look
  My love steals my heart's delight.

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest you become entangled in my wrongs.
My transgressions, I confess, are but the seeds
Of a deeper love, for love binds us,
And in that love, we find comradeship's swell.
That love, when broken, tears me apart,
A beacon telling the world my plight;
No other voice or judgment hears
My plea for love's kind, let alone understands.
  To ally myself with your company,
  Take my life, and leave me alone.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
His fiery torch near hand, left poor and hobbled,
Doubting the fiery legacy of his boundless might.
The lovely widow, clothed in simple light,
Bathed in sweetest of flowers which instantered,
In him she gazed upon youth, whom the hallowed skies granted
And, holy mother, her radiant glow spread
When low she fell, no herald could match
The radiant star that in youth she danced;
  And thus fate had her revenge, setting fire to flame
  On an ancient maiden's head, holy as any holy flower.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
And an ignorant knight, false both sworn.
Best sworn to passion unadorned,
And to deeds sharpened by true commitment.
My unspoken love sighs thus inward,
A bubbling tempest that stirs the hearts of men.
When certainty's compass does track thy track,
Or beneath the surface lies hidden treason,
My peace proclaims, a gracious gift.
  For to thy truth I stand, and truly stand,
  For none can truly know me beyond the breath.
