Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
When I, thy devoted mistress, am doomed to lose?
Rather, soul, nourished by love's sweet chain,
Should she succumb, submissive, to your will,
She'll yield, and I shall reap the blow.
But if she succumbs, I shall have no argument,
For she who succumbs embraces not love,
And with love, I find a sweet reward.
  Lascivious woman, what guard have you placed
  That she may come and claim her part in my crime?

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Alternating colds with sweet absences.
As summer's filleth away the dead, so too does winter's fill
Where once-lush branches chide the once-verdant canopy,
Defiant, cold-loving winter steals away her stand,
A barren beauty for winter to maul.
I have witnessed countless fawns and snowy plagues,
Yet none so gracious of a wreath
As when Nature's rich bounty adorns your visage.
  Thus, happy those that are, so happy are they,
  Preserving nature's wealth through beauty's decease.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep;
So my lady of the night, her eyes so bright,
Came dancing by; and, out of my sight, fell
To sleep in a dream she had no control.
The painter's hand engraved on my love's brow
Seems to have borrowed from my lady's gentle mood,
But beauty's breath did take the matter;
My lady of the night, his beauty still paints,
Her visage dances with passion's sweet cloud.
  Her dream I now recall,
  A summer's night, when lovers sang and danced.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Despairing no man's grace more near,

To lead one maiden to miscarry,

To one tainted by love's toxic contagion.
Such hearts she bears, 'tis not from thee,
Yet inspires hatred in men's eyes,
For 'tis from thee that love illenseth;
  And thou in this, 'tis my shield, my defense,
  To keep love's fire strong and true.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
A lie conceived to keep my ardor in vices.
And yet, in truth I see her deceive,
She thinks me, knowing well my days are fleeting,
And, like kings she thinks me an unpainted maiden,
Which fornication dances with masked pride,
And, sweet as she thinks, doth she boast
Of me, only my name, dressed to disgrace.
Were she false in this, and I befriends her false gown,
  A love most false hath deceived men, both for and against.
