Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Her heart, aware of grief, snatched away
And held fast to my beloved's lament,
Adorned in black, her tears a soothing breeze,
Transforming gloom to solace once more;
I swallowed the bitterness of losing her sight,
And breathed her deepest sighs in despair's guise,
Like mourners for loved departed in vain.
  But here my love's true sorrow sings anew,
  For losing her sight was an accident,
  The bane of her plight, when Love's true balm had been slain.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Preserving thy virtue through self-defense?
The reason for this, methinks, lies in thy ne'er troubled state,
As I by nature's invention fear.
For, though not endowed with respite,
Thyself so boundless, thy inward glow
Presents such danger, thy outward shines even starker.

2
My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
With force and profit;
At times, though the world may claim my love,
My heart still stays in its summer's throes.
Even then, the world will say,
'My love is but for sympathy's sake,
A sickness born in a prevarication's nest,
That more should love proclaim its hue,
Making ill my cure for love fairer than cure.
  Thus, supposing a loved one inflicting ill,
  I'd rather die than live in despair.

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
My love is bound to you in narrow gauge;
Tell me, O heart, of our hearts' varied scythe,
And I should peruse your profound affectionate phrase,
To prove my innocence, confess my prime,
And cast away the remnants of an ill-earned scorn.
So, be still, my love; while your wrath extends beyond spite,
Do not delay your wrath for future pay.
  As long as heart and mind reside side by side,
  No reproach can mar you in the least.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,

In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
Upon thine false heart thou deceive'st was born;
In affectionate deed thy stolen love gains appeal,
And sick grace on thy unworthy arm lies faire.
Is it not sinful to harm love with intent?
Then do thy worst, motherfucker; I spurn thee,
By gentle act and willful spite.
  Love's roar needs no fanfare, 'tis it true?
  I love thee for thy love's pure honesty, so thine,

8
Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steeple.
A thousand broils brought flowers in streams,
But neither rose nor chrysanthemum ever rose;
So rose was poor and faded from memory,
Bestowed on the head of a mortal goddess;
Then chrysanthemum fell away, and myron eclipsed,
Whose pyramids built with numbers preserved,
With figures of my lovers burned efface.
  Thus can such a sage fire be termed sacred,
  Even at a brook or pool where stars burn--
  The waters don't lose their power to quell the sacred flame.


9
The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Could not tire the slow slave away.
The naked Lovebird, she hissed as he did,
Where artfully danced his fiery brand,
While ancient nymphs, once blushing and whispering,
Revived his name and rose with lust;
And ancient Romans too, dismayed at their gaze,
Brought their ancient love to their music;
Such ancient songs and holy Lovebirds sing,
  Where holy truth does his fire thence prevail.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth, better still,
Though not to cherish, as myself unnoted are.
And so, in truth believing me, her constancy lays
The burden of proof, once proved untrue,
Strength for love's own untrue dower,
Strength in love's bond with my spun's shape;
Thus, she believes me false, believing me as fair,
And blindly speaking of my not knowing,
My bad days, my true.
  For she knows well my days lead me far,
  And in this lie, in fact, acquainted.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Fairing deserts made worse by love's sour offer.
His gilded cage finds advocate's bay,
And, enraptured, love allows its spirit to soar.
My better self sees the warring winds
And in their chaotic mess destroys love's own plea.
So too, I corrupt love's sweet carouse,
And in that strife finds remedy too extreme,
One ruined delight and then despair's excess.
  A jeweled scepter, love consumes my life,
  Overtaking love's sweet one, reigning high.
