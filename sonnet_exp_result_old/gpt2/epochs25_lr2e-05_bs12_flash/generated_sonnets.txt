Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
As thought's storehouse, ill-mixed by temptation?
Yet, like a ship and powerless to steer,
Your sweet orbit obstructs all from my hearing,
Leaving me weak, in ignorance and withal,
And from your plight, none can reach your good hands.
I know not a fortress where you abide,
Where your will commands your every endeavor,
That you might claim my plea alone.
  This, in my humble verse, is falsified,
  To deceive you, and in my deed, thou morn.

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep
Against my heart's decree alone.
Alone among the birds in celestial light,
His cheek plucked out but healing glee fell
Afected by burning, fell lover's fire.

Ajar, such pains should not ensue in bed.

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
The mother of malign feature was most admired;
The fouler loves oft sing their glee,
And nature's charm concealed their fond sorrows.
I once set out on an impassioned contest,
To find love grounded, and away, swiftly to the end.
For they so admired me, that I soon fell to scorn,
Though not wanting to name, heart, or ill or weed.
  So returning blows both affirmals were made,
  All embracing one, and the care of our sleepy bud.
