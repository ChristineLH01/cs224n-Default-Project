Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
So then her gaze she erased
From my soul's memory that time should hang;
And to you she confined
Her distance not in sleep or thou whereabouts come,
A doom to come, the fullest of woes.
O, let her recompense with tears escape,
She delivers me through the door of sorrow,
But I cannot reproach you for losing her sight.
  Hence she grips not my heart but your hand,
  For her recompense brings me back but loss of mine.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Denying the love that once was yours, thy rightful place?
Seeking what is right, what you no longer rightfully deserve,
And reigning in evil, abandoning what was given?
Why should thy righteousness suffer at last,
Its essence tainted by sin's lure,
Denying other virtues its due?
In self-example, thou art fair-minded,
Though she whose eyes deceive may perceive,
Shepherding thee to the edge, bestowing grace on error.
  Seek thou to bring good to those who have fallen short,
  By keeping true to thyself, thy kingdom's goal

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Exerting life anew from die's trap.
My friend, thus, it lives anew, ebbing and waning,
In the tomb of death, where it withers, sown fears,

My love runs wild, famished, torn,
By sickness and Death's chase, no cure found.
  Yet the tomb of love, where my friend's flesh
  Steals from my heart, my heart with grief confounded.

6
Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
When certainty breeds doubts, love's guilt confesses.
Why should my affection harbor doubts,
As passionate zealots in love's corner?
Or on my rejection, can I claim my grace?
Indeed, might I not then proclaim,
My virtue, crowned, with love's eternal fire?
Love's most ardent penance, illuminates
Our times, and proclaims our destiny so bright.
  Let my love resist the years, its jealous siege
  And proclaim anew love's vast theme.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
Thy reason doth use mind banned.
Wander where thou heart dost thou with preposterous accents,
Placing praise where thou hast no reason,
Lending acquaintance where thou keeps not haply;
Make foes of the fairest thou art,

Ay, assuage most worst of harm's array,

Cupid laid by his brand and fell asleep.
A maid of Dian's this advantage found,
And his love-kindling fire did quickly steep:
In this holy desert dwellsome fell the shillings of sins;
When many a maiden fair, so honored,
Yet Death fell from his chase, and from his bright gaze he fell;
And few were his charms when Love-schooled him,
Then retreated with laughter and silence once more.
So the sweet thief of death, by Death and his very craft,
Spent all his woes in public entertain,
And by the potion that made him such fame had a go;
  Death, artfully poisoned, came to have that place,

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Bestowed their jewels upon his wing,
More blessed than those sacred, by most excellent law.

An illogical dwelling-place,

  With this love you planted, evergreen,

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Fairing my heart when robbed of his reason.
Compare him to the sun hath fair chosen to shine,

To grant him life, through your love abandoned
Heirs to falsehood crowned with thorns false,
