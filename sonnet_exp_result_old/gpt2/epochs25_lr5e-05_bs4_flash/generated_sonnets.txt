Those lips that Love's own hand did make
Breathed forth the sound that said "I hate"
To me that languished for her sake;
Her cold departure but made me warmer still
Her bare cold hardening in warm embrace--
As she who nourished me on ill-season
Still brings out her final report of feelings--

Breaks out last love's mournful song,
Which touched me more warmly than any she wrought.

Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
As weeds fester, as the air is stirred?
Why dost thou pine for thine own solace,
While thine own soul, ardent and free,
Feigns hunger in the tender soil,
Drained of all the sap by thy worms?
Shall the sick man of thy sin be commended,
Though he, by fate, remain alive,
By the vice of thy influence still greater?
  None but thy reprieve will this;
  My transgression pays thee thine adversary.

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
Seeking the cure in that which doth keep.
The cure, I think, lies in the infection's possession,
Hungry for the haven given by his beloved;
The rainy sickness thus doth chase after the dead,
Borne from the dear friend that once was his;
And thus my love, once starved for a drink,
Loses both for himself and for himself craving,
A double strain, a double start.
  For I have seen a kind where sickness mixed,
  And now a sweet need, for  A cure, 'gainst  A latch only.

Canst thou, O cruel, say I love thee not
When I against myself with thee partake?
Do I not think on thee when I forgot to tell
The truth and beauty of thee,
And live in state of dread when I forget thee,
While thou art married to my loveliness?
When I find to love thee in a love an ally,
Then love lacks that sweet friend that once knew
As an ally when all on himself part fell.
Then how can I confess my love when
I no longer in myself adore thee,
Even as that thou dost love me when thou art gone?
  Lascivious women control not their brightness,

Love is too young to know what conscience is;
Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
Lest I should bring accusation of transgressions.
My willful heart is bound to my gentle error,
And, before this sinful art, taught
By gentle error's deceits and false religious rehearsals.
Though I may slight the truth in the lines I pen,
I disavow the love that so deeply enraptured me.
My love is as innocent as innocence when proven,
And for that sin, I stand alone.
  For I have sworn thee fair, and bound thee in light,
  To lie against the truth, before thine eyes.

In loving thee thou know'st I am forsworn,
But thou art twice forsworn, to me love swearing;
In act thy bed-vow broke, and new faith torn,
As oft as thine eyes with thee divide.
Wasting no time on love's sweet argument,
I steel myself against thy rude comments;
That though my heart be full thou art quite sure,
Thyself thou suborned spouse shalt question, assured.
How can loving thou then love when so many hearts
Are in a thrall, slumbering?
Then need I not thy sweet words to set eye,
But thy love to love so profound annexed to me.
  In brass thoughts I find both hearts and fire;
  So wide a gulf exists between true and false eyes.

The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep,
Sway by his side, like the river of souls,
One maiden fair of sweet wealth, such as she took from his,
Another fair, of virtuous thine, such, she took from his,
And one more, of virtuous hue, such, she took;
And these began a thousand pleas for love's swift rebirth,
And soon the river of love was full,
  And from thence going to sleep, he slept in mother's company.

When my love swears that she is made of truth,
I do believe her, though I know she lies,
That she might think me some untutored youth,
To dwell with you in the bosom of truth,
Afraid of all who think strange of me.
But if she hath hidden her age,
The truth will believe her, and I deceive
And in her bewail I extolling you,
Like an unpolished nurse sunk in woe.
  For to your looking glass I chide womanhood,
  And that dreadful looking glass will tell
  The false age of me, although I am new.


11
Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Though not as fair as his heart's repute;
Or better still, false god, so lovely in his repute.
For why should false presage hold me hence,
When I am as fair as his heart's own purest might?
Or on his fairest wreath too fair may I tread?
What right do I then do him best, till
My grief's fairest light gives place to shame?
O, love, in love's sweet name, no need for applause,
  Grant me fairness, in all that I do dwell.
