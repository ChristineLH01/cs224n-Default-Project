Poor soul, the center of my sinful earth,
Pressed with these rebel powers that thee array,
Why dost thou pine within and suffer dearth,
Denying the light of thy eternal quest?
Redeem thy hours of delight, have mercy on thy foes'
And defeat them in battle, blunt thou their might.

Even so, should they find their day's gain,
In confronting Time's relentless clock.
  Yet, should they fail, and turn away,
  This verse will grow cold and hard to read,

My love is as a fever, longing still
For that which longer nurseth the disease,
Feeding on that which doth preserve the ill,
With sharp remedies, but no cure of sweet death.
So I, unblessed by the chains of time,
Gather comfort from the whispers of the old,
Yet in my heart's vast chamber, I find a medicine
That wards off the deadly fevers in me.
No pains can ever soothe my heart's core,
Nor can death's chilling sound ever so near,
To say the least of my dearest, unblessed rest.
  Yet this my love cannot fail to rouse:
  For in the sweetest of vices, life ebbs and flows.

Yet who knows not conscience is born of love?
Then, gentle cheater, urge not my amiss,
When I have shown thee what honor means.
